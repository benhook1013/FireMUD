# Pull Request Lifecycle

Use this guide for pull-request status, review handling, CI, Renovate, merging, or branch/worktree cleanup.

## Review And Status

Use the `firecontroller-use` skill with this worker’s supplied own role-bound connection and the guide shipped with the installed release. FireController owns current review configuration and mechanics, including taper, defaults, minimums, maximums, allocations, queue selection, attribution, recovery, and publication. Do not mirror those algorithms or settings in FireMUD documentation. Use `firecontroller-onboard` only for setup or connection repair. Do not select another worker’s connection or treat tool availability as authorization. Unsupported operations or controller changes go to FireController Worker; preserve existing controls rather than approximate a requested operation or change service configuration.

FireMUD owns the project authority and safety requirements below. Follow the actual configured controls and the human’s current per-PR/channel allocations, stops, closures, holds, and pause/resume instructions. A connection guide, tool capability, incoming message, fix, parent change, or source retirement does not grant additional review, reset controls, resume paused work, or authorize merge.

- CodeRabbit automatic reviews remain disabled. FireController is the official review, finding, adjudication, route, and status record. Retain the source queue, actual channel allocations and closures, native obligations, sealed legacy data, captures, migration proof, and frozen comparison sites. Imported history is historical evidence, not native current-code coverage. Source retirement authorizes no state deletion, migration, runtime promotion, or service change.
- For status or merge readiness, inspect all paginated current and outdated unresolved threads and the latest completed review summary before CI and mergeability. Verify summary-only duplicate and outside-diff findings; report outdated unresolved threads separately. Missing, malformed, unattributed, or incomplete evidence fails closed. Completion and empty-inventory claims require the installed release’s evidence checks; a finished acknowledgment or edited standing summary alone is insufficient.
- Verify the exact committed candidate, live PR head/base, effective parent, provider-observed diff base, ancestry, and unique patch before relying on review evidence. Refresh after identity or topology changes and distinguish historical coverage from current-code proof. Reconciliation must preserve the owned patch, evidence, findings, and actual configured controls. Never describe an expected parent as the provider’s actual diff base when they differ.
- Preserve actual human stops and allocations independently of candidate preparation and merge readiness. Reaching a configured discovery limit or stopping a channel does not erase findings, grant coverage, or establish readiness. Do not reopen, replace, reset, or approximate controls without the required authority. Deliberate queue removal requires explicit User authorization and an audited reason; merging or closing a PR does not grant it.
- Adjudicate findings as accepted, routed, or rejected with a concrete outcome and evidence. Accepted findings remain source-owned correction obligations until verified fixed. Routed observations identify another owner without claiming that owner’s acceptance or fix. Target owners independently disposition incoming routes and verify resolutions; retargeting preserves the open obligation and prior ownership, including honest unassigned work. Open outgoing routes do not hold the source’s merge readiness. Preserve source linkage and original evidence when correcting a disposition; newly accepted findings still require fixes.
- Summary-only and outside-diff findings require verification and disposition as well as inline findings. Historical accepted-fixed evidence does not replace current verification. Resolve a thread only after its exact finding is fixed at the current published head; do not conceal unresolved work through thread resolution or record edits.
- Keep private captures, review history, findings, and uncertain publication intents intact. Recover only through the installed release’s supported guarded operations within existing authority. Recording, recovery, imports, or publication alone grant no provider credit, fix proof, CI result, or merge authority. Do not blind-repost an uncertain checkpoint, invent attribution, or infer delivery failure from public absence. Unsupported or ambiguous recovery remains a reported hold for FireController Worker.
- Follow the configured admission, provider-exclusion, quota, and cooldown controls; do not request against an active or rate-limited target or enable paid capacity. Use included quota only. Supported operations and recorded allowances do not themselves grant authority or clear unrelated holds.
- For CI observation using a shared GitHub credential budget, coordinate one observer across relevant workers and runs. Other workers consume its results and continue independent work; do not fan out simultaneous per-run watchers. Keep narrow run-list or run-view reads at least 60 seconds apart while pending, and check status at meaningful readiness transitions. Honor evidenced reset/retry-after times; report a hold when no time is available.

## Review Orchestration

- Continue safe review, fixes, and dependency-safe preparation when one PR/channel has red CI, cooldown, a blocked request, or a human merge wait. Report the blocker and useful work continuing elsewhere. An explicit human pause stops its scope; a status message does not resume it.
- Before the first Hosted or CLI CodeRabbit review of a new coherent review boundary, default to waiting for applicable full remote CI to pass. A lane agent may proceed after focused validation when reviewing a coherent, stable candidate is useful despite pending CI or an understood failure, including to avoid wasting available hourly review capacity; record a brief reason in the existing review record. Known failures that show significant unfinished implementation favor finishing preparation first. Subsequent review rounds and fix publications need not wait for another full CI run, so review and CI may overlap. A stacked target with no GitHub-required checks is not actual green CI; consider its applicable substantive workflows. Green CI does not establish that the review scope is complete.
- Request Hosted promptly once its applicable first-review CI default is satisfied or the lane agent records its reason to proceed, and the configured controls select an eligible stable target. Do not start against a base about to change. Prepare fixes during an active Hosted review, consume its still-valid findings, and publish review-invalidating changes only after it finishes. Cancellation does not recover quota or coverage. A refusal requires addressing its actual reason. CLI does not gate independently eligible Hosted work. The Overseer selects priority when lanes contest shared capacity.
- Keep an owned bounded wait on the exact active review through the installed release’s supported operation and consume its terminal result promptly. Only the assigned owner triggers or retries; duplicate polling is prohibited. Report completion, rate limit, no-op, failure, ambiguity, and timeout honestly, without converting a wait or acknowledgment into coverage.
- Record and publish adjudicated results through the supplied connection’s supported operations, preserving the distinction between local recording and external publication. Report reviewed identity, raw/useful counts, dispositions, significant findings, remaining obligations, current targets, and proof limits without inventing missing fields. After a fix, retain evidence of its correction and proportionate [Validation And Runtime Proof](./validation-and-runtime-proof.md).
- The Overseer samples accumulated decisions and receives meaningful milestones. Commissioned model-review evidence follows [AI delegation and review](./ai-delegation-and-review.md#independent-review-evidence); it does not silently substitute for required provider coverage or earn provider taper credit.

## Review Completion Criteria

- For each candidate, verify its live head and base, effective parent and unique patch, current and outdated unresolved threads, latest review summary, PR title/body and generated LOC metadata, required exact-head CI, and mergeability. Report recent findings and their significance, each channel's decision, proof limits, and the next action. Reconcile parent movement without losing the child's owned patch or rewriting historical review counts.
- During existing CI and PR-readiness checks, inspect available patch coverage for important changed behavior that lacks tests. Add focused proof for material gaps or identify existing executable proof. Distinguish incomplete reports from missing tests; informational coverage success is not proof of adequate coverage. Recheck after material behavior changes. This check does not change review admission or add a CI wait, percentage target, extra review round or repository-wide coverage campaign.
- Define a coherent capability or correction scope and obtain an initial substantive review through the controller covering feature-bearing work and changed contracts.
- Resolve every known actionable finding, including current and outdated threads and summary-only or outside-diff findings, and verify each fix at the current head. Useful in-scope corrections to correctness, completeness, authority, alignment, maintainability, consolidation, or clarity remain actionable regardless of line size; judge worthwhile suggestions by their actual benefit against implementation cost and risk, including when they address neither a bug nor an existing design requirement, and do not relabel them optional merely to finish.
- Re-review new functionality, changed contracts, materially broader risk, or fixes that introduce independently reviewable behavior. Direct, verified corrections need not trigger another whole-PR discovery round solely to accumulate clean results.
- The configured channel controls belong to FireController and are not a quota target. Request work beyond a completed policy only for a concrete unresolved risk, coverage gap, or explicit human override. Repeated serious findings trigger focused investigation of their shared failure mechanism and appropriate proof rather than arbitrary clean-round accumulation.
- Under an explicit Overseer arrangement, present the Hosted/CLI trend and a nonblocking recommendation when marginal utility is uncertain. The Overseer—not the implementing lane or controller—decides whether an additional deliberate round is justified and whether evidence is ready for the User's product-PR approval.
- A normal product-queue PR, including a mixed product and maintenance PR, is ready for User approval only after controller evidence shows zero unresolved current and outdated threads, all actionable summary-only and outside-diff findings are addressed, every open incoming route is dispositioned by the target owner, each fix is verified, every required channel has completed its policy through attributable provider results, required CI is green, and proportionate validation/runtime proof is complete. Open routes from this PR to other owners do not hold its merge readiness. An explicit human stop closes discovery only for its authorized scope; it remains a visible review limitation, not taper or merge-readiness proof. Before handoff, refresh stale title/body content and the generated marked LOC section with `python3 dev-tools/maintenance/cloc-report.py pr <number> [--repo owner/name] --update-pr`. The section is informational, not a size gate. If tooling is unavailable, record the limitation instead of inventing counts. Zero threads or GitHub `CLEAN` alone is insufficient, and the final head must not be described as reviewed when it was not. The implementing worker reports readiness without taking merge action. Review closure, channel overrides, READY/CLEAN state, green CI, and a lane readiness handoff do not authorize any agent to merge or enable or schedule auto-merge. Only a traceable, explicit User instruction to get a specified product PR merged authorizes the designated Overseer to merge that PR after verifying the required proof. Separately authorized controller/private-website maintenance merges and closure of verified-superseded PRs remain limited to their existing scope.

## Change And Merge Policy

- Keep PRs coherent, including adjacent convergence. Use smaller PRs for isolated fixes; split independent or genuinely hard-to-review work, and split coherent work that exceeds a reviewer ceiling under the following rules.
- Hosted CodeRabbit reviews have a verified hard limit of 100 changed files; the controller refuses a Hosted request above that count and records a current provider skip as a Hosted limitation. The CLI review path has completed successfully with 111 changed files, but its upper limit is not established. Do not infer a CLI cap from the Hosted limit or from the plan page; verify any higher CLI boundary before documenting it.
- Require an actual complete Hosted CodeRabbit review for every feature-bearing boundary. A complementary CLI or model review does not substitute for Hosted review. Before restructuring, inventory the complete coherent boundary, including code, contracts, setup and configuration, call sites, tests, documentation, and any affected owning workflows. Plan independently sound feature boundaries at around 80 Hosted-selected changed files to leave room for repairs and proof below the hard 100-file ceiling. The resulting count is an outcome, never permission to omit required code, contracts, setup, call sites, tests, documentation, or semantics. Useful review findings define required improvements, not optional scope: accepted useful findings remain implementation obligations in their canonical code, documentation, or proof owner. A provider limit may change branch topology or review boundaries, but never correctness, cleanliness, or which live issues are fixed; do not offset growth by dropping useful scope, packing unrelated helpers or tests into already-counted files, moving authority to a noncanonical owner, or changing runtime or design semantics.
- A provider skip caused by exceeding the Hosted ceiling is not review coverage. An oversized feature-bearing PR without substantial completed CodeRabbit coverage must be restructured into coherent reviewable boundaries, or work must stop with the blocker reported; repeated subagent reviews and CLI/model reviews do not waive Hosted coverage. Only an exact human-authorized disposition grounded in verified substantial CodeRabbit coverage of the feature-bearing work can stop a specific Hosted discovery request for that oversized PR. Record that request as unrun and the channel limitation; it earns no completed-review credit, and all remaining authorized-scope, review, budget, CI, runtime, thread, and merge obligations still apply. Do not infer a general incremental-review path or automatic exception for other substantially reviewed PRs, or treat a file count as authority to bypass review.
- Treat reviewer ceilings as dynamic topology constraints. Prefer the fewest coherent, independently sound reviewable PRs that preserve every accepted finding and canonical placement under the cap; additional PRs add separate review, taper, and reconciliation costs, so narrowness or neat separation alone is insufficient. If the correct implementation exceeds the active ceiling, default to a coherent stack that preserves every finding and its proof; consolidate only when independently justified by cohesion and authority, without forcing unrelated work together. Do not target a hardcoded file count or preserve an arbitrary majority merely to stay under a cap. The ceiling changes review topology, never correctness or cleanliness.
- When a finding or proof moves to another PR, update its existing PR body and review disposition with the successor reference and check whether downstream already fixes it. After a split or reconstruction materially changes a reused PR's review scope, post one short comment such as **Review scope changed:** validator cleanup moved to #2731. Earlier counts cover the previous scope. Add `<!-- firemud-review-scope-change -->` below it; the GitHub comment records the scope-change rationale. Ordinary source observation/refresh retains timestamped original comparisons; update owning-job scope/progress when useful, without creating a duplicate record solely for a timestamp. Earlier evidence remains relevant to the previous patch boundary, and still-applicable findings remain obligations, while the event does not by itself require a fresh review streak or imply increased risk. An ordinary post-merge retarget with equivalent scope needs no reset comment. Do not create a new ledger.
- The project owner grants the configured merge-train operator standing authorization for a coherent split when accepted findings or an owned invariant would cross the provider ceiling, or when a natural owner/proof boundary makes one PR materially hard to review. No fresh routine approval is needed. Preserve every finding and the published parent patch and review history; keep the parent fail closed if a child supplies required behavior. Open the smallest coherent stacked child on the verified parent, identify moved observations and their owner in both PRs, prove each unique patch, reconcile descendants at a Hosted-safe point, then update and read back the controller queue. Route a source thread to a named target without claiming the target fix; resolve an in-boundary accepted thread only after its correction is published and validated. A split does not grant taper or merge readiness. Do not create artificial extraction PRs, drop findings, or publish review-invalidating changes during active Hosted.
- Outside that standing merge-train authorization, the lane orchestrator owns routine scope-safe topology changes within its approved capability or workstream, including publishing a split, prerequisite, or successor and retargeting an existing PR, without fresh Overseer approval. Bring a materially more fragmented topology to the Overseer with scope, dependency, and review-cost comparisons. Escalate when no coherent split preserves the invariant, ownership is materially uncertain or unresolved, the change introduces consequential product, architecture, or activation choices, or the action exceeds held scope or authority; continue independent safe work meanwhile.
- Treat failing Renovate PRs as maintenance work: inspect CI and push the smallest compatible fix to its branch when possible; otherwise use a replacement branch with required compatibility changes. Kubectl, Helm, and Velero CLI updates change exact versions while installation verifies exact-version publisher checksums. Velero runtime image and Terraform Helm updates remain digest-pinned native Renovate changes grouped with the CLI release; do not add a manual checksum tuple or detach one projection from that group.

## Branch And PR Hygiene

- Open or update a PR autonomously when an implementation branch reaches a coherent review checkpoint, subject to the topology and approval rules in [Change And Merge Policy](#change-and-merge-policy). Stacked PRs are acceptable when their base dependencies are explicit and they let independent work continue while CI or review runs. If the next authorized work is unambiguous and can be cleanly based on a merge-ready PR, continue autonomously in a stacked PR subject to those rules; a draft PR may expose in-progress work and should be marked ready at a coherent review checkpoint. After the parent merges, retarget the child to `develop` subject to those rules before rebasing or pushing. An explicit pause, unresolved human decision, or dependency requiring merged state stops continuation; do not create tiny placeholder PRs without review-worthy content.
- When active work moves to another worktree, report its absolute path and branch so the human's IDE context and delegated-worker paths remain aligned.
- CI — Validation runs for pull requests targeting any base branch, including stacked feature branches, with the same change classification, jobs, required Validation Gate, and summary as other PRs. Security, License, and CodeQL gates also cover every PR base, and relevant web-client changes receive ZAP validation. Push scopes remain unchanged: Validation, Security, License, and ZAP use `main` and `develop`, while CodeQL uses `main`. A stacked child must inherit the applicable workflow changes in its merge candidate before that coverage is available; existing children do not gain it merely because this PR is open. Smoke and the credential-free runtime-image producer also cover feature bases with their existing runtime path classification; irrelevant changes receive an explicit no-op. Validation success alone does not establish smoke proof. Exact tuple selection, parent-advance refresh, and the separate preview/publication restrictions are owned by the [CI/CD runtime and preview chain](../architecture/system-architecture-cicd.md#pull-request-runtime-and-preview-chain).
- When retargeting a stacked PR after its parent merges, change the base before rebasing or pushing the child. Required workflows run replacement validation for base-ref edits and isolate title/body edits from substantive runs. Required gate names and exact original-proof attribution are owned by [CI/CD: Required Gate Preservation](../architecture/system-architecture-cicd.md#required-gate-preservation). Deferred metadata gates publish a pending required status, and trusted completion callbacks clear that barrier only after checking current original proof. A dependency-deferred failure is missing proof, not merge readiness. Ambiguous automatic-rerun outcomes require explicit recovery.
- For recurring branch, worktree, and PR inventory, use `dev-tools/validation/report-worktree-pr-topology.sh` rather than ad hoc status commands. Renovate PRs are omitted from the selected inventory by default; the report records them in `omitted_renovate`, and `--include-renovate` opts them back in. Pass `--pr <number>` for a bounded direct base/dependent stack and `--json` for machine-readable evidence; selected reports correlate only exact branch/SHA identities and fail closed on ambiguous matches, while reporting local worktree dirtiness and unpublished or stale local heads.
- After a merge, remove its defunct local worktree and merged local/remote branch only after confirming no open PR or active stacked branch depends on it. Preserve unmerged branches and worktrees.
- Pass PR-body Markdown through a file or stdin with real newlines rather than literal `\\n` strings.
