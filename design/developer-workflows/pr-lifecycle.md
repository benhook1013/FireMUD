# Pull Request Lifecycle

Use this guide for pull-request status, review handling, CI, Renovate, merging, or branch/worktree cleanup.

## Review And Status

**Review policy:** Follow the configured queue. Hosted and CLI advance independently, with at most one active request per channel. A default Hosted review period requires at least two completed attributable rounds and allows at most three; its normal taper remains one zero-useful result. A default CLI period requires at least three completed attributable rounds and allows at most six; its normal taper remains three consecutive zero-useful results. The budget closes further discovery when exhausted, whether or not taper completed. An accepted finding owned by that PR breaks its channel's zero-useful streak and must be fixed; a routed finding does neither. Specific human stops and existing per-PR allocations govern their own PR and channel. Head, parent, patch identity, fixes, and ordinary carry do not reset taper or budget counters. Merge readiness still requires accepted fixes, clear threads, and required CI.

Use the `firecontroller-use` skill with this worker’s supplied own role-bound connection and the guide shipped with the installed release. That external owner defines command syntax, connection selection, storage, attribution, recovery, and publication mechanics. Use `firecontroller-onboard` only for setup or connection repair. Do not select another worker’s connection or treat tool availability as authorization. FireController Worker owns controller service changes; this repository owns the FireMUD policy below.

- CodeRabbit automatic reviews remain disabled. FireController is the official review, finding, adjudication, route, and status record. The source review queue, channel allocations and closures, native obligations, sealed legacy data, captures, migration proof, and frozen comparison sites remain retained. Imported history is historical evidence, not native current-code coverage. Repository source retirement does not authorize state deletion, migration, runtime promotion, or service changes.
- For status or merge readiness, inspect all paginated current and outdated unresolved threads and the latest completed review summary before CI and mergeability. Verify summary-only duplicate and outside-diff findings. Missing, malformed, unattributed, or incomplete evidence fails closed. Report outdated unresolved threads separately.
- Each request must bind the PR, exact committed child head, effective parent identity and exact parent head, provider-observed diff base/ref, merge-base SHA, and unique patch identity. The effective parent is the nearest preceding unmerged configured PR, or the repository default base after predecessors merge. Ordinary admission requires coherent adjacent ancestry and parent identity; parent movement and unreconciled state remain holds. A known topology warning may be acknowledged only through supported guarded operations with exact candidate identity and admission-time rechecks. Such acknowledgment cannot bypass candidate uncertainty, provider ceilings or quota, active requests, caps, channel stops/completion, or accepted-finding obligations. Never describe the expected queue parent as the provider’s diff base when they differ.
- Preserve completed review history across fixes, head/base/parent changes, reconciliation, and ordinary carry. Hosted taper is one completed, attributable zero-useful result for that PR; CLI taper is three consecutive zero-useful results. Default-period results count across reviewed heads. Accepted source-owned findings break that channel’s active streak; routed findings do neither. Invalid, duplicate, and already-fixed-only completed results are zero-useful; a literal zero/zero is unnecessary. Failures, partial results, rate limits, duplicates of the same result, provisional captures, and unattributable attempts do not consume review credit or budgets.
- Completed taper does not reopen automatically. An explicitly authorized reopen or fresh-taper allocation starts a fresh streak while retaining earlier history. Ordinary bounded allocations do not reset taper. A channel judgment must bind the verified evidence and exact candidate transition; it grants no review credit. Legacy transitions retain honest missing-evidence labels, do not invent checkpoints, and do not silently convert unverified evidence into counting results. Retiring missing or ambiguous legacy evidence requires exact authenticated evidence and the supported guarded recovery path.
- Starting a new default period requires an explicit authorized, attributable administrative decision with an exact head and a retained completion boundary or stable current-history baseline. Preserve findings, routes, captures, reservations, publication intents, reconciliation, other channels, and prior periods. Results already completed but awaiting publication or recovery remain baseline results; late historical or unverified completions earn no new credit. Budget exhaustion, ordinary fixes, and parent movement never authorize a reset. An explicit User stop requires actual User resumption or renewal; recording an override is not authority to grant it.
- Record human stops and allocations separately for each PR and channel, independently of request readiness or stack reconciliation. A bounded allocation counts attributable completed results beginning at the decision; a verifiably admitted in-flight request counts when it completes. The default additional allowance is one result. Minimum-only allocations have no maximum; maximum-only allocations have minimum zero; reject minimum greater than maximum. An exact additional allocation sets equal minimum and maximum and preserves taper; it cannot also request fresh taper. Ordinary taper may stop after the minimum and before the maximum; exact allocations require all allocated results. Fresh taper begins at the decision, not at an earlier dry result. Recording an allowance clears no admission hold.
- Exhausting the maximum closes discovery for that PR/channel and advances that channel independently, even with accepted fixes pending. Retain the visible “cap exhausted; findings pending” obligation and audited human-cap stop. This is neither taper completion nor merge proof. A human stop also advances only discovery and persists across identity changes; it does not erase findings or retire an admitted run. At most one active request per channel may run repository-wide. An already admitted run finishes with its reservation, capture, attribution, and eventual findings intact.
- For a channel needing review, stop at the first held, unstable, unreconciled, over-ceiling, or judgment-blocked PR; skip completed or explicitly stopped channels. A verified terminal non-counting Hosted response may permit the next coherent PR’s Hosted window while leaving the ambiguous source visibly incomplete. Never request again on that source or grant coverage without resolving attribution or obtaining an authorized stop. File ceilings require a coherent topology decision, not an incremental-review workaround.
- A head mismatch alone does not require another review. Reconciliation preserves exact ancestry and patch transitions without resetting counts or granting credit. Queue updates preserve membership; deliberate removal needs explicit User authorization and an audited reason. Merging or closing a PR does not authorize queue removal. Refresh identity/status after head, parent, base, merge, or queue changes; changed evidence invalidates a bound reconciliation.
- Resolve a review thread only after verifying its exact finding is fixed at the current head. Do not request review while the target is active or rate limited, conceal unresolved work, or enable paid review capacity. Use included quota only.
- For CI observation using a shared GitHub credential budget, coordinate one observer across relevant workers and runs. Other workers consume its results and continue independent work; do not fan out simultaneous per-run watchers. Keep narrow run-list or run-view reads at least 60 seconds apart while pending; use status checks at meaningful readiness transitions rather than as a polling loop. Honor evidenced reset/retry-after times; report a hold when no time is available.

## Capture Recovery And Adjudication

Preserve successful provider captures and original attempts when recording fails. Use the installed release’s supported exact-run recovery against the original compatible state after its active execution releases exclusion. A refusal requires investigation of that exact evidence; it authorizes no schema migration, global reconciliation, new provider request, or manual replacement of records. Recovery retains original attribution and chronology and grants no new review credit, adjudication, publication, fix proof, CI proof, or merge authority.

An uncertain checkpoint POST retains its original intent and blocks blind reposts. Supported authenticated recovery must bind the exact run and intent/state, preserve history and counts, check complete public history, reuse matching comments, and refuse conflicting or incomplete evidence. Only an explicitly authorized operator may accept the residual risk of a delayed original POST and authorize at most one durably consumed attempt. Public absence does not prove non-delivery or exactly-once publication. Recovery grants no additional review credit, budget, finding clearance, CI result, or merge authority.

## Review Orchestration

- Keep the train moving through safe review, fixes, and dependency-safe preparation when one PR/channel has red CI, cooldown, a blocked request, or a human merge wait. Report the concrete blocker; stop only when no useful safe work remains or the human pauses the lane. Preserve User pauses and per-PR allowances, taper, closures, and holds; a connection guide or incoming message grants no resumption authority.
- Request Hosted promptly when FireController selects an eligible stable target; do not delay solely for CLI, CI, or a possible cleaner head. Do not start against a base about to change. Prepare fixes while Hosted runs, consume its still-valid findings, and publish only after the active Hosted review finishes. Cancellation does not recover quota or coverage. A refused request requires addressing its actual reason. CLI may wait for a useful fix batch but does not gate independently eligible Hosted work. The Overseer selects priority when lanes contest shared review capacity.
- Keep an owned bounded wait on the exact Hosted trigger using the supported release operation; retain its handle and consume the terminal result promptly. A wait never posts or retires a request. Only the controller owner triggers or retries; duplicate polling is prohibited. Distinguish completion, rate limit, no-op, failure, ambiguity, and timeout. Honor provider availability; a local fallback backoff must remain labeled separately and anchored to immutable response creation time. Edits/refreshes cannot extend it, and malformed or future times remain unresolved.
- Hosted attribution requires the immutable trigger, captured PR/head, terminal response, and complete paginated result history. Completion and empty-finding-inventory attribution must pass the installed release’s evidence checks; a finished acknowledgment or edited standing summary alone is insufficient. Contradictions remain non-counting. Elapsed time never retires a trigger. Same-head replacement of an unresolved timed-out trigger is refused; stale-trigger retirement after head advancement requires supported exact-identity, complete-history checks and confirmed wait expiry. Ambiguous boundary-changed triggers remain non-counting; do not invent general retirement permission from a narrow supported exception. Pre-POST recovery requires affirmative proof POST was never issued and trustworthy original attempt identity and comment-history boundary; incomplete legacy reservations remain held.
- CLI review covers an isolated exact published committed candidate and verified published file list, excluding dirty source edits. Retain immutable attempts, provider events, timing, captures, and any warning acknowledgment. Do not infer current-code coverage from imported history or recording success.
- Adjudicate every raw finding as accepted, routed, or rejected with a concrete outcome/reason. Accepted findings are source-owned correction obligations; routed findings name another owner without claiming that owner’s acceptance or fix. Keep routes stable and source-linked, avoid duplicate routes, and notify a named target with a concise issue, source checkpoint, and route reference. Target owners independently disposition incoming routes and verify resolutions; retargeting retains the open obligation and prior ownership history, including when honestly unassigned. Routes do not hold the source’s review selection/taper or merge readiness. Corrections preserve original provider events, publication intents, checkpoint counts, and audit history; newly accepted findings still require verified fixes.
- Summary-only dispositions bind the immutable summary identity and its whole finding bucket. Individually route each routed observation; accepted-unfixed remains a hold, and historical accepted-fixed evidence does not clear a current-head summary obligation without current verification. Thread resolution remains separate.
- Promptly finalize an adjudicated native completed run through the supported operation to freeze counts and publish or reuse its canonical checkpoint; fixes and their proof may follow. Local imports, captures, repairs, and subagent completion do not themselves publish. Recording and GitHub publication are not atomic: retain frozen counts and the original intent after failure and use supported recovery. Publication grants no fix, taper override, CI, or merge-readiness proof.
- Public checkpoints report `Hosted|CLI: R Found / A Accepted / T Routed · <sha> · N files · <duration>` with controller-provided linkage and duration markers. Raw found equals accepted plus routed plus rejected/duplicate findings. Preserve immutable reviewed identity, request-time file count, and exact elapsed seconds. Show durations as `Ns`, `Mm SSs`, or `Hh MMm SSs` as appropriate. Hosted duration spans trigger to attributable terminal result; CLI duration spans only provider execution, excluding cooldown, preflight, waits, and delayed publication. Historical two-count or duration-less evidence remains historical with unknown fields, never fabricated coverage.
- After review/fix work, report raw/useful counts, significance, dispositions, channel targets, remaining obligations, and whether more review is justified. Use [Validation And Runtime Proof](./validation-and-runtime-proof.md) proportionately. The Overseer samples accumulated decisions and receives meaningful milestones; commissioned model-review evidence follows [AI delegation and review](./ai-delegation-and-review.md#independent-review-evidence).

## Review Completion Criteria

- For each candidate, verify its live head and base, effective parent and unique patch, current and outdated unresolved threads, latest review summary, PR title/body and generated LOC metadata, required exact-head CI, and mergeability. Report recent findings and their significance, each channel's decision, proof limits, and the next action. Reconcile parent movement without losing the child's owned patch or rewriting historical review counts.
- During existing CI and PR-readiness checks, inspect available patch coverage for important changed behavior that lacks tests. Add focused proof for material gaps or identify existing executable proof. Distinguish incomplete reports from missing tests; informational coverage success is not proof of adequate coverage. Recheck after material behavior changes. This check does not change review admission or add a CI wait, percentage target, extra review round or repository-wide coverage campaign.
- Define a coherent capability or correction scope and obtain an initial substantive review through the controller covering feature-bearing work and changed contracts.
- Resolve every known actionable finding, including current and outdated threads and summary-only or outside-diff findings, and verify each fix at the current head. Useful in-scope corrections to correctness, completeness, authority, alignment, maintainability, consolidation, or clarity remain actionable regardless of line size; judge worthwhile suggestions by their actual benefit against implementation cost and risk, including when they address neither a bug nor an existing design requirement, and do not relabel them optional merely to finish.
- Re-review new functionality, changed contracts, materially broader risk, or fixes that introduce independently reviewable behavior. Direct, verified corrections need not trigger another whole-PR discovery round solely to accumulate clean results.
- The channel defaults above are the controller's normal completion policy, not a quota target. Request work beyond a completed policy only for a concrete unresolved risk, coverage gap, or explicit human override. Repeated serious findings trigger focused investigation of their shared failure mechanism and appropriate proof rather than arbitrary clean-round accumulation.
- Under an explicit Overseer arrangement, present the Hosted/CLI trend and a nonblocking recommendation when marginal utility is uncertain. The Overseer—not the implementing lane or controller—decides whether an additional deliberate round is justified and whether evidence is ready for the User's product-PR approval.
- A normal product-queue PR, including a mixed product and maintenance PR, is ready for User approval only after controller evidence shows zero unresolved current and outdated threads, all actionable summary-only and outside-diff findings are addressed, every open incoming route is dispositioned by the target owner, each fix is verified, every required channel has completed its policy through attributable provider results, required CI is green, and proportionate validation/runtime proof is complete. Open routes from this PR to other owners do not hold its merge readiness. An explicit human stop advances only that channel's discovery queue; it remains a visible review limitation, not taper or merge-readiness proof. Before handoff, refresh stale title/body content and the generated marked LOC section with `python3 dev-tools/maintenance/cloc-report.py pr <number> [--repo owner/name] --update-pr`. The section is informational, not a size gate. If tooling is unavailable, record the limitation instead of inventing counts. Zero threads or GitHub `CLEAN` alone is insufficient, and the final head must not be described as reviewed when it was not. The implementing worker reports readiness without taking merge action. Review closure, channel overrides, READY/CLEAN state, green CI, and a lane readiness handoff do not authorize any agent to merge or enable or schedule auto-merge. Only a traceable, explicit User instruction to get a specified product PR merged authorizes the designated Overseer to merge that PR after verifying the required proof. Separately authorized controller/private-website maintenance merges and closure of verified-superseded PRs remain limited to their existing scope.

## Change And Merge Policy

- Keep PRs coherent, including adjacent convergence. Use smaller PRs for isolated fixes; split independent or genuinely hard-to-review work, and split coherent work that exceeds a reviewer ceiling under the following rules.
- Hosted CodeRabbit reviews have a verified hard limit of 100 changed files; the controller refuses a Hosted request above that count and records a current provider skip as a Hosted limitation. The CLI review path has completed successfully with 111 changed files, but its upper limit is not established. Do not infer a CLI cap from the Hosted limit or from the plan page; verify any higher CLI boundary before documenting it.
- Require an actual complete Hosted CodeRabbit review for every feature-bearing boundary. A complementary CLI or model review does not substitute for Hosted review. Before restructuring, inventory the complete coherent boundary, including code, contracts, setup and configuration, call sites, tests, documentation, and any affected owning workflows. Plan independently sound feature boundaries at around 80 Hosted-selected changed files to leave room for repairs and proof below the hard 100-file ceiling. The resulting count is an outcome, never permission to omit required code, contracts, setup, call sites, tests, documentation, or semantics. Useful review findings define required improvements, not optional scope: accepted useful findings remain implementation obligations in their canonical code, documentation, or proof owner. A provider limit may change branch topology or review boundaries, but never correctness, cleanliness, or which live issues are fixed; do not offset growth by dropping useful scope, packing unrelated helpers or tests into already-counted files, moving authority to a noncanonical owner, or changing runtime or design semantics.
- A provider skip caused by exceeding the Hosted ceiling is not review coverage. An oversized feature-bearing PR without substantial completed CodeRabbit coverage must be restructured into coherent reviewable boundaries, or work must stop with the blocker reported; repeated subagent reviews and CLI/model reviews do not waive Hosted coverage. Only an exact human-authorized disposition grounded in verified substantial CodeRabbit coverage of the feature-bearing work can stop a specific Hosted discovery request for that oversized PR. Record that request as unrun and the channel limitation; it earns no completed-review credit, and all remaining authorized-scope, review, budget, CI, runtime, thread, and merge obligations still apply. Do not infer a general incremental-review path or automatic exception for other substantially reviewed PRs, or treat a file count as authority to bypass review.
- Treat reviewer ceilings as dynamic topology constraints. Prefer the fewest coherent, independently sound reviewable PRs that preserve every accepted finding and canonical placement under the cap; additional PRs add separate review, taper, and reconciliation costs, so narrowness or neat separation alone is insufficient. If the correct implementation exceeds the active ceiling, default to a coherent stack that preserves every finding and its proof; consolidate only when independently justified by cohesion and authority, without forcing unrelated work together. Do not target a hardcoded file count or preserve an arbitrary majority merely to stay under a cap. The ceiling changes review topology, never correctness or cleanliness.
- When a finding or proof moves to another PR, update its existing PR body and review disposition with the successor reference and check whether downstream already fixes it. After a split or reconstruction materially changes a reused PR's review scope, post one short comment such as **Review scope changed:** validator cleanup moved to #2731. Earlier counts cover the previous scope. Add `<!-- firemud-review-scope-change -->` below it and record the timestamped scope transition in FireController using the installed release’s supported operation. Earlier evidence remains relevant to the previous patch boundary, and still-applicable findings remain obligations, while the event does not by itself require a fresh review streak or imply increased risk. An ordinary post-merge retarget with equivalent scope needs no reset comment. Do not create a new ledger.
- The project owner grants the configured merge-train operator standing authorization for a coherent split when accepted findings or an owned invariant would cross the provider ceiling, or when a natural owner/proof boundary makes one PR materially hard to review. No fresh routine approval is needed. Preserve every finding and the published parent patch and review history; keep the parent fail closed if a child supplies required behavior. Open the smallest coherent stacked child on the verified parent, identify moved observations and their owner in both PRs, prove each unique patch, reconcile descendants at a Hosted-safe point, then update and read back the controller queue. Route a source thread to a named target without claiming the target fix; resolve an in-boundary accepted thread only after its correction is published and validated. A split does not grant taper or merge readiness. Do not create artificial extraction PRs, drop findings, or publish review-invalidating changes during active Hosted.
- Outside that standing merge-train authorization, an explicit Overseer arrangement permits isolated preparation of a concrete topology proposal, but publishing a split, prerequisite, or successor, or retargeting an existing PR, requires Overseer approval. An approved proposal covers its planned routine retargets and publication without repeated approval. Without an explicit Overseer arrangement, the human's selection of an active workstream authorizes routine scope-safe topology changes. Bring a materially more fragmented topology to the Overseer with scope, dependency, and review-cost comparisons. Escalate when no coherent split preserves the invariant, ownership is materially uncertain, or the change introduces consequential product, architecture, or activation choices; continue independent safe work meanwhile.
- Treat failing Renovate PRs as maintenance work: inspect CI and push the smallest compatible fix to its branch when possible; otherwise use a replacement branch with required compatibility changes. Kubectl, Helm, and Velero CLI updates change exact versions while installation verifies exact-version publisher checksums. Velero runtime image and Terraform Helm updates remain digest-pinned native Renovate changes grouped with the CLI release; do not add a manual checksum tuple or detach one projection from that group.

## Branch And PR Hygiene

- Open or update a PR autonomously when an implementation branch reaches a coherent review checkpoint, subject to the topology and approval rules in [Change And Merge Policy](#change-and-merge-policy). Stacked PRs are acceptable when their base dependencies are explicit and they let independent work continue while CI or review runs. If the next authorized work is unambiguous and can be cleanly based on a merge-ready PR, continue autonomously in a stacked PR subject to those rules; a draft PR may expose in-progress work and should be marked ready at a coherent review checkpoint. After the parent merges, retarget the child to `develop` subject to those rules before rebasing or pushing. An explicit pause, unresolved human decision, or dependency requiring merged state stops continuation; do not create tiny placeholder PRs without review-worthy content.
- When active work moves to another worktree, report its absolute path and branch so the human's IDE context and delegated-worker paths remain aligned.
- CI — Validation runs for pull requests targeting any base branch, including stacked feature branches, with the same change classification, jobs, required Validation Gate, and summary as other PRs. Security, License, and CodeQL gates also cover every PR base, and relevant web-client changes receive ZAP validation. Push scopes remain unchanged: Validation, Security, License, and ZAP use `main` and `develop`, while CodeQL uses `main`. A stacked child must inherit the applicable workflow changes in its merge candidate before that coverage is available; existing children do not gain it merely because this PR is open. Smoke and the credential-free runtime-image producer also cover feature bases with their existing runtime path classification; irrelevant changes receive an explicit no-op. Validation success alone does not establish smoke proof. Exact tuple selection, parent-advance refresh, and the separate preview/publication restrictions are owned by the [CI/CD runtime and preview chain](../architecture/system-architecture-cicd.md#pull-request-runtime-and-preview-chain).
- When retargeting a stacked PR after its parent merges, change the base before rebasing or pushing the child. Required workflows run replacement validation for base-ref edits and isolate title/body edits from substantive runs. Required gate names and exact original-proof attribution are owned by [CI/CD: Required Gate Preservation](../architecture/system-architecture-cicd.md#required-gate-preservation). Deferred metadata gates publish a pending required status, and trusted completion callbacks clear that barrier only after checking current original proof. A dependency-deferred failure is missing proof, not merge readiness. Ambiguous automatic-rerun outcomes require explicit recovery.
- For recurring branch, worktree, and PR inventory, use `dev-tools/validation/report-worktree-pr-topology.sh` rather than ad hoc status commands. Renovate PRs are omitted from the selected inventory by default; the report records them in `omitted_renovate`, and `--include-renovate` opts them back in. Pass `--pr <number>` for a bounded direct base/dependent stack and `--json` for machine-readable evidence; selected reports correlate only exact branch/SHA identities and fail closed on ambiguous matches, while reporting local worktree dirtiness and unpublished or stale local heads.
- After a merge, remove its defunct local worktree and merged local/remote branch only after confirming no open PR or active stacked branch depends on it. Preserve unmerged branches and worktrees.
- Pass PR-body Markdown through a file or stdin with real newlines rather than literal `\\n` strings.
