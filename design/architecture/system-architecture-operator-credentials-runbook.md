# FireMUD Operator Credentials Runbook

This runbook describes how operators obtain, rotate, and revoke the credentials used to access FireMUD’s internal-only control-plane surfaces (for example diagnostic endpoints and management APIs protected by mTLS).

This document is intentionally narrow and operational. For the architecture and trust model, see `design/architecture/system-architecture-security.md`.

## Operator Client Certificates (mTLS)

Operator access to internal-only endpoints relies on mTLS client certificates. Target endpoint validators additionally require the current environment-owned bounded operator credential generation/allowlist and exact-match the generation's non-empty `activeLeafFingerprints` set plus any currently unexpired `overlapLeafFingerprints` set, together with the required URI SAN/profile; active and overlap sets are disjoint, and every overlap leaf is rejected at or after the absolute `overlapExpiresAt` deadline, while a replacement that is in the active set remains accepted after that deadline. CA trust and NetworkPolicy placement alone do not authorize. The binding representation and environment selection follow the [canonical target operator credential binding record](./system-architecture-deploy-preflight-policy.md#canonical-target-operator-credential-binding-record), selected by the [deployment preflight operator-credential rules](./system-architecture-deploy-preflight-policy.md#operator-credential-representation-rule), rather than a second endpoint-local configuration. Validators and workflows use that record's immutable `publishedAt` as the publication/effective timestamp and require any `overlapExpiresAt` to be later than it.

**Target-state disclaimer:** Initial issuance, routine rotation, and incident revocation below are target guidance until the canonical binding record, endpoint-side generation/leaf-identity enforcement, and accepted/rejected readback evidence are implemented. The current repository's binding-reference/fingerprint validators do not establish those lifecycle guarantees, so none of the three procedures is current operational proof.

### Issuance

- Operator certificates must include `clientAuth` extended key usage and the required operator URI SAN/profile.
- Issuance is source-specific: `SECRET_BACKED` resolves the selected dedicated existing operator-managed Secret and file-backed client material without invoking cert-manager; `CERT_MANAGER` uses the selected issuer/profile, separate from workload identities, and its dedicated output Secret; and `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY` updates and verifies the provider-projected client identity and trust bundle without creating a substitute certificate or Secret.
- Operator gRPC clients must resolve the trust bundle, client certificate chain, and private key from the selected `operator-credential-binding/v1` record according to its binding type: a Secret-backed binding reads its named dedicated operator Secret; a cert-manager binding resolves the named Certificate/issuer binding and its declared Secret output; and a workload-identity binding resolves the platform-projected client identity and trust bundle from that binding. Missing, unknown, or mismatched source type fails closed. Do not present the service `FIREMUD_GRPC_*` environment variables as operator-tool inputs: those paths remain the internal workload gRPC TLS identity and peer-trust configuration. Gateway HTTP management tooling instead uses its dedicated `FIREMUD_GATEWAY_HTTP_*` trust bundle and client identity paths, as described in the [environment and secrets catalog](./infrastructure/environment-and-secrets-catalog.md#gateway-http-management-plane-tls-target-state). See `system-architecture-security.md`.
- Initial issuance creates generation 1 with its immutable `operationId`, `operationKind=INITIAL_ISSUANCE`, the exact active leaf, URI SAN/profile, endpoint/workload audience, immutable `publishedAt`, and an empty overlap set. Immutable readback must prove every field of the complete canonical record—not only the leaf—including the exact target environment and binding selector, recorded `bindingType` and all source-specific resolution evidence, generation, `publishedAt`, active/overlap leaf sets, URI SAN/profile, endpoint/workload audiences, `operationId`/operation kind, the required absence of an overlap deadline for initial issuance, and immutable readback evidence bound to the record and generation; it then proves the new leaf is accepted and no previous leaf exists. The full evidence contract remains owned by the [deployment preflight policy](./system-architecture-deploy-preflight-policy.md#canonical-target-operator-credential-binding-record).

### Storage and Distribution

- For Secret-backed bindings, store the operator certificate and private key in the named dedicated Kubernetes Secret, which is not mounted by normal workloads. For cert-manager bindings, cert-manager owns the named Certificate and its declared output Secret; operators must not hand-edit that output. For workload-identity bindings, use the platform-projected client identity and trust bundle and do not create a substitute operator Secret.
- Restrict Kubernetes RBAC or the workload-identity provider policy to the operator tool or workload that resolves the selected binding; a Secret-backed or cert-manager output Secret must be readable only by the minimal approved operator-facing tools or jobs.
- Restrict which pods can reach operator endpoints using NetworkPolicies; possession of a valid certificate must not be sufficient outside approved network placement.
- For workstation use, export only a Secret-backed or cert-manager-issued credential from its selected binding output and store it in a secure operator-only location (password manager, encrypted vault, or OS keychain); workload-identity credentials remain provider-projected. Do not commit exported keys to the repository.

### Rotation

Rotate operator certificates:

- On a fixed cadence, and
- Immediately after personnel changes, suspected device compromise, or any incident involving operator surfaces.

Rotation steps:

1. Resolve the selected binding type and stage its replacement without changing what approved tools use: prepare the dedicated existing operator-managed Secret and file-backed client material for `SECRET_BACKED`; issue through the selected issuer/profile into the dedicated output Secret for `CERT_MANAGER`; or stage and verify the provider-projected client identity and trust bundle for `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY`. Before publication, validate only the source material and pre-publication candidate fields: exact leaf/identity fingerprint, required URI SAN/profile, certificate chain or provider trust evidence, target environment and binding selector, recorded `bindingType` and source-specific resolution, proposed generation, operation kind, active/overlap leaf sets, and the overlap-deadline presence/order rule. Do not claim complete immutable record/generation-bound readback before publication; that evidence can be produced only after the atomic generation publish.
2. Keep approved operator tools/workstations on the current active leaf while the replacement is staged and the endpoint remains on the current binding generation. Do not distribute or activate a `SECRET_BACKED` or `CERT_MANAGER` replacement yet. For `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY`, approved tools/workloads continue using the current provider-projected identity and trust bundle; do not export a credential or create a substitute Secret.
3. For a routine rotation, atomically publish the next environment binding generation with its new immutable `operationId`, `operationKind=ROTATION`, the validated replacement as the active leaf, and the old leaf in the bounded `overlapLeafFingerprints` set, together with immutable `publishedAt` and an absolute UTC `overlapExpiresAt` satisfying `overlapExpiresAt > publishedAt`. Only after successful publication, read back and exact-match every field of the complete immutable canonical record and generation-bound evidence, including that `operationId`, and verify that both exact leaves are accepted only before that deadline. Only after that readback succeeds, activate the replacement in approved tools: install the staged `SECRET_BACKED` or `CERT_MANAGER` output, or switch approved `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY` tools/workloads to the refreshed provider projection. If publication or readback fails, keep the tools on the old leaf and do not activate the replacement.
4. Atomically advance the generation for cutover with its own immutable `operationId` and `operationKind=ROTATION` no later than `overlapExpiresAt`, removing every old leaf from the active set, clearing the old overlap leaf, and applying the canonical presence rule for the overlap deadline after the overlap set is empty. At or after the deadline, endpoint validation must reject the old leaf even if cutover evidence is late; read back every field of the complete binding record and endpoint decision—including exact target environment and selector, recorded type/source and all source-specific resolution evidence, audiences, active/overlap leaves, URI SAN/profile, `operationId`/operation kind, generation, `publishedAt`, overlap-deadline presence/value, and immutable record/generation-bound readback evidence—to prove the replacement exact leaf is accepted and the previous leaf is rejected. Verify access from an approved operator surface and verify that normal workloads cannot reach operator endpoints.

### Revocation / Incident Response

If an operator credential is suspected compromised:

- Rotate immediately with no post-incident overlap while preserving the old leaf until cutover: for `SECRET_BACKED`, stage and validate the selected dedicated operator-managed Secret/file-backed client material; for `CERT_MANAGER`, issue and validate the selected issuer/profile's dedicated output Secret; for `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY`, stage and verify the provider-projected client identity and trust bundle. Keep approved tools/workloads on the current active leaf and do not distribute, activate, or switch them to the replacement before publication. Validate the replacement's exact leaf/identity fingerprint, URI SAN/profile, and certificate chain or provider trust evidence. Atomically publish the next binding generation with its new immutable `operationId`, `operationKind=REVOCATION`, immutable `publishedAt`, containing only that replacement, an empty `overlapLeafFingerprints` set, and no `overlapExpiresAt`; read back every canonical binding field—the exact target environment and selector, recorded `bindingType` and all source-specific resolution evidence, generation, `publishedAt`, active/overlap leaf sets, URI SAN/profile, endpoint/workload audiences, `operationId`/operation kind, the required absence of an overlap deadline, and immutable record/generation-bound readback evidence—and verify old rejection plus replacement acceptance. Only after publication and readback succeed, activate the replacement in approved tools/workloads: install the staged `SECRET_BACKED` or `CERT_MANAGER` output, or switch to the refreshed `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY` provider projection. If staging, publication, or readback fails, keep the old leaf active and do not activate the replacement.
- Tighten NetworkPolicies/allowlists so that access is limited to the smallest possible operator blast radius during the response window.
- Record the binding generation, exact old/replacement identities, readback results, and reason in the incident record. **Current implementation/proof gap:** the canonical binding record, endpoint-side generation/leaf-identity allowlist enforcement, and accepted/rejected readback evidence are not yet live in the repository; the target-state disclaimer above applies.
