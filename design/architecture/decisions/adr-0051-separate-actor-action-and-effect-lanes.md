# ADR 0051: Separate Actor-Action and Effect Lanes

## Status

Accepted

## Implementation Status

The live bounded scheduler proves only the universal selection model. It does not yet prove the revised actor-action/passive-effect lane classification, lane-specific budgets, or retry and replay behavior; those remain implementation and focused-proof gaps.

## Context

Regional hybrid ticks provide deterministic fairness and bounded work, but the existing universal one-work-item-per-entity rule conflates actions an entity chooses with effects imposed on it. Poison, incoming attacks, environmental work, remote follow-ups, or their retries could consume a player's only action opportunity. Reversing priority could instead starve accepted effects and reconciliation.

## Decision

FireMUD retains independently advancing regional ticks but separates two deterministic scheduling lanes.

The **actor-action lane** selects at most one root intentional action originated by an entity per tick. Player commands, AI decisions, automation commands, timers explicitly defined as making that actor act, and retries of those actions compete in this lane. A retry preserves the original action identity and never creates additional same-tick agency.

The **passive/inbound-effect lane** processes status pulses, environmental effects, incoming remote effects, actor-generated consequences, and retries of already-admitted effects without consuming the target entity's actor-action slot.

Canonical phase order is:

1. due start-of-tick passive effects;
2. one selected actor action per eligible entity; and
3. effects generated by those actor actions.

Only phase-1 effects completed before phase-2 eligibility evaluation can make an entity ineligible in that same tick. Work selected but not completed, or deferred by lane or budget overflow, affects a later tick and retains its ordering and effect identity. Actor-generated effects do not retrospectively remove an already executed action. Neither lane may recursively grant another root actor action in the same tick.

Both lanes use persisted deterministic ordering, per-entity count and cost caps, region-wide work budgets, bounded carry-over/backoff, and pressure metrics. Overflow is deferred without changing its ordering or effect identity. New work sources must declare their lane and cost class; they cannot silently gain a custom scheduler.

## Consequences

- One actor cannot take multiple turns in a tick.
- Incoming attacks and routine passive work cannot be used to confiscate the target's turn.
- Passive-effect floods remain bounded by separate entity and region budgets.
- The scheduler becomes more complex and may execute more than one mutation involving an entity during a tick.
- Deterministic phase order creates explicit gameplay timing semantics that authored effects and tests must respect.

## Alternatives Considered

### One Universal Work Item Per Entity

Rejected because work associated with an entity is not necessarily an action chosen by that entity. It permits denial-of-action through incoming work and forces player agency to compete with convergence.

### Unbounded Passive Effects Beside One Action

Rejected because it preserves agency but allows effect floods, chained amplification, and unbounded tick time.

## Implementation and Proof Obligations

Implementation must classify every current work source, preserve deterministic ordering and replay identities, apply both per-entity and regional budgets, prove bounded deferral under effect floods, and cover simultaneous command/poison, multiple incoming attacks, action/effect retries, ineligibility after start-of-tick effects, and reset/replay behavior.

## Reversibility and Revisit Triggers

Lane classification and budgets can evolve without changing the regional tick timeline. Revisit the two-lane boundary if measured workloads show phase-order anomalies, passive-effect cost dominates tick budgets, or a gameplay system needs more than one intentional actor action per tick as an explicit mechanic.
