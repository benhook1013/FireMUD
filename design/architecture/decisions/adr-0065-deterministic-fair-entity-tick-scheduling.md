# ADR 0065: Deterministic Fair Entity Tick Scheduling

## Status

Accepted

## Decision Record

- Decision date: 2026-07-19
- Decision key: `TICK-09`
- Primary capability: `GR-1.2` tick scheduling, execution, and deterministic command resolution
- Affected capabilities: `SF-2.2`, `SF-2.3`, `GR-4.1`, `PO-4.2`
- Decision owner: FireMUD human product and architecture owner
- Consultation: human-led adversarial review with independent contract validation and global-order, per-entity scheduler, pipelined-tick, and concurrent-execution alternative analysis

## Context

A deterministic global sort is replayable but not necessarily fair. If a region budget can serve fewer entities than remain backlogged, old work from the same early entities can repeatedly fill the budget before later entities are reached. One global sequence allocator also serializes admission from commands, timers, retries, and remote follow-ups.

The former “one item per entity” summary additionally conflicts with ADR 0051: one intentional actor action is selected per entity, while separately bounded passive, inbound, and generated effects may also involve that entity.

## Decision

Ticks retain three fixed semantic phases:

1. start-of-tick passive and inbound effects;
2. actor actions; and
3. effects generated by those actions.

Actor-action selection admits at most one intentional action per entity. Passive/inbound and generated effects use separately reserved regional count/cost budgets and per-target caps; they do not consume the actor-action slot or its reserved capacity.

Within one entity's eligible queue, work uses persisted deterministic ordering:

`(priority, due_tick_id, entity_enqueue_seq, source_kind, commandId_or_effectKey)`

`entity_enqueue_seq` is monotonic within the entity's current region epoch and assigned by the owning Game Session admission/scheduling path. Same-tick derived children use the persisted parent order plus deterministic child ordinals rather than completion timing. New sources map into this tuple and do not introduce private tie-breakers.

Across eligible entities, each lane uses persisted deterministic rotating/deficit scheduling. The scheduler accounts for declared work cost, advances only with the durable selected batch, and ensures a permanently eligible entity eventually receives capacity within a healthy epoch. Non-best-effort priority classes declare bounded aging or `maxDeferralTicks`; work explicitly classified as best effort follows its declared delay/drop policy.

Source-specific quotas may bound intake inside a lane, but source kind does not create a competing scheduler or allow timer, retry, automation, or remote work to bypass the lane rules.

The selected ordered manifest and scheduler-state advance are persisted atomically before source work becomes exclusive. Replay executes the recorded order rather than recomputing it from changed queues. Tail loss before that boundary neither advances the durable scheduler state nor removes the authoritative source record.

One region has one in-flight tick through final coordination cleanup. Tick `N+1` cannot stage until tick `N` is durably terminal and its matching epoch/tick/lease/fence cleanup has cleared pending state and locks.

## Consequences

- Sustained backlog cannot let the same early entities monopolize a region indefinitely.
- Replay remains deterministic because selection and scheduler advancement are durable facts.
- Entity-scoped sequence allocation removes one region-global admission counter.
- Persisted scheduler state, aging, and cost accounting add implementation and proof burden.
- Final cleanup remains on the serial tick path; this intentionally forgoes pipelining until its safety and measured need are demonstrated.

## Alternatives Considered

### One Region-Global Ordering Tuple

Rejected because determinism alone permits starvation under a bounded regional budget and requires one ordering allocator across all sources.

### Independent Source-Weighted Queues

Rejected as the ordering authority because source weights silently become gameplay priority and may starve recovery or player work. Bounded source quotas remain allowed inside the semantic lanes.

### Pipeline the Next Tick after Durable Commit

Deferred because old cleanup could race new pending state and locks. Adoption requires tick-namespaced coordination, stale-cleanup rejection, and cross-tick failure proof.

### Concurrent Entity Execution

Deferred because entity locks do not prove independence from shared room or multi-entity state, and completion timing would break deterministic within-tick visibility. It requires a separately recorded dependency/wave plan.

## Implementation and Proof Obligations

Proof must cover one actor action plus bounded effects, per-entity ordering, deterministic child ordinals, cost-aware rotating/deficit fairness, bounded non-best-effort deferral, reserved lane budgets, source-flood resistance, crash before and after batch persistence, replay from the recorded manifest, epoch reset, scheduler-state recovery, and the prohibition on staging tick `N+1` before matching cleanup of tick `N`.

The current runtime remains narrower and game-instance scoped; this decision does not claim the region scheduler or proof exists.

## Reversibility and Revisit Triggers

Weights, costs, and bounded deferral values may be calibrated without changing the authority model. Revisit the scheduler if measured state/selection cost is excessive. Revisit tick pipelining only when cleanup latency is a demonstrated bottleneck and the required namespacing and failure proof exist.
