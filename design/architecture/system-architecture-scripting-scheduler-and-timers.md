# FireMUD System Architecture: Scripting Scheduler and Timer Lifecycle

This document defines the runtime scheduler, timer, and reload lifecycle for scripting and automation. It complements [Scripting DSL Reference & Event Lifecycle](./system-architecture-scripting-dsl-reference-and-lifecycle.md), which remains the canonical owner for DSL semantics, trigger identity, event fan-out, and determinism rules.

## Implementation Status

The current implementation provides the following substrate:

- `NotifyScriptVersionUpdate` persists the durable patch-scoped schedule-definition catalog in PostgreSQL (`script_schedule_definitions`), and each observed `(tenantId, gameInstanceId)` pin materializes durable `script_schedule_instances` rows. Those rows retain `scheduleDefinitionId`, patch/plugin ownership, cadence/unit, priority, binding target identity and priority/exclusivity, `scheduleSemanticsHash`, the pin operation's `controlPlaneRequestId`, and mutable due-point state.
- Wall-clock timers persist `nextDueAt`. Tick-aligned schedules remain `PENDING_RUNTIME_PROGRESS` until Game Session reports `(tenantId, gameInstanceId, regionId, regionEpoch, tickId)` through `ObserveRuntimeTickProgress`; Automation then stores `runtimeRegionId`, `runtimeRegionEpoch`, `lastObservedTickId`, `lastRuntimeProgressObservedAt`, and the next future `nextDueTickId`.
- Game Session advances durable `lastCommittedTickId` on the runtime-ownership row that also carries `regionId`, `regionEpoch`, and `executorFence`, exposes that state through runtime ownership status, and publishes it after successful tick cycles. True region partitioning remains target-state; the live ownership row currently carries a transitional non-empty `regionId` scope marker, but consumers must not derive or reconstruct that value from `gameInstanceId`.
- Runtime observations stamp the fenced region scope onto wall-clock timers as well as tick schedules. Due tick and wall-clock candidates emit deterministic timer-derived `ScriptWorkItem` rows through the normal durable work-item, audit, and `automation:queue:*` projection path, then advance or clear the consumed due point.
- A newer observation that proves the runtime scope changed fences the stale due point instead of reminting it under the new epoch. The service records `runtime_scope_changed` against the old Trigger Identity and increments `automation_script_timer_runtime_fence_dropped_total`.
- Due candidates are selected in round-robin passes across complete stable schedule-instance identities and capped by `script.scheduler.max-catch-up-firings-per-observation`; this current per-observation behavior does not yet prove the accepted one-per-schedule-instance durable resume-window cap across repeated observations. `ObserveRuntimeTickProgress` reports updated rows, fired work items, and truncated candidates; timer-fired, catch-up-truncated, and runtime-fence metrics expose aggregate outcomes.
- Control-plane timer audit reads expose scheduler-owned `script_event_audit` rows, including plugin ownership, admitted routing, due-point coordinates, and skipped-versus-persisted outcomes. Plugin-owned definitions retain `pluginId`/`pluginVersionId`/`bindingId`, and materialization admits only the enabled runtime plugin version and resolved signed-bundle binding.
- The richer Redis leader/checkpoint model below remains target-state.

## Target-State Design

The sections below define the target-state scheduler contract. Current behavior remains limited to the implementation facts listed above unless a bullet explicitly identifies a target-state requirement.

### Script Timers vs Tick Timers

Every authored timer declares both its clock unit and recovery class. Tick/game time is the default for gameplay timers; wall clock must be explicit and is a scheduler-eligibility clock only. Timers remain layered on the authoritative runtime timeline, but not every deadline is a tick deadline:

- **Wall clock** timers use a persisted absolute due time such as `dueAt`; changing tick cadence does not rescale an already persisted deadline. `dueAt` remains only in internal durable scheduling, identity, seed, fencing, and audit metadata; it is excluded from DSL-visible trigger facts and gameplay inputs. Wall-clock eligibility enters the next canonical tick, and scripts can observe only that canonical tick/game-time context rather than a wall-clock value.
- **Tick/game time** timers use committed tick identifiers for eligibility and cadence; changing tick cadence changes the real-world duration of future intervals while preserving their game-time cadence.
- Time scaling is applied explicitly when a new duration or due point is calculated. A later cadence or time-scale change does not silently rewrite an already persisted due point.
- Recovery classes are:
  - **Correctness-bearing one-shot**: durable intent exists outside Redis before acknowledgement and converges to one logical execution or explicit terminal outcome; retries preserve identity.
  - **Durable recurring**: the schedule declares exactly one missed-occurrence policy, `SKIP_MISSED` or `COALESCE_ONE`. The latter may create at most one synthetic firing for the complete stable schedule-instance identity in one durable resume-window identity.
  - **Advisory or cosmetic**: missed occurrences may drop and the timer resumes with a future occurrence under its declared cadence.
- Redis timer indexes, due indexes, leader checkpoints, and wake-up hints are rebuildable coordination projections. Durable timer identity, due state, recovery policy, resume-window identity, and required terminal or skipped outcomes live outside Redis.
- A correctness-bearing one-shot must durably terminalize under its original identity with one of the accepted outcomes: `applied`, `canceled`, `superseded`, `expired`, or a feature-defined terminal outcome. A scheduler or worker crash may reclaim a fenced lease, but an inconclusive one-shot remains nonterminal; it must not be silently marked skipped or reminted under a newer scope.
- For timer catch-up, Automation owns one durable timer-recovery resume-window record per `<tenantId, gameInstanceId, playableStateScope, regionId, regionEpoch>`. A durable compare-and-set creates or returns the one `OPEN` record; its `resumeWindowId` is `<tenantId, gameInstanceId, playableStateScope, regionId, regionEpoch, resumeGeneration>`. The record freezes recovery start and through observations. Leader, worker, takeover, and attempt IDs are not identity inputs; retries and takeovers reuse the same `resumeWindowId`, and only a later recovery episode after completion allocates the next generation.
- An epoch transition atomically moves the prior epoch's `OPEN` resume window out of `OPEN` before creating the new-epoch `OPEN` window; prior claims remain fenced and reconciled under their original epoch and resume-window identity and cannot enter the new window, as owned by [ADR 0072](./decisions/adr-0072-class-specific-timer-durability-and-recovery.md).
- Missed firings are handled in a bounded, deterministic way:
  - `COALESCE_ONE` schedule instances may enqueue at most one synthetic firing for that complete stable schedule-instance identity within one `resumeWindowId`; `SKIP_MISSED` schedule instances enqueue none. One global fair cap applies across independent schedule instances within that same ID, and excluded candidates are dropped rather than deferred as backlog.
  - After a due candidate passes admission, and before enqueueing that firing, the scheduler must claim or insert a durable trigger-instance row keyed by an instance-aware uniqueness projection. For `triggerMode=CATCH_UP`, the firing claim uniqueness and comparison include `resumeWindowId`, and the candidate-audit record carries the same ID, so failover or duplicate consumers cannot create duplicate logical triggers or consume a later window's cap.
  - The projection must define the event-scope due-candidate identity, including `tenantId`, `gameInstanceId`, `playableStateScope`, `regionId`, `regionEpoch`, `eventType`, `eventSchemaVersion`, `scriptPatchVersion`, `isDryRun`, `scheduleDefinitionId`, the persisted due point (`dueTickId` or `dueAt`), and `triggerMode`; include `entityId` only when the schedule target scope is entity-scoped, and `resumeWindowId` for catch-up claims. When present on the durable schedule, its stable schedule-owned `scriptId`, `pluginId`, `pluginVersionId`, and `bindingId` are also part of the due-candidate/firing-claim identity. A handler-only binding field not present on that schedule must not be fabricated before handler resolution. The firing claim derives one event-scope `scriptEventId` from this complete due-candidate identity and propagates that ID unchanged into every resolved handler's full Trigger Identity. Globally unique schedule IDs do not replace runtime scope, version, dry-run namespace, or timeline fencing.
  - Candidates rejected by cadence, reload, version, or event-policy gates before handler binding are not replayed later; they are recorded as event-scope `scheduleCandidateId` audits with no firing claim or `scriptEventId`. A candidate that passes those gates creates at most one event-scope firing claim and deterministic event-scope `scriptEventId` for that due candidate, resolves handlers, and fans out zero or more handler work items, each with its own full Trigger Identity containing that same propagated ID; handler quota or budget denial is recorded per resolved handler in `script_event_audit` with no handler work item, lease, or execution marker.

Within that model:

- The Game Session Service owns authoritative tick progression and tick timers, as described in [Tick System and Runtime Design](./system-architecture-ticks.md).
- The Automation & Scripting Service owns script timers and intervals, which are scheduled against tick heartbeat information but do not own ticks themselves.
- Scheduler data structures such as `automation:timer:{tenantRegionTag}` are used to track when script timers should fire relative to tick progression. `{tenantRegionTag}` is the opaque shared-key-builder tag derived from the full `<tenantId, gameInstanceId, regionId>` scope; it is not a competing schedule identity. Durable script schedules and quotas live in the Automation & Scripting Service’s PostgreSQL schema, while Redis indexes are coordination structures that can be rebuilt or reset without changing which scripts should eventually run.

From the tick system’s perspective, script timers are a source of work that ultimately enters the tick scheduler. Every scheduler and timer source must declare immutable `lane` (`actor_action` or `passive_effect`) and bounded `cost_class` before admission, and must carry those fields unchanged through durable schedule state, firing claims, work items, retries, and recovery. Missing or changed values reject timer firing, admission, retry, or recovery rather than being inferred or reclassified. Under ADR 0051, `passive_effect` is bounded non-actor work governed by the passive lane’s budgets: status pulses, environmental changes, passive inbound or remote envelopes, actor-generated consequences, and retries of already-admitted passive effects. Root intentional player, AI, automation, movement, or other gameplay commands remain `actor_action`; a timer is `actor_action` when it makes an actor act. If a passive inbound envelope emits a gameplay command, that resulting command is `actor_action` and cannot grant another same-tick root action. The determinism rules in the DSL reference apply equally to timer-driven triggers, while lane selection and budgets remain owned by the tick contract.

#### Automation Command Admission (Target-State)

The target `EnqueueAutomationCommandIfAbsent` contract carries the complete applicable Trigger Identity and complete child Command-Handoff Identity (with `(automationDispatchId, commandOrdinal)` as only its final discriminator), the target runtime timeline and due point, immutable `lane` and bounded `cost_class` values, an immutable request digest, and the deterministic evaluated command descriptor/payload. The durable admission row stores those exact values before any tick-queue mutation. Duplicate admission is a replay/no-op only when the complete identities, target timeline/due point, request digest, deterministic command descriptor/payload, lane, and cost class all match; any missing, changed, or contradictory value is a non-mutating validation rejection and must remain fenced. Field ownership and canonical replay rules remain in the [Command-Handoff Identity](./system-architecture-scripting-normative-contract-tables.md#command-handoff-identity-target-state) and [tick-effect idempotency](./system-architecture-transactions.md#tick-effects-are-at-least-once-idempotency-is-mandatory) contracts.

The complete identity, target timeline/due point, request digest, deterministic command descriptor/payload, lane, and cost class are part of every target conflict, request-digest, readback, retry, and recovery comparison. A readback or retry with any missing, changed, or contradictory value rejects and remains fenced; recovery preserves the originally admitted values rather than deriving new scheduling metadata. This contract is target-state and does not make the current live wire or durable rows claim these fields: the live `EnqueueAutomationCommandIfAbsent` boundary remains narrower and does not yet carry the full Trigger Identity, `commandOrdinal`, `lane`, or `cost_class`, so live enforcement and focused proof remain outstanding as tracked in [Automation and Scheduler Runtime](../project-management/implementation-tracking/automation-and-scheduler-runtime.md).

#### Timer Resume Rule (Normative)

When reload, rollback, or schedule preservation keeps a logical timer alive across a version transition, the scheduler must recalculate its next due point using the same explicit rule:

- Inputs:
  - `resumeTickId` = the latest committed tick known for the timer's region when scheduling resumes for the runtime scope.
  - `previousDueTickId` = the durable due point stored before pause or reconciliation.
  - `intervalTicks` = the preserved schedule cadence in ticks.
- Preconditions:
  - Apply the preserved-due-point formula only when the preserved row's `runtimeRegionId`, runtime scope (`gameInstanceId` and `playableStateScope`), and `runtimeRegionEpoch` match the resumed runtime's corresponding region, scope, and `regionEpoch`.
  - If the runtime region, runtime scope, or region epoch differs, fence the old due point and derive a new due point on the new timeline after the replacement/new-scope schedule entry is durable; do not feed the stale `previousDueTickId` into this formula.
- Rule:
  - `intervalTicks` must be a positive integer. A schedule with `intervalTicks <= 0` is invalid and must be rejected before any modulo or due-point calculation.
  - if `previousDueTickId >= resumeTickId`, keep `nextTick = previousDueTickId`;
  - otherwise calculate `remainder = (resumeTickId - previousDueTickId) % intervalTicks`; set `nextTick = resumeTickId` when `remainder = 0`, and permit exactly one firing at that boundary under the durable schedule-instance plus resume-window identity; otherwise set `nextTick = resumeTickId + intervalTicks - remainder`, the first cadence boundary strictly after resume.
- Consequences:
  - the scheduler resumes on the next valid cadence boundary at or after resume, never by replaying every missed firing from the paused window;
  - a preserved schedule may fire immediately after resume only when its next valid cadence boundary is exactly `resumeTickId`;
  - reload and rollback preservation and downtime recovery remain distinct behaviors. The resume rule governs preserved timers after a version transition on the same runtime region, scope, and epoch; a scope or epoch migration fences the old due point and starts from the new timeline. The declared recovery class and durable resume-window policy govern missed firings after scheduler downtime within the same logical schedule instance and version.
  - Wall-clock timers preserve their persisted absolute due time across cadence changes and do not adopt tick rescaling; each schedule uses only its declared clock unit.

### End-to-End `onInterval` Timer Lifecycle

This section summarizes how a single `onInterval` timer behaves across normal operation, leader changes, and script reloads, and which Redis keys are authoritative at each step.

All durable timer identity and scheduler checkpoints must be instance-aware even when Redis keys are region-scoped for slotting and locality:

- `gameInstanceId` is a required part of timer identity, deterministic scheduler `scriptEventId` derivation, catch-up deduplication, and checkpoint projection state.
- If a Redis key name omits `gameInstanceId`, the stored timer or checkpoint payload must still include it, and rebuild logic must treat instance mismatches as corruption rather than as reusable timer state.
- Plugin timers must additionally carry `pluginId`, `pluginVersionId`, and `bindingId` in their durable identity so sibling bindings and plugin rollback, disablement, or revocation cannot cross-contaminate interval state.

#### Interval-Entry Identity and Due-Point Persistence

A durable interval has two identities that must not be conflated.

Stable schedule-instance row identity:

`<tenantId, gameInstanceId, playableStateScope, scriptId, pluginId?, pluginVersionId?, bindingId?, eventType, eventSchemaVersion, scriptPatchVersion, isDryRun, scheduleDefinitionId, targetScopeType, targetScopeId>`

- `scheduleInstanceId` is the stable durable row identity, whether represented by this natural key or by a surrogate row ID. It excludes the mutable runtime scope and due point. `runtimeRegionId`, `runtimeRegionEpoch`, `nextDueTickId`/`nextDueAt`, and last-observed progress are schedule state that may advance on heartbeat or reconciliation without creating a new schedule row.
- `scheduleDefinitionId` is the stable logical schedule identity within its patch or plugin owner, not the firing claim identity. `scriptPatchVersion` is the current definition owner, plugin-owned entries additionally carry `pluginId`, `pluginVersionId`, and `bindingId`, and `isDryRun` separates test execution from live scheduling. Live durable interval entries persist `isDryRun=false`.
- `duePoint` is exactly one mutable persisted value: `dueTickId` for tick-aligned schedules or `dueAt` for wall-clock timers. `nextTick` and `nextRunAt` are projections of that stored due point, not substitutes for it.
- Each due candidate has at most one event-scope durable firing claim and one deterministic event-scope `scriptEventId`, keyed by the complete stable schedule-instance identity (including schedule-owned `scriptId`, `pluginId`, `pluginVersionId`, and `bindingId` when present) plus the applicable runtime scope (`regionId`, `regionEpoch`), target selector identity (`entityId` only for an entity target scope), trigger mode, and tagged due point. The claim then fans out to zero or more handler work items, each with its own full Trigger Identity containing that same propagated `scriptEventId`; handler-only identity fields are added only after resolution. Advancing the schedule due point must never rewrite a prior claim or mint a second ID.

#### Normal Operation

- When an instance first observes a pinned patch, Automation materializes the patch-owned schedules for that `(tenantId, gameInstanceId)` scope into durable `script_schedule_instances` rows keyed by the stable schedule-instance identity above, with plugin owner metadata and binding target identity. Each row stores `scriptPatchVersion`, `isDryRun`, `scheduleDefinitionId`, cadence, unit, scheduler priority tag, normalized schedule metadata hash, `controlPlaneRequestId` from the pin observation, and exactly one mutable due-point field (`dueTickId` or `dueAt`). Wall-clock timers may initialize `dueAt` from the observed pin timestamp; tick-aligned schedules remain persisted but `PENDING_RUNTIME_PROGRESS` until Game Session's `ObserveRuntimeTickProgress` input supplies the current runtime region epoch and tick id from Game Session's durable runtime ownership row. At that point Automation records the runtime region fields and derives the first future `dueTickId` as `observedTickId + cadenceValue`; later observations that reach or pass it admit the due candidate before creating at most one event-scope firing claim and one deterministic event-scope `scriptEventId` for that due candidate, then use the same durable work-item, audit, and queue projection path as other admitted script events. Plugin-owned schedules are materialized only for the plugin version currently enabled in Automation's runtime registry for that instance; other published or previously active plugin versions must not keep durable schedule ownership. Region-scoped Redis indexes such as `automation:timer:{tenantRegionTag}` remain a derived coordination layer above those durable rows rather than the first source of schedule identity.
- Leaders track these interval entries alongside other automation timers using bounded scans. Event scope applies cadence, reload, version, and event-policy gates, then creates at most one event-scope firing claim and one event-scope `scriptEventId` per due candidate before resolving handlers. Quota-allowed handlers become durable queued work, each with its own full Trigger Identity containing that same propagated ID; the later execution scheduler considers queued handler work in canonical order and reserves the artifact-estimate ordered prefix up to `AUTOMATION_TICK_BUDGET_MS`, leaving the remainder durably queued/deferred under unchanged handler identity. The reservation is not actual execution time, a per-run timeout, or a lease debit; actual runtime never changes the selected prefix or creates a same-tick refund, and the quota owner separately governs fenced capacity lease acquisition and recovery.
  - When an interval becomes due, the scheduler derives a deterministic `scheduleCandidateId` from the stable schedule-row identity, applicable runtime region and epoch, and tagged due point. It applies cadence, reload, version, and event-policy gates at event scope before handler binding. A gate failure is durably consumed with its `scheduleCandidateId`-keyed audit outcome: advance the canonical due point according to the cadence, or quarantine/tombstone the schedule when safe advancement is impossible; it creates no firing claim or `scriptEventId`. Once the candidate passes those event gates, the scheduler creates at most one event-scope durable firing claim and one event-scope `scriptEventId`, resolves handlers, and lets the quota owner decide each handler. Quota-allowed handlers create durable queued work, each with its own full Trigger Identity containing that same propagated ID; handler quota denial is handler-scoped `ADMISSION/quota_denied` with no work item, lease, or execution marker. Before `EVALUATED_COMMITTED`, a failed handler evaluation may retry under that same full Trigger Identity and same propagated `scriptEventId`; after the boundary, recovery replays its durable descriptors without re-entering the DSL. The later execution scheduler applies the artifact-estimate ordered-prefix reservation to queued handler work and defers the remainder without changing identity.

An admitted firing must use one crash-safe durable transition. The transition atomically creates or claims the firing, records one handler-scoped `script_event_audit` row for every resolved handler's admission outcome, including quota-denied handlers with `finalOutcome=quota_denied`, persists work items and rebuildable `automation:queue:*` projections only for quota-allowed handlers, and advances the canonical due point. Leases and execution-start markers remain later execution-stage artifacts. If the storage boundary cannot provide one transaction, it must persist a resumable idempotent reconciliation keyed by the firing claim and `scheduleCandidateId`; recovery must link or complete every missing audit, work-item, queue-projection, and due-point update before the firing is considered committed, without re-running the DSL or minting a second `scriptEventId`/`automationDispatchId`. A crash may leave the operation resumable, but must not leave a durable claim that can be rediscovered as new work or a due point advanced without the corresponding durable handler outcomes and admitted work evidence.

#### Leader Changes

- Leaders advance a per-region notion of time by consuming the tick heartbeat stream and tracking how far they have progressed. In addition to the current heartbeat `(tenantId, gameInstanceId, regionId, regionEpoch, tickId)`, schedulers maintain a derived discovery hint such as `script-scheduler:{tenantRegionTag}:lastTickId`. The opaque `{tenantRegionTag}` physically carries the full `<tenantId, gameInstanceId, regionId>` scope, while the payload stores `regionEpoch` and `latestTickId`. Consumers must compare the stored epoch with the authoritative current epoch before reusing the progress value. The separate per-consumer `tick-events-offset:{tenantRegionTag}:<consumerId>` record is the sole owner of that consumer's `streamOffset`.
- When leadership changes, the new leader:
  - reads the scheduler hint and canonical event-offset record for the current `regionEpoch`;
  - rejects either value when its stored `regionEpoch` does not match the current heartbeat/status epoch. It must not walk old-epoch ticks, reuse an old stream offset, or reuse old-epoch due points. The mismatch is recorded in scheduler audit with `reason=checkpoint_region_epoch_mismatch` and increments `automation_script_timer_runtime_fence_dropped_total{scope, script_category, eventType, reason="checkpoint_region_epoch_mismatch"}`;
  - discards each stale value, rebuilds the current-epoch scheduler hint and event-offset record from the authoritative durable status/heartbeat, and then reconciles durable due points under the bounded catch-up policy; and
  - enumerates the authoritative PostgreSQL `script_schedule_instances` rows for the current runtime scope to determine which current-epoch "every N ticks" boundaries were crossed during the gap. The Redis region index `automation:timer:{tenantRegionTag}` may accelerate this scan, but it is rebuildable and must not be the source of truth; if it is missing or stale, rebuild it from the durable rows before admitting candidates. It obtains or creates the one `OPEN` durable resume-window record by CAS, freezes that record's recovery start and through observations, and admits each due candidate before claiming or inserting a durable firing claim keyed by the stable schedule-instance identity, current runtime scope, tagged due point, and `resumeWindowId`; only then may it enqueue an `onInterval` trigger.
  - Each `COALESCE_ONE` schedule instance may enqueue at most one missed `onInterval` trigger before normal scheduling resumes; `SKIP_MISSED` schedule instances enqueue none. `SCRIPT_TIMER_CATCH_UP_MAX_FIRINGS_PER_RESUME` is the global cap across complete stable schedule-instance identities for the one durable `resumeWindowId`. Candidates beyond this cap are dropped, not deferred, and surfaced through candidate audit and `automation_script_timer_catchup_truncated_total`. The window is marked `COMPLETE` only after admitted and cap-excluded candidate outcomes are durable; only a later recovery episode then allocates the next generation.
- Leader changes and takeover reconciliation preserve each firing's declared lane and cost class and validate the current runtime epoch/fence before admitting it; stale or ambiguous claims are fenced and reconciled rather than reclassified or replayed as new work.
- Catch-up truncation must use one stable fairness algorithm when due candidates exceed `SCRIPT_TIMER_CATCH_UP_MAX_FIRINGS_PER_RESUME`:
  - Collapse each overdue schedule instance to its earliest missed tagged due point in its declared clock domain (`dueTickId` for tick/game time or `dueAt` for wall clock). Do not numerically compare tick IDs with wall-clock timestamps. Build the resulting schedule-instance candidate set by bounded `priorityTag` (`high`, `normal`, `background`), then complete stable schedule-instance identity ASC.
  - Admit catch-up firings in round-robin passes across distinct complete stable schedule-instance identities (the identity defined above, including target scope and plugin-binding dimensions), not merely `scheduleDefinitionId`, so independently materialized instances sharing one definition each receive an opportunity and one noisy instance cannot consume the entire resume window.
  - Admit at most one missed firing for any logical schedule instance in the complete `resumeWindowId`, using its earliest missed tagged due point as the coalesced identity.
  - Once the resume cap is reached, remaining overdue firings are truncated rather than deferred into an unbounded backlog; emit audit/metrics that distinguish how many were coalesced or dropped.
- If a per-script index is used, it is reconciled against the region index as needed; discrepancies are treated as projection bugs and corrected, not as new timers.
- Because the authoritative schedule configuration lives in PostgreSQL and Redis holds only coordination state (timer indexes and checkpoints), leader changes do not reset cadences; they only introduce a bounded delay before the new leader catches up.

#### Script Reload

- During reload, leaders set `reloadState=RELOADING` for the affected runtime scope after observing that Game Session has pinned a tenant-`READY` patch for that instance. `onLoad` is not part of this instance reload path; it has already completed as part of tenant patch readiness. Existing timer entries in the region index `automation:timer:{tenantRegionTag}` and any derived per-script projections remain in Redis but are treated as pending until reconciliation completes.
- Once reload succeeds and `activePatchVersion` is switched, the leader:
  - re-reads its heartbeat checkpoint and the current `tickId`;
  - reconciles durable schedule definitions for the newly pinned patch before any timer is allowed to fire again, updating the instance-scoped `script_schedule_instances` rows first;
  - updates each surviving interval entry’s next due point (`nextTick` or `nextRunAt`) using the normative timer resume rule so the cadence resumes from the latest tick and time rather than replaying the paused window; and
  - resumes normal scheduling for `onInterval` using the updated `activePatchVersion`. No interval runs against a partially loaded script definition.
- If reload fails or durable reconciliation evidence is unavailable, `pendingPatchVersion` is marked failed/fenced and any partially loaded schedule state is discarded. The affected runtime scope admits no new timer firing until reconciliation confirms the current pin, schedule owner, scope, epoch, and due state; timer due candidates encountered while fenced are recorded in their `scheduleCandidateId` candidate audit with the applicable fenced/version-unavailable outcome and reason, with no firing claim or `scriptEventId`. A prior active patch may remain observable for diagnosis, but the scheduler must not use a "last known good" fallback to mint or execute new timer work.

Timer reconciliation on patch or plugin change is a required part of reload and rollback safety:

- Durable timer identities must be version-scoped by the schedule definition that created them, including `scriptPatchVersion` and, for plugin-owned schedules, `pluginId`, `pluginVersionId`, and `bindingId`; they must not be keyed just by entity and cadence.
- Every compiled timer or interval definition must carry a stable `scheduleDefinitionId` generated by the Game Design compilation pipeline and persisted with the schedule metadata. This identifier represents one logical schedule definition within a patch or plugin version.
- A patch or plugin version must not contain duplicate `scheduleDefinitionId` values within the same owning scope; compilation and publish must fail deterministically if duplicates are present.
- Implementations must persist a `scheduleSemanticsHash` or equivalent canonical normalized fingerprint with each compiled definition. Reconciliation may preserve a `scheduleDefinitionId` only when the old and new definitions have the same digest; a changed digest requires a new `scheduleDefinitionId`, even if the logical intent appears similar.
- When an instance observes a newly pinned `scriptPatchVersion`, the scheduler must compare the durable schedules for the previously observed patch with those for the newly pinned patch before resuming timer admission. Plugin activation, disablement, rollback, and revocation perform the same comparison for `(pluginId, pluginVersionId, bindingId)`.
- Schedules that do not exist in the newly pinned patch must be tombstoned so they can no longer generate triggers. When there is no replacement owner, disablement or revocation must fence admission and tombstone the displaced row atomically under the same control-plane operation; it must not fabricate a replacement schedule.
- Schedules may be preserved across patch or plugin changes only when the old and new definitions share the same `scheduleDefinitionId`, the same resolved `playableStateScope`, and the same canonical `scheduleSemanticsHash`. This three-field match is required before any mutable due state is carried forward. Matching by cadence, node shape, entity binding, or other inferred semantic similarity is not sufficient; if any of the three fields differs, the replacement has a new schedule identity and must derive due state through the normative resume rule rather than inheriting the old due point.
- `controlPlaneRequestId` is the reconciliation idempotency key. The same nonblank value from the initiating patch pin, plugin activation, disable, rollback, or signer-revocation request must be persisted on and carried through every schedule-reconciliation step, including preserved-schedule replacement, retirement, and retry; a sub-step must not mint a new reconciliation key.
- When a preserved schedule keeps the same `scheduleDefinitionId`, resolved `playableStateScope`, and `scheduleSemanticsHash`, reconciliation must create or confirm the replacement `script_schedule_instances` entry keyed by the newly pinned `scriptPatchVersion` and, when applicable, `(pluginId, pluginVersionId, bindingId)`, then retire the old version-owned entry before scheduling resumes. Replacement creation and retirement must commit as one atomic durable reconciliation result. If they cannot commit atomically, reconciliation must be a durable, resumable, idempotent operation keyed by `controlPlaneRequestId`: retries must create or confirm the replacement before finalizing the old-row tombstone, and the old entry remains authoritative until the replacement is durable. An interrupted rollback must never leave the logical schedule without a live row. Only this three-field match permits carrying the old due state; otherwise the new entry must recalculate `nextTick` or `nextRunAt` from the normative timer resume rule. Reconciliation creates only the replacement schedule identity; it does not create a firing claim or `scriptEventId`. After a due candidate passes admission, the scheduler mints the new firing claim and derives its `scriptEventId`; it must never rewrite the old entry or reuse its trigger identity.
- The replacement-before-retirement rule applies only when a replacement owner exists, including plugin activation or repin and rollback flows: reconciliation must create or confirm the replacement schedule owned by the new `(pluginVersionId, bindingId)` before retiring the displaced row under the same `controlPlaneRequestId`, and normal scheduling must not resume until that atomic or resumable operation completes. Disablement or signer revocation without a replacement instead fences admission and atomically tombstones the displaced row under the same `controlPlaneRequestId`.
- Canceling outbox work items alone is insufficient for rollback safety; old-version timer schedules must also be reconciled so they cannot mint new `scriptEventId` values after the version has been displaced.

Plugin example:

- If plugin version `combat-helper@v4` and `combat-helper@v5` both compile a "reapply guard aura every 20 ticks" timer with `scheduleDefinitionId=combat-helper.guard-aura.v1`, reconciliation atomically replaces the `v4` entry with a `v5` entry, or durably resumes an idempotent replacement operation that creates or confirms `v5` before retiring `v4`. It recalculates the next due point from the resume rule before scheduling resumes; the `v5` entry receives a firing claim and `scriptEventId` only after a due candidate passes admission.
- If `combat-helper@v5` replaces that timer with a different logical schedule such as "pulse only while threat > 0" compiled to a different `scheduleDefinitionId`, reconciliation must create the new timer for `v5` before retiring the old `v4` timer under the same atomic-or-resumable rule.

Under this model, durable script schedules, quotas, and firing-claim de-duplication live in PostgreSQL, while `automation:timer:{tenantRegionTag}`, `script-scheduler:{tenantRegionTag}:lastTickId`, and related coordination keys form a reset-tolerant coordination layer for interval state. Stored entries and reconciliation logic remain instance-aware even though the Redis keys are region-scoped for slotting. The combination of tick heartbeat, durable firing claims, epoch-validated checkpoints, and script patch versioning preserves both correctness and determinism across failures and leader changes: losing or resetting these Redis keys may delay or slightly reshuffle timer firings within the tail-loss envelope but must not change which scripts are eventually scheduled according to their stored configurations or cause duplicate logical trigger creation.

### Scheduler Leadership & Coordination

Scheduler leadership and coordination ensure that script timers and Automation-owned scheduling windows are processed safely in a distributed environment:

- Leadership must remain explicit and bounded, but the canonical design does not currently require a separate first-class `script-leader:*` Redis prefix. Scheduler ownership should instead be documented through the same runtime and coordination surfaces used by the Redis and automation docs unless a later design update introduces a dedicated lease family.
- Only the current leader for a runtime scope processes that scope’s script timers and automation queues. Implementations may lease by tenant for operational simplicity only if the leased worker still preserves per-instance isolation in its timer, queue, and reload state.
- Automation scheduling coordinates with tick heartbeat streams to ensure that:
  - `onInterval` triggers fire on the correct tick boundaries;
  - per-tenant and per-script quotas are respected; and
  - work is sharded in a way that keeps multi-key operations hash-tag-local.

See [Tick System and Runtime Design](./system-architecture-ticks.md) and [`automation-scripting-service/README.md`](./microservices/automation-scripting-service/README.md) for the current leadership and sharding model.

### Hot Reload & Resume Behavior

- Scripts are versioned and published via the Game Design Service; the Game Session Service pins an active `scriptPatchVersion` per game.
- When a new script patch is published, the Automation & Scripting Service:
  - ingests the new definitions and validates them for tenant readiness;
  - runs tenant-scoped `onLoad` handlers while the patch is still in `PENDING_VALIDATION` / `ONLOAD_RUNNING`;
  - marks the patch `READY` or `FAILED` for the tenant; and only then
  - uses instance-scoped reload state (for example, `reloadState=RELOADING`) when a running game pins that already-`READY` patch.
- During reloads, triggers are paused or skipped while in-flight runs drain under existing concurrency settings:
  - New triggers for the affected runtime scope are not admitted; attempts to schedule additional runs receive explicit backpressure outcomes (`skipped_reloading` during `RELOADING`, `rollback_paused` during rollback pause). For a low-rate runtime event denied before handler resolution by rollback pause, ingress records `admissionOutcome=TRIGGER_ADMISSION_OUTCOME_BACKPRESSURE_ROLLBACK` and `admissionReason=rollback_pause` in `script_event_ingress_audit`; this is an event-scope outcome, not a handler `finalOutcome`. While that event-scope claim remains `IN_PROGRESS`, a concurrent retry may wait for the same claim to finalize or receive a retryable transport/application signal without an admission result; recovery resumes the same claim under fencing. Once finalized, a retry with the same event-scope identity replays permanent denials and successful final results exactly; an explicitly retryable transient backpressure denial stores retry eligibility and hint, and after that window the same identity may atomically reclaim and reopen the fenced claim for a new admission attempt, retaining the same `scriptEventId` and event identity. Once a handler has resolved, retries of that handler use its same full Trigger Identity and the same propagated `scriptEventId`; no separate handler ID is minted. A pre-handler `FAILED` reload is different: ingress records `admissionOutcome=TRIGGER_ADMISSION_OUTCOME_VERSION_UNAVAILABLE` and `admissionReason=reload_failed` with no retry hint, while timer candidate skips remain in `scheduleCandidateId` candidate audit and create no firing claim or `scriptEventId` until durable recovery returns the scope to `IDLE`. Resolved-handler failures continue to use `finalOutcome` in `script_event_audit`.
  - In-flight runs remain bounded by each script’s configured `maxConcurrent` and `concurrencyPolicy` (for example, `queue_until_free`); any short per-script waiting queues are allowed to drain, but no new entries are added while `reloadState=RELOADING`.
  - Pending timer-based triggers that became due during reload remain in the scheduler’s timer indexes until version reconciliation completes, then resume only for schedules that still exist under the newly observed patch or plugin version with recalculated `nextTick` or `nextRunAt` so cadences remain coherent.
- On success, the new `scriptPatchVersion` becomes active for future triggers; on failure:
  - `activePatchVersion` remains an observed diagnostic value, but it does not authorize new timer work while reconciliation is unresolved;
  - `pendingPatchVersion` is marked failed/fenced along with an error reason, and leaders discard any partially loaded state for that patch;
  - Ordinary pre-handler ingress referencing a failed or unknown patch, or a scope whose reconciliation evidence is stale, is rejected in `script_event_ingress_audit` with `admissionOutcome=TRIGGER_ADMISSION_OUTCOME_VERSION_UNAVAILABLE` and the applicable `admissionReason`; it does not receive a handler `finalOutcome`. Timer due candidates under the same condition are recorded by `scheduleCandidateId` in candidate audit with the applicable outcome/reason and no firing claim or `scriptEventId`. Only a resolved handler records `finalOutcome=version_unavailable` (or another applicable fail-closed outcome, with specific cause in `finalReason`) in `script_event_audit`; metrics such as `automation_script_triggers_dropped_total{reason="version_unavailable"}` must not change that stage split.

- Timer-based triggers such as `onInterval` and `onTimerExpire` always execute against the currently pinned `scriptPatchVersion` for the game at the moment they are evaluated; they do not continue running older definitions after a patch is promoted.
- Older script versions remain in the Automation & Scripting Service database for auditing and potential rollback, but only the pinned active version is used for live execution.
