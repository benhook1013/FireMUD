# Stripe Integration Design

This document is the canonical Account owner contract for FireMUD's **Stripe v1 hosting-plan billing** in a multi-tenant, auditable way. It does not enable creator/player monetization.

At a high level, the goals of the integration are:

- Provide one concrete Stripe integration for FireMUD's own hosted-service subscriptions.
- Keep hosting-plan billing, provider lifecycle, reconciliation, and tenant entitlement authority in Account.
- Keep sensitive Stripe data and API keys confined to the Account Service boundary.
- Ensure idempotent, auditable billing flows that cooperate with existing saga and multi-tenancy patterns.

## V1 Scope and Deferred Commerce

Stripe is the sole supported v1 provider for FireMUD hosting-plan subscriptions under [ADR 0143](../../decisions/adr-0143-stripe-v1-hosting-billing-and-deferred-creator-monetization.md). Account owns provider identifiers, billing state, webhook inbox, reconciliation, audit correlation, billing-owner binding, and the hosting-plan entitlement/availability outcome consumed by lifecycle and admission services. Checkout, payment-instrument management, subscription changes, hosting-plan refunds, and billing-owner transfer use HTTPS account/control-plane and Stripe-hosted flows. Billing-safe management remains reachable when gameplay is denied for billing reasons.

Player purchases that grant gameplay value, player-paid game subscriptions, creator donations/tips, revenue sharing, platform fees on creator transactions, creator payouts, and settlement are deferred. Existing generic PaymentIntent, donation, creator-share, currency, or refund surfaces are partial substrate and do not constitute a supported product path or entitlement authority. Off-platform receipts, provider claims from an unintegrated system, and creator assertions never create FireMUD entitlements. A future marketplace requires a separate accepted settlement/entitlement design covering merchant of record, creator verification, KYC/sanctions/tax, fees, payouts/reserves, fraud, disputes/chargebacks, negative balances, fulfillment, and value-conservation/refund behavior.

The future direction in [ADR 0179](../../decisions/adr-0179-firemud-managed-creator-commerce-boundary.md) adds no V1 capability or provider scope. Any future managed creator-player transaction would require explicit marketplace opt-in by the Account-owned Creator Party and would use Account for provider reconciliation and entitlement authority; hosted-content acceptance, payment, tenant roles, or billing ownership do not enroll a party. Exact marketplace routes, schemas, fee, payout, and settlement behavior remain unimplemented and must not be inferred from existing payment code.

## Implementation Status

The hosting-subscription, provider-reconciliation, and account-deletion billing-owner precondition contracts below are canonical target-state behavior. Current payment code records payment transactions, donations, refunds, platform fees, and creator shares, but still needs a verified webhook lifecycle, durable idempotent hosting-billing fulfillment/reconciliation, caller-stable subscription create retry evidence, active-subscription deletion guards, and end-to-end entitlement enforcement. The current generic `CreatePaymentIntent`, `CreateDonation`, and `RefundPayment` implementations are provider-mutating drift: those methods are unsupported/deferred and must reject before any Stripe mutation. Only separately typed hosting-billing operations may mutate Stripe; this authority slice does not change the code. `purchase_entitlement` and paid-but-unfulfilled compensation are reserved for a separately accepted future marketplace path under [ADR 0179](../../decisions/adr-0179-firemud-managed-creator-commerce-boundary.md), and remain unsupported until its launch gates are implemented and proved.

The account-owned wallet contracts are also target state. Per-subscription instrument binding, replacement-before-detach, the single-use transfer proof and database transfer fence, immutable predecessor-provider history, and the audited `cross_tenant_billing_safe` ownership-transfer route remain absent or partial until the corresponding routes and enforcement are implemented; the [Authorization Route Matrix](../../system-architecture-authz-route-matrix.md) records that coverage drift.

## Domain Model

The Account Service owns billing records and maps them to Stripe resources. Records use immutable internal IDs for stable identity; for `subscription`, the internal `subscriptionId` is the sole stable identity and `tenantId` is its immutable tenant-scope binding. `accountId` and `plan_code` are mutable lookup, authorization, and constraint fields, not key material. No generic update or billing-owner transfer may change `tenantId`.

The database is the final authority for the one-subscription-per-tenant invariant. If historical subscription rows are retained, the target schema has an explicit immutable supersession marker such as nullable `superseded_at` and an explicit PostgreSQL partial unique index on `(tenant_id) WHERE superseded_at IS NULL`, for example `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_subscription_active_tenant ON subscription (tenant_id) WHERE superseded_at IS NULL`; a schema that never retains superseded rows uses a plain unique constraint on `tenant_id`. Superseded rows remain retained for history and are excluded only by the index predicate. A forward migration must query duplicate `tenant_id` values among rows that would satisfy the active predicate (for example, `SELECT tenant_id, COUNT(*) FROM subscription WHERE superseded_at IS NULL GROUP BY tenant_id HAVING COUNT(*) > 1`), reconcile every duplicate group before creating or validating the active-tenant unique index or constraint, preserve superseded history, and prove the resulting uniqueness and concurrent-create behavior; a `find-then-insert` check or application lock without this migration-backed database invariant is not sufficient. The create transaction locks the tenant authority row or an equivalent tenant-scoped database/advisory lock, reads the current non-superseded row, and inserts the operation and subscription under that same transaction. Replacing a canceled current row uses that same tenant lock in one atomic transaction: Account locks the current non-superseded row, verifies `status = canceled` and the expected row version, marks the row superseded with its immutable audit linkage, and only then inserts the replacement with a new immutable `subscriptionId` and operation identity. The canceled row remains retained for history, the partial active/non-superseded index excludes it after the supersession update, and a rollback leaves the original row current. The concurrent-create proof covers both empty-tenant insertion and canceled-row replacement: competing replacements serialize on the tenant lock, while a stale row version cannot supersede the row or insert a second replacement. A concurrent loser is resolved by the unique index and returns the existing canonical subscription or a deterministic idempotency conflict without calling Stripe. Provider resources, transfer history, and superseded history can never create a second local authoritative subscription.

The target persistence contract has a separate provider-subscription mapping invariant: every non-superseded subscription row with a non-null `provider_subscription_id` is the one canonical local mapping for that provider ID, and no other non-superseded row may carry the same ID. `provider_subscription_id` may remain null while provider linkage is not confirmed; any subscription eligible for activation or billing still requires a confirmed non-null ID. The history-retaining schema enforces the mapping with `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_subscription_active_provider ON subscription (provider_subscription_id) WHERE superseded_at IS NULL AND provider_subscription_id IS NOT NULL`. This partial predicate deliberately permits null pending or provider-absent terminal rows and preserves provider IDs on superseded history while guaranteeing that each current provider ID resolves to exactly one canonical local `subscriptionId`; a provider ID with no current local row is an unresolved mapping, not an invitation to select a row heuristically.

The forward migration runs under a subscription/provider-write fence before either partial index is relied upon. It snapshots every subscription row, provider ID, immutable `subscriptionId`, tenant, subscription operation, transfer cutover, webhook-inbox correlation, and provider evidence; groups duplicate non-superseded provider IDs and reconciles each group against those durable records and authoritative Stripe state; marks losing or stale local rows superseded with immutable audit linkage to the canonical subscription; and repairs a canonical row's stale provider ID only through its row-version/CAS boundary. Old IDs and superseded rows, transfer history, and webhook evidence are retained rather than deleted or silently reassigned. An unresolvable conflict blocks index creation and is an explicit migration failure requiring audited resolution, not a guessed mapping. After reconciliation, the migration creates and validates both partial indexes, proves that the query `SELECT provider_subscription_id, COUNT(*) FROM subscription WHERE superseded_at IS NULL AND provider_subscription_id IS NOT NULL GROUP BY provider_subscription_id HAVING COUNT(*) <> 1` returns no rows, and replays every linked subscription-lifecycle inbox case to verify that provider-ID lookup returns exactly one canonical local subscription. A missing mapping remains durably pending for reconciliation; a duplicate mapping is an invariant failure. This migration proof, plus a concurrent-create test against the tenant index, is required before the target webhook lookup is considered unambiguous.

The provider-ID history mapping is immutable and separate from the current subscription row. It records each predecessor or replacement Stripe subscription ID, the canonical immutable `subscriptionId`, its `transferRequestId` when applicable, its role (`canonical`, `predecessor`, or `replacement`), the effective boundary, and terminal resolution state. Webhook and reconciliation lookup resolves a predecessor ID through this mapping to the canonical subscription and transfer; predecessor events may update transfer evidence but must never overwrite the canonical row's provider binding. Once predecessor termination is durably confirmed, later events for that mapped ID are terminally ignored for billing authority. An unknown, conflicting, or missing mapping remains pending or quarantined for explicit reconciliation; no provider ID may be assigned to a local row by recency, tenant, customer, plan, or any other heuristic.

- `payment_transaction`  
  - Represents a financial/provider attempt or completed charge. In v1, the supported product is hosting-plan billing; one-time purchases and donations remain deferred substrate.
  - Key fields include internal ID, `accountId`, tenant binding where applicable, amount/currency, status (`pending`, `succeeded`, `refunded`, `failed`), provider (`stripe`), and provider ID. Legacy `platform_fee_cents`, `creator_share_cents`, and `donation` fields do not widen product scope.
  - Payment rows are financial evidence only and never entitlement authority.

- `purchase_operation`
  - Is deferred future-marketplace substrate for a one-time purchase or donation; it is not a supported v1 hosting-billing API or entitlement authority. Any future use requires the separately accepted marketplace/settlement and value-conservation contract.

- `refund_operation`
  - Is the durable operation and webhook-correlation record for one requested refund. The current canonical contract supports only a full refund of the transaction's remaining refundable amount; partial refunds require a separate financial-state and entitlement policy before they may be admitted. Before any Stripe refund call, Account commits its immutable refund operation ID, authenticated actor, target `payment_transaction`, exact full remaining amount/currency and reason, request digest, expected transaction/entitlement versions, stable Stripe idempotency identity, and outbox work.
  - Provider IDs, transaction status, entitlement revocation or non-revocable-consumption evidence, and the terminal outcome are reconciled against that same operation. A timeout, lost response, early webhook, or local commit failure remains nonterminal and retries the same provider identity rather than issuing another refund.
  - Any future unused-hosting-prepayment refund arising from changed-term exit is a separate target-only billing outcome under [ADR 0181](../../decisions/adr-0181-changed-hosted-terms-decline-and-existing-content-continuity.md); it does not authorize marketplace or purchase-value refunds and is not implemented by the current generic refund surface.

- `stripe_event_inbox`
  - Idempotently retains each verified Stripe event by provider event ID together with either a typed command correlation and processing state or a `subscription_lifecycle` correlation containing the event's `provider_subscription_id` and resolved immutable local `subscriptionId` when known. Command correlations are exactly one of `purchase` with the immutable purchase-operation ID, `refund` with the immutable refund-operation ID, `subscription_provisioning` with the immutable `subscription_provisioning_request_id`, `subscription_update` with that update's immutable operation-specific request ID, `transfer` with the immutable `transferRequestId` plus its provider-operation identity, or `customer_provisioning` with the immutable `customer_provisioning_request_id`; provider IDs are lookup and evidence fields, not command-operation identity.
  - Stripe provider metadata carries typed command correlation for every resource created by those operations. A subscription lifecycle event has no command operation: Account resolves its provider subscription ID to the canonical local subscription, records the resolved internal `subscriptionId`, and never fabricates or reuses a provisioning or update operation identity. If provider-ID linkage is not yet available, the event and correlation remain durably `pending` with retry state until reconciliation repairs the linkage. Timeouts, lost responses, duplicate deliveries, and partial outcomes remain nonterminal; retries reuse the same typed command identities where applicable and never create a duplicate resource or transition.

- `purchase_entitlement`  
  - Reserved for a future separately accepted marketplace path. It is not created by v1 hosting-plan billing or by a raw PaymentIntent.
  - If later accepted, it would carry immutable purchase/grant identity, account, an optional independent `tenantId` binding for tenant-scoped grants, an optional actor reference `{tenantId, playableStateNamespaceId, characterId}`, product code, grant lifecycle, provider evidence, and explicit value-consumption/refund state. Account-scoped grants omit the independent tenant binding, while an actor reference does not replace it. `playableStateScope` remains separately validated routing/authorization evidence rather than an actor key. No v1 hosting-billing PaymentIntent or receipt creates this record.
  - Fulfillment uniqueness is enforced only on the immutable `(purchase_operation_id, product_grant_key)` pair. `provider_event_id` is retained as Stripe audit/evidence and webhook-correlation data; it is not an entitlement identity and cannot create a second grant.
  - A future marketplace would make this table the entitlement authority for its own grants. `payment_transaction` remains financial/provider evidence and must not be treated as proof that gameplay value is active.

- `subscription` (status lifecycle and plan-change timing are canonical in [Subscription Management](./subscription-management.md))
  - Represents a recurring billing agreement between a creator (platform account) and the platform for a specific tenant’s hosting plan.  
  - Key fields: immutable internal `subscriptionId`, immutable `tenantId`, `accountId`, `plan_code`, `status` (`pending`, `provisioning`, `trialing`, `active`, `past_due`, `grace`, `suspended`, `canceled`), current period start/end, `provider_subscription_id` (Stripe `subscription` ID), `provider_customer_id` (Stripe `customer` ID), an immutable `subscription_provisioning_request_id`/subscription-operation idempotency identity, the reserved payment-instrument version, a reference to the separately immutable customer-provisioning request identity, and a non-null persisted subscription-specific binding to the selected account-owned payment instrument. `accountId` and `plan_code` remain mutable lookup/constraint fields; only the explicit billing-owner handoff may replace `accountId`, and no supported operation relocates a subscription to another tenant.
  - `pending` and `provisioning` are durable pre-activation states. `pending` means the local subscription intent and provider work item have committed but provider creation has not been claimed; `provisioning` means the provider call is in progress or awaiting recovery. Neither state grants hosting entitlements or permits billing.
  - Plan metadata defines quota-related attributes (for example, maximum active sessions, world size tiers) that the platform uses to drive per-tenant resource limits as described in [Multi-Tenancy](../../system-architecture-multi-tenancy.md#tenant-configuration--scaling).

- `subscription_operation`
  - Records each provider-affecting subscription command as either initial `subscription_provisioning` or a later `subscription_update`. Initial provisioning stores the caller-stable `requestId` and Account-computed versioned `requestDigest` for the normalized create tuple, plus the immutable `subscription_provisioning_request_id`; a retry with the same `requestId` and normalized create fields recomputes the same digest and replays the operation, while changed fields conflict before provider work. Every plan, instrument, cancellation, or other update uses a distinct immutable operation-specific request ID, request digest, expected subscription row version, provider idempotency identity, outbox item, and terminal result.
  - Provider metadata and webhook inbox correlation preserve the exact operation type and ID. Reconciliation may update the canonical subscription row only through that operation's row-version/CAS guard; it must not attribute a later update to the initial provisioning identity.

- `subscription_transfer`
  - Is the durable local handoff record for a billing-owner transfer of one immutable `subscriptionId`. It contains an immutable `transferRequestId`, `tenantId`, the exact subscription row and expected row version, immutable snapshots of the current owner/customer/instrument/provider binding and proposed replacement owner/customer/instrument binding, the paid-through `effective_at`, transfer audit fields, the eventual replacement provider-subscription ID, and distinct stable operation identities for replacement creation and predecessor end/cancellation.
  - At most one nonterminal transfer may reference a subscription, enforced by a partial unique database index and the transaction that locks the canonical subscription row before inserting the transfer. `pending` means the transfer intent and outbox work are committed while the existing subscription binding remains authoritative. `provider_transition` means replacement creation or predecessor-end reconciliation is in progress. `cutover_pending` means both provider boundary operations are confirmed and the worker is waiting for `effective_at`. `cutover_committed` means the canonical subscription row now carries the replacement binding while provider cleanup or final reconciliation may still be pending. `completed` is terminal only after both provider resources and the canonical subscription row converge. Any timeout, ambiguous provider result, or partial outcome remains nonterminal and must not create another replacement or cut over optimistically.
  - The durable transfer also stores the Account-issued single-use, expiring proof binding (`transferRequestId`, immutable `subscriptionId`, new owner, selected instrument/version) evidence and a distinct cleanup-cancellation provider idempotency identity in addition to replacement-creation and predecessor-end identities.

- `billing_customer`
  - Maps one global `accountId` to its Stripe customer ID so the account has one consistent provider identity across tenants.
  - Also records the durable account-keyed customer-provisioning intent, its own immutable `customer_provisioning_request_id`/customer-provider idempotency identity, provider customer ID when known, and reconciliation state. A timeout, lost response, or failed local commit must reconcile this same intent and provider metadata before another customer-create attempt; ambiguous state remains nonterminal rather than creating a duplicate customer. This identity is never reused as the idempotency identity for a subscription create or update.

- `payment_instrument`
  - Represents an account-owned Stripe PaymentMethod reference plus provider-approved display metadata. FireMUD never stores raw card numbers, security codes, or equivalent payment credentials.
  - A saved instrument may be selected by several subscriptions owned by the same account, but each subscription records that selection explicitly before activation or charge. Established subscriptions must not fall back to a mutable customer-wide default.

### Billing Identity and Instrument Ownership

- `actorAccountId` is the authenticated caller or operator. It identifies who requested the action and is not, by itself, the account that owns a subscription or payment instrument.
- `subscription.accountId` is the subscription account and current billing owner recorded for the tenant subscription. A delegated or cross-tenant actor may act for that account only through the explicit audited billing-owner authorization or handoff contract.
- `payment_instrument.accountId` is the instrument owner. Account may select an instrument for a subscription only when it matches the authorized billing-owner account for that operation and its Stripe customer matches that account; `tenantId`, actor identity, or subscription ownership alone must not substitute for this ownership check.

### Billing-owner Transfer State and Cutover

Billing-owner transfer is not a plan change. Because Stripe cannot move a subscription between Customers, Account creates a replacement provider subscription for the new owner while retaining the same immutable local `subscriptionId`, and coordinates both provider resources at the existing subscription’s paid-through boundary without proration or cross-customer credit movement.

1. Account authorizes and audits the handoff, locks the canonical subscription row, validates its current owner/customer/instrument/provider binding and row version, and confirms that no nonterminal transfer already references it. The new billing owner independently selects and authorizes an instrument from that owner's wallet; Account issues the high-entropy, expiring, single-use proof bound to the `transferRequestId`, immutable `subscriptionId`, new owner, and selected instrument/version. The `billingAdmin` route carries only that proof and its bounded result and has no wallet access. One transaction atomically consumes the proof, creates `subscription_transfer` with the old and proposed bindings, the replacement owner’s selected instrument, the distinct replacement-creation, predecessor-end, and cleanup-cancellation identities, and the outbox work item. The canonical subscription row remains unchanged and is the only local entitlement and billing authority.
2. The transfer worker creates or recovers the replacement provider subscription using the transfer’s stable replacement-creation identity and the new owner’s confirmed customer and instrument. It separately schedules or reconciles the current provider subscription to end at `effective_at`, using the distinct predecessor-end identity. The replacement must not collect before `effective_at`. Provider metadata carries the `transferRequestId` and immutable `subscriptionId` so a lost response can be reconciled; retries reuse the operation-specific identities and never issue an unkeyed create or cancellation.
3. `effective_at` is the canonical subscription row’s immutable paid-through boundary in UTC. The replacement billing start and current-provider billing end must be confirmed at that exact boundary with no proration. If either provider boundary cannot be confirmed, the transfer remains `pending` or `provider_transition`, the existing local binding stays authoritative, and reconciliation blocks cutover. Transfer processing must not inherit a generic plan-change proration setting or approximate the boundary.
4. A transfer may enter `cutover_pending` only after Account has confirmed the replacement provider ID, its new-owner customer and instrument binding, its no-collection-before-boundary configuration, and the current provider subscription’s end-at-boundary configuration. At or after `effective_at`, one database transaction locks the transfer and canonical subscription row, rechecks the expected row version and complete old binding, then atomically replaces `accountId`, provider customer, payment instrument, provider subscription, period boundary, and row version with the proposed binding and records the cutover outcome. It emits the normal Account-owned billing events through the same transaction/outbox boundary.
5. Before that guarded update commits, only the old binding on the canonical row authorizes entitlement or billing. After it commits, only the replacement binding on that same row authorizes entitlement or billing, even if a delayed webhook still reports the old provider subscription as nonterminal. A failed guard leaves the row unchanged and the transfer nonterminal; elapsed time, provider state, or a webhook alone can never switch local authority.
6. `cutover_committed` remains unresolved until predecessor termination, replacement activation, webhook correlation, and all related outbox/provider work reconcile durably. If a replacement must be abandoned before it becomes authoritative, cleanup cancels it with the transfer's distinct cleanup-cancellation identity; an ambiguous cleanup result remains nonterminal and retries that same identity. Reconciliation updates the one canonical subscription row and its transfer by immutable `subscriptionId`, `transferRequestId`, and stable provider-operation identities. Only then may the transfer become `completed`.

## Payment Flows

Supported v1 payment flows are Account-owned hosting billing. They follow the same high-level pattern: commit the durable operation/intent, stable provider idempotency identity, and webhook-correlation state before any external Stripe call; create or reuse a Stripe customer; persist or confirm the applicable internal authorization and billing state; create the provider resource; and rely on Stripe webhooks to finalize state transitions. Customer provisioning and subscription provisioning are separate durable operations/work items. Each has its own stable idempotency identity, retry/reconciliation state, and recovery boundary; a subscription operation may depend on a confirmed customer identity but must not conflate customer creation with subscription creation. For subscriptions, a persisted subscription-specific instrument binding is a prerequisite for activation and every charge; provider defaults are not a substitute. The one-time purchase and donation prose below is deferred future marketplace substrate, not a supported v1 route or player entitlement authority.

Hosting charges, saved-instrument changes, hosting subscriptions, hosting refunds, and billing-owner transfers complete through the HTTPS account/control plane and provider-hosted flows. Creator/player purchases, donations, payouts, settlement, and revenue-sharing flows remain deferred. A Telnet or other gameplay client may explicitly initiate an eligible hosting action and receive a short-lived, single-use opaque HTTPS handoff bound server-side to the authenticated account, tenant, action, immutable plan and amount/currency where applicable, request ID, and the originating effective security-policy version/digest resolved by Account. Before handoff disclosure, step-up, provider-backed completion, or atomic handoff consumption, Account re-resolves the exact current policy and requires exact equality of both values; advancing the policy invalidates the unconsumed handoff. The URL carries no payment credential and cannot change its bound hosting action. Gameplay receives completion only after Account verifies the provider result and current-policy equality, applies the idempotent billing workflow, and publishes the outcome.

### Hosting handoff and provider-result linearization

A hosting action started by a gameplay-to-HTTPS handoff has one durable Account request or operation committed before any Stripe mutation. It preserves the exact handoff and `requestId` identity, captured security and effective-policy tuple, immutable hosting and commercial fields, and the operation's Stripe idempotency identity. Account's authorization linearization point is the one transaction that revalidates that tuple, atomically consumes the unconsumed handoff, and commits the local hosting outcome, including any authoritative active subscription or hosting-entitlement state and Account-owned outbox and audit evidence. If that transaction commits before a policy or security authority advance, the outcome remains valid under the captured tuple and later changes affect only later operations. If the authority advances first, stale handoff is never consumed and Account must not commit a hosting entitlement or authoritative active billing outcome under it. If Stripe may already have succeeded, the same durable request or operation records and preserves the provider and webhook evidence, and reconciliation reuses the same durable operation and its Stripe idempotency identity and provider metadata; no replacement create or charge is issued. A separately keyed, action-specific, predeclared typed cleanup, cancellation, or refund operation may run only after reversal is proven safe. Only after that operation durably proves reversal or provider absence may Account commit that operation's defined non-authorizing terminal outcome; otherwise it remains nonterminal for bounded reconciliation then audited authority remediation and blocks terminal billing or account-deletion completion as applicable.

### One-Time Purchases and Donations (Deferred Future Marketplace Substrate)

The following mechanics are illustrative future substrate only. They are not a supported v1 route, do not authorize player gameplay value, and cannot be enabled until a separately accepted marketplace, settlement, and revenue-sharing decision defines the product and entitlement contract.

The current generic PaymentIntent/refund/donation-shaped RPC substrate is unsupported/deferred and is not proven exact-method-authorized. It must reject before any Stripe mutation; only separately typed hosting-billing operations may call Stripe. Until the generic methods are removed or converged, any internal-only call must authenticate an exact workload identity with mTLS and an exact method allowlist; a bearer token alone is insufficient. Account must derive the authoritative account and billing owner plus product, price, currency, and donation bounds from trusted server context/catalog rather than caller fields, reject missing or contradictory context, and fail closed. These surfaces cannot create FireMUD entitlements, and this containment does not make creator or player purchases/donations a supported v1 route.

If a future marketplace is separately accepted, it must define disjoint typed operations rather than redefine the current generic RPCs:

- A fixed product purchase supplies an immutable `product_code`; it does not accept caller-authoritative `amount_cents` or currency. Account resolves the active product, price, currency, and catalog revision from the server-side Stripe product catalog. The resolved values are immutable inputs to the purchase digest and durable `purchase_operation`, and the same values are used for the Stripe `PaymentIntent`, checkout binding, webhook correlation, and reconciliation.
- A variable donation uses a separate donation amount path. The caller may supply an amount only within the server-configured minimum, maximum, precision, and currency bounds; Account rejects fixed-product fields on that path and records the accepted bounded amount and server-authoritative currency in the digest and durable operation. A donation amount must never be interpreted as a fixed product price.

1. A future typed purchase or donation operation calls the Account Service with the appropriate fixed-product or bounded-donation variant, authenticated context, optional `tenantId`, and caller request ID. Under the target containment contract, the generic `CreatePaymentIntent`, `CreateDonation`, and `RefundPayment` methods reject before any Stripe mutation; their current provider-mutating implementation remains the drift recorded above. For an accepted future typed operation, Account derives the authoritative account from authenticated context and, before any external Stripe customer or `PaymentIntent` call, commits one durable `purchase_operation` and its webhook-correlation identity bound to the operation type, caller request ID, immutable purchase digest, resolved commercial values, customer-operation reference, and separate stable PaymentIntent idempotency identity. The webhook must resolve through this durable operation identity rather than a provider ID that may not yet have been persisted.
2. The Account Service provisions or reuses the Stripe customer through the same durable intent contract as `billing_customer`: persist the account-keyed provisioning intent and stable customer-provisioning idempotency identity, reconcile provider metadata before retrying after a timeout, lost response, or failed local commit, and leave ambiguous state nonterminal. A generic lookup-or-create flow after an ambiguous attempt is prohibited. Once the customer is confirmed, Account calls Stripe to create the `PaymentIntent` using the purchase operation's distinct stable payment-intent idempotency identity and non-secret provider metadata carrying the durable purchase-operation correlation identity.
3. A `payment_transaction` row is created in `pending` status and linked to the purchase operation, with the returned `payment_intent` ID recorded as `provider_id`. If the provider call may have succeeded but the response or local linkage commit is lost, the operation remains nonterminal and reconciliation uses the same idempotency and provider-metadata identities; it never creates a new logical purchase attempt.
4. The client completes payment using Stripe’s client-side flow (for example, via Stripe.js); Stripe later calls a configured webhook when the intent succeeds or fails.  
5. The webhook handler in the Account Service:
   - Verifies the webhook signature and idempotently records the event in `stripe_event_inbox` by Stripe event ID before applying business state.
   - Resolves the durable purchase operation from its provider metadata correlation identity and then locates or repairs the linked `payment_transaction` by operation identity and `provider_id`. An event that arrives before local provider-ID/linkage persistence remains pending in the inbox and is reconciled after linkage becomes available.
   - Sets `status` to `succeeded` or `failed` and records Stripe failure codes where applicable.  
   - For product purchases that grant ongoing value, idempotently creates the corresponding `purchase_entitlement` row or transitions an existing row only from `pending` to `active`, using the immutable purchase-operation ID and product grant key as the sole fulfillment uniqueness inputs. A duplicate delivery for an already-active row replays the result; a `refunded` payment transaction and `revoked`, `consumed_nonrevocable`, or `paid_unfulfilled_compensated` entitlement are terminal and cannot be reactivated. If the charge is confirmed but this fulfillment cannot commit, Account records `PAID_UNFULFILLED`, leaves the grant non-active, and never treats the paid transaction as gameplay authority. The Stripe event ID is retained only as audit/evidence and webhook-processing evidence.
   - Emits domain events or saga steps so other services (for example, Logging & Admin, in-game unlocks) can react.
   - Marks the purchase operation and inbox event terminal only after provider linkage, transaction state, and any required entitlement or failure outcome have converged durably.

`PAID_UNFULFILLED` is a post-charge, non-authorizing outcome, not a successful fulfillment or a failed payment. Fulfillment reconciliation reuses the original purchase operation and never creates another charge. If the bounded fulfillment deadline or retry budget is exhausted, Account starts one idempotent full-refund compensation through `refund_operation` with reason `FULFILLMENT_COMPENSATION`, expected transaction/entitlement versions, and a stable provider idempotency identity. A successful refund records the financial compensation and commits terminal `PAID_UNFULFILLED_COMPENSATED` on the purchase operation and grant, if a grant row exists; that state cannot become `active`, be reactivated by a duplicate webhook, or authorize gameplay. An ambiguous or failed compensation remains `COMPENSATION_PENDING` and blocks deletion or terminal operation completion until reconciled.

Before calling Stripe’s `Refund` API, Account commits one `refund_operation` and outbox item with a stable provider idempotency identity and expected transaction/entitlement versions. The worker claims that operation and rejects any request whose amount is not the transaction's full remaining refundable amount; partial refunds remain unsupported until the domain model has explicit partially-refunded financial state and an entitlement-value policy. It then calls or reconciles Stripe using the same identity and records the provider refund ID before terminalizing the operation. A webhook is first retained in `stripe_event_inbox` and correlated by the typed refund-operation identity; an early event or lost response remains pending until linkage is repaired. One guarded local transition updates the `payment_transaction` to `refunded` and revokes any resulting `purchase_entitlement`, unless it has already been consumed under a product contract that is explicitly non-revocable. Non-revocable consumption must be recorded as `consumed_nonrevocable` with a reason so support, audit, and revenue-sharing reports can explain why financial refund and product state diverged. Retries return or resume the same durable outcome and cannot issue a second refund.

### Subscriptions and Hosting Plans

Subscription creation, lifecycle, and entitlements are covered in more detail in the [Subscription Management Design](./subscription-management.md). At a high level:

1. A creator chooses a hosting plan for a tenant in the admin UI; the caller-bound tenant variant of `CreateSubscription` derives actor identity from auth context and requires `tenantId`, `plan_code`, and either an explicit saved `paymentInstrumentId` or deterministic instrument-selection input that resolves to exactly one saved instrument. Choosing or paying for a hosting plan does not establish the tenant's Creator Party or accept hosted terms. Cross-tenant billing/admin workflows use separate admin variants when acting on another account or tenant. Before any provider call, the Account transaction enforces the database/concurrency boundary described above, so exactly one non-superseded local subscription can win for the tenant.
2. For a new subscription, before any Stripe customer or subscription provisioning, the Account Service resolves the authoritative billing owner for the tenant and captures the exact owner authority version/generation from that snapshot. It verifies that the actor is authorized to act for that owner or has an explicit billing-owner handoff. It allocates the immutable internal `subscriptionId`, then in the same local intent/outbox transaction locks or CAS-validates the resolved owner and the captured version/generation before committing a durable subscription operation/correlation record and outbox work item bound to the operation, authenticated caller, account owner, tenant, and caller request identity. An owner change or fence mismatch fails closed before any provider work. For an existing subscription update, it reads the current owner from the existing row. A cross-tenant `billingAdmin` or `platformAdmin` action must use the dedicated audited billing route. Account then links the subscription operation to that owner's separate durable account-keyed customer-provisioning operation, which must complete or reconcile to a confirmed Stripe customer identity before the subscription work item can transition to provider creation. The customer operation has its own immutable `customer_provisioning_request_id`; the subscription operation has a different immutable `subscription_provisioning_request_id`. Every retry recovers the existing operation of the relevant type and reuses only that operation's provider-resource identity. Customer recovery must not create a subscription, and subscription recovery must not create a second customer. The requested or deterministically selected `payment_instrument` must be owned by that same account and belong to that customer.
3. Account atomically persists the subscription’s immutable internal `subscriptionId`, owner, non-null subscription-specific instrument binding and reserved instrument version, durable `pending` status, the separate customer-operation reference, the immutable `subscription_provisioning_request_id`, and the outbox work item in one database transaction. The provider customer reference may remain null only while the subscription stays `pending`; a confirmed non-null customer reference is mandatory before the work item becomes eligible for `provisioning` or any provider-subscription call. The subscription-operation idempotency identity is reused for every retry or recovery of that subscription operation; it is never reused for customer provisioning or a later update to an existing subscription. Customer retries and reconciliation reuse only `customer_provisioning_request_id` and its provider customer identity. Every existing-subscription plan, instrument, cancellation, or other provider update has its own immutable operation-specific request ID and expected subscription row version/CAS; Account rejects a stale version, and retries reuse that same update identity and guard. The provider call must use the persisted customer, payment-method, and operation-specific idempotency identifiers; Stripe customer defaults and other provider defaults are prohibited. A missing, stale, or mismatched binding fails closed and cannot be repaired by a provider default. A permanent provider or local failure, intent expiry, or explicit abandonment enters one idempotent terminal cleanup keyed by a distinct immutable cleanup/cancellation operation identity. The creation/provisioning identity `subscription_provisioning_request_id` is retained only for lookup and attribution and is never reused as the cleanup provider-mutation idempotency key. Before local cancellation or reservation release, cleanup uses that distinct identity to reconcile and cancel or end any provider subscription object attributable to the operation; an ambiguous provider result keeps cleanup pending for retry. Only after provider absence or terminal cancellation is durably recorded does cleanup lock the subscription and instrument, validate the exact owner, binding, reserved instrument version, reservation identity, and row version, mark the local subscription `canceled` with the durable terminal outcome, advance the instrument version exactly once, and release the reservation in the same transaction and outbox boundary. No terminal response or detach that relies on the released instrument is returned before that cleanup commits.
4. A durable worker claims the work item only after an atomic transaction revalidates the persisted instrument owner, exact reserved instrument version, matching Stripe customer, and attachable state, then moves the row to `provisioning`. A pending or provisioning reservation blocks detach; if detach commits before the reservation or claim, its version advance makes the stale reservation or claim fail before any Stripe call, while a claim that owns the reservation makes detach wait for terminal cleanup. Cleanup retries return the committed terminal result, and a stale cleanup cannot clear a different reservation after a competing version advance. The worker then creates or recovers the provider subscription using the same `subscription_provisioning_request_id`. A crash, timeout, or lost response is recovered by retrying that same operation identity and reconciling Stripe before any new subscription create is attempted. Customer reconciliation remains a separate operation using only its customer identity. If Stripe created the subscription but the local provider ID was not recorded, reconciliation locates the resource by the subscription operation's idempotency identity/provider metadata and repairs `provider_subscription_id` without creating a second subscription.
5. Account records the confirmed Stripe `subscription` ID before moving the local subscription into `trialing` or `active`; activation and invoice charging are forbidden while the row is `pending`, `provisioning`, or missing a confirmed provider ID. Stripe webhook and reconciliation processing rechecks the persisted binding before accepting activation or any invoice charge, including renewals. Changed-term renewal protection and any period-end exit/refund are target-only under [ADR 0181](../../decisions/adr-0181-changed-hosted-terms-decline-and-existing-content-continuity.md); they must not be inferred from a payment, decline, or current subscription state.
6. Stripe webhooks caused by initial provisioning or an explicit existing-subscription update resolve through the exact typed `subscription_provisioning` or `subscription_update` operation identity carried in provider metadata. Provider-lifecycle events such as subscription status or invoice events that do not originate from a current command use a separate `subscription_lifecycle` correlation: Account verifies the signature, deduplicates by Stripe `event.id` in `stripe_event_inbox`, resolves the provider subscription ID from the event payload to the unique canonical subscription and its immutable internal `subscriptionId`, and does not fabricate or reuse a command operation identity. If the provider ID is not yet linked or the local row cannot be resolved, the inbox correlation remains durably `pending` with retry state; reconciliation looks up current provider state, repairs the provider-ID/local-row linkage, and then applies a guarded transition. Replayed events are no-ops after durable deduplication, while out-of-order or ambiguous lifecycle events remain pending for reconciliation rather than regressing local state. Both command and lifecycle paths keep the internal `subscription` table in sync.
7. Changes to `subscription.status` are propagated to tenant-management and quota-enforcement components so that tenant availability and resource limits reflect the current billing state. For hard cutoff transitions such as `suspended` or `canceled`, Account transactionally advances the durable tenant authority generation with the committed billing state and emits `SubscriptionStatusChanged` plus `TenantBillingStateChanged`. Downstream services consume those events to invalidate projections and terminate affected gameplay sessions; they never advance Account authority themselves. See [Subscription Management](./subscription-management.md#tenant-availability-and-quota-enforcement) and [Session Behavior](../../system-architecture-session-behavior.md#session-and-identity-management).

## Multi-Tenancy and Security

Stripe integration must preserve tenant isolation while allowing platform-level reporting:

- Each hosted game (`tenantId`) that requires billing has exactly one non-superseded authoritative subscription row, enforced by the database uniqueness constraint and tenant-scoped creation fence above. Its immutable `subscriptionId` survives billing-owner transfer; only the guarded transfer workflow may replace its mutable owner/customer/instrument/provider binding. Superseded rows, if retained for history, are never selected for entitlement or billing authority.
- Account deletion is blocked while the account owns an authoritative subscription in a nonterminal state (`pending`, `provisioning`, `trialing`, `active`, `past_due`, `grace`, or `suspended`). It is also blocked while the account appears as the old or proposed owner in a nonterminal `subscription_transfer` (`pending`, `provider_transition`, `cutover_pending`, or `cutover_committed`), or while related customer, subscription, transfer, cancellation/end, webhook, outbox, or provider-reconciliation work remains unresolved. The creator must first cancel or drain committed provider/outbox work, reconcile ambiguous provider outcomes, and then cancel terminally or complete billing-owner transfer for every affected tenant; the platform must not delete the account and leave Stripe resources, payment instruments, or tenant billing ownership orphaned.
- A transfer that has reached its effective boundary is still a deletion blocker until its provider history, predecessor end or replacement cleanup, webhook correlation, and outbox work are terminally reconciled. Deletion may proceed only after the transfer is `completed`, or it is terminally canceled with provider absence or old-subscription cancellation, and every other billing blocker has cleared; `BILLING_RECONCILIATION_TERMINAL_FAILURE` remains unresolved work.
- Stripe customer IDs and saved instruments are per-account, not per-tenant, to reduce duplication. Every tenant subscription nevertheless persists one selected instrument binding before activation or charge, so changing tenant A’s subscription does not change what tenant B will be charged.
- Instrument listing, attachment, and detachment are account-scoped operations available to the authenticated billing-owner subject. A `tenantAdmin` role alone does not reveal or mutate another account’s instruments. Global `billingAdmin` or `platformAdmin` intervention is a bounded recovery or administrative action, not ordinary owner-wallet access: it uses an explicit cross-tenant billing route requiring recent reauthentication with `privileged_control` assurance and a durable audit record containing actor, target account, affected subscriptions, reason, scope, and outcome. It grants no standing wallet access and does not replace the billing-owner restriction for ordinary operations.
- Detaching an instrument is rejected while any subscription references it unless the same idempotent workflow supplies and successfully installs a replacement for every affected subscription. The owner sees the safely displayable affected tenant/subscription set; it is not exposed to unrelated tenant operators.
- Billing-owner transfer is an explicit, audited handoff through the dedicated `cross_tenant_billing_safe` route, recording the actor, current owner, new owner, immutable `subscriptionId`, reason, paid-through effective boundary, and outcome. The durable `subscription_transfer` snapshots the old and proposed bindings, while the canonical subscription row retains the old binding until guarded cutover. Because Stripe cannot reassign an existing subscription to another Customer, Account coordinates a replacement provider subscription scheduled to begin billing at the old paid-through boundary and the old provider subscription scheduled to end there. Exactly one binding on the canonical local row may authorize hosting entitlement or billing at any committed state. Ambiguous or partial outcomes remain nonterminal and reconcile by the transfer’s stable replacement-creation and predecessor-end identities. Immediate mid-period transfer, cross-customer credit movement, and proration are unsupported; saved instruments never transfer between accounts.
- `CreateSubscription`, activation, and every subscription charge must supply the persisted customer and payment-method identifiers; Stripe customer defaults and other provider defaults are prohibited. Provider webhook processing and reconciliation verify the persisted per-subscription binding for activation and every charge, including renewals; a missing or mismatched binding fails closed.
- If a future product requirement needs tenant-isolated payment instruments, the platform must move to tenant-scoped billing customers rather than treating the current account-owned wallet as implicitly tenant-safe.
- Internal current-state queries select the one authoritative subscription row for the requested `tenantId`, then apply the required `accountId`/`tenantId` authorization filters. They use only that row’s current binding and never infer authority from a pending transfer or replacement provider resource. Transfer history, reconciliation, and audit queries may expose the old and proposed binding snapshots only through explicit variants that label them non-authoritative. Cross-tenant reports are restricted to roles with appropriate `globalRoles` as defined in the shared role model:
  - `platformAdmin` for full cross-tenant reporting, and
  - `billingAdmin` for billing-focused reporting surfaces.
  Cross-tenant support troubleshooting is exposed only through explicitly support-safe variants (`cross_tenant_support_safe`) with high-level redacted fields. These rules are enforced by the Tenant Authorization Contract.  
- Stripe API keys, webhook secrets, and any PCI-relevant configuration remain confined to the Account Service. Other services never communicate with Stripe directly.

## Service APIs

The Account Service exposes gRPC and REST endpoints for initiating and inspecting billing flows:

- `CreatePaymentIntent` – Existing generic payment substrate; unsupported/deferred and gated. Target containment requires rejection before any Stripe mutation; the current implementation remains provider-mutating drift and is not a supported v1 entitlement path. Only a separately typed hosting-billing operation may call Stripe.
- `CreateDonation` – Existing generic donation substrate; unsupported/deferred and gated. Target containment requires rejection before any Stripe mutation; the current implementation remains provider-mutating drift and is not a supported v1 product path.
- `RefundPayment` – Existing generic refund substrate; unsupported/deferred and gated. Target containment requires rejection before any Stripe mutation; the current implementation remains provider-mutating drift. Hosting refunds use a separately typed hosting-billing operation and durable refund workflow; future marketplace refunds require the separately accepted value-conservation contract.
- `CreateSubscription` – Create one recurring hosting subscription for an immutable `tenantId` and initial `plan_code` with caller-stable `requestId` and Account-computed versioned `requestDigest`; a retry with the same `requestId` and normalized create fields recomputes the same digest and replays the durable operation, while changed fields return `IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT`. Its provider `subscription_provisioning_request_id` is a separate identity, and creation rejects update-only fields. The current proto/implementation do not yet expose the caller retry pair.
- `UpdateSubscription` – Apply a plan, instrument, cancellation, or other supported mutation to an existing immutable `subscriptionId` using a distinct operation identity and expected row version; it rejects creation-only fields and any `tenantId` change.
- `TransferBillingOwner` – Start or recover the audited provider-replacement handoff through the `subscription_transfer` state machine and guarded update of the canonical subscription row.
- Subscription and transaction query APIs – Must be split into explicit tenant-scoped billing-safe, cross-tenant support-safe, and cross-tenant billing-safe variants (no mixed-mode endpoint behavior). Per-tenant caller-bound billing history is visible to `tenantAdmin`; global `platformAdmin`/`billingAdmin` access uses cross-tenant billing-safe variants only.

Edge REST endpoints use JWT-based authentication, and tenant-scoped REST operations must validate that the caller is allowed to act on the specified `tenantId` using the Tenant Authorization Contract from [Authentication & Authorization](../../system-architecture-authentication.md#tenant-authorization-contract). The edge JWT authenticates the external control-plane subject only; it is not the identity for an internal billing call and is not a substitute for workload authentication. Internal payment and subscription gRPC methods use the authenticated caller's mTLS workload identity plus explicit method-level authorization; mTLS authenticates the service caller but does not grant blanket permission. Each internal method must authorize its declared caller, tenant, account, and operation scope, and an end-user JWT is not a substitute for the internal caller contract.

## Operational Concerns

Operational behavior around Stripe integration focuses on observability, idempotency, and resilience:

- Webhook handlers are idempotent and keyed by Stripe event IDs; repeated deliveries do not change internal state after the first successful application. Subscription lifecycle events resolve provider IDs through the immutable current/history mapping, map predecessor IDs to the canonical subscription without overwriting its binding, and terminally ignore later events after predecessor termination. An unknown or conflicting ID remains durably pending or quarantined for explicit reconciliation and is never assigned heuristically.
- Subscription provisioning is an outbox-backed, restart-safe workflow. Reconciliation must inspect `pending`/`provisioning` rows, reuse the subscription operation's stable provider idempotency identity for that provisioning operation, repair missing provider IDs, and surface ambiguous provider state for operator resolution rather than issuing an unkeyed create. Terminal cleanup uses its distinct immutable cleanup/cancellation identity; the provisioning identity remains lookup/attribution evidence and is never reused for the cleanup provider mutation. Customer provisioning is reconciled separately with its own stable identity; neither operation may reuse the other's provider-resource identity.
- Billing-owner transfer is an outbox-backed, restart-safe workflow. Reconciliation inspects every nonterminal `subscription_transfer`, reuses the stable replacement-create, predecessor-end, and distinct cleanup-cancellation identities, repairs missing provider IDs or binding evidence only through immutable transfer/history mappings, and verifies both provider boundaries against `effective_at`. A webhook or provider lookup may update transfer evidence but cannot replace the canonical subscription row's binding outside the guarded row-version cutover transaction. Every billing/provider reconciliation operation has a bounded retry budget, backoff, and absolute reconciliation deadline. Repeated attempts with no new provider or local evidence are classified as stalled; exhausted retries, a deadline, or a stalled threshold records terminal `BILLING_RECONCILIATION_TERMINAL_FAILURE` with the affected tenant, subscription/provider identities, last evidence, attempts, and reason, stops automatic retries, and creates an explicit operator-resolution obligation. Operator resolution must use an audited typed operation to prove provider absence, terminal provider cancellation, or completed billing-owner transfer, then commit the corresponding Account row/outbox state under its CAS before the blocker can clear. The transfer remains nonterminal until it converges or reaches that terminal failure, and terminal failure remains unresolved billing work: account deletion stays blocked until the operator resolution succeeds.
- Metrics track payment and subscription statuses (for example, counts of `payment_transaction` by `status`, and subscriptions in `past_due` or `grace` states).  
- Alerts fire when webhook processing fails repeatedly, when Stripe API calls start failing at elevated rates, or when the number of tenants in `grace` or `suspended` billing states exceeds thresholds.  
- During Stripe outages, new hosting-billing operations and subscription changes fail closed, but existing tenants remain in their last-known-good state until internal policies (for example, maximum grace period) dictate otherwise. Deferred player purchases and creator donations are not admitted. Webhook reconciliation may repair Account's committed billing state and outbox, but Account remains the sole producer of `SubscriptionStatusChanged` and `TenantBillingStateChanged`; downstream consumers process those Account-owned events. Failures in either stage remain independently observable so potential entitlement-enforcement drift can be correlated without introducing a second event source.
- A terminal billing reconciliation failure is not a successful cancellation, provider absence, or transfer. It remains a durable deletion and billing-authority blocker until an explicitly authorized operator-resolution workflow records one of those safe outcomes and Account commits the matching canonical state and outbox evidence.
