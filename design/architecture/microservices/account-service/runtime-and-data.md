# Account Service Runtime and Data

This document defines the Account Service runtime model, persistent data ownership, Redis role, token/session responsibilities, and monetization-related domain notes.

## Normative Target Contract

Account owns platform identity, hosted Creator Party/operator and terms currentness authority, durable membership, entitlement authority, Account-local authentication persistence, global `account_security_lock`/`platform_access_ban` enforcement, Stripe v1 hosting-billing state, and future marketplace financial reconciliation. The hosted terms and creator-party authority are target-only under [ADR 0180](../../decisions/adr-0180-account-owned-hosted-terms-acceptance-gate.md) and [ADR 0181](../../decisions/adr-0181-changed-hosted-terms-decline-and-existing-content-continuity.md); future creator commerce remains separately gated by [ADR 0179](../../decisions/adr-0179-firemud-managed-creator-commerce-boundary.md). This document records only local runtime and data consequences; fixed-category moderation and appeal ownership is defined by [Moderation Policies](../logging-admin-service/moderation-policies.md), gameplay/chat enforcement remains in Game Session and Social & Groups, gameplay admission and JOIN identity binding are defined by [Authentication](../../system-architecture-authentication.md), and token refresh, replacement, generation, revocation, fencing, and logout are defined by [JWT and Token Contracts](../../system-architecture-jwt-and-token-contracts.md).

## Implementation Status

The account lifecycle state machine, global deletion preconditions, account-scoped versus tenant-scoped portable-data export split, separate safety-restriction lifecycles, hosting-billing authority, and future-marketplace `purchase_entitlement` substrate are the canonical target design. The current service has live identity/authentication, profile, external-link persistence, recovery/verification, payment, subscription, notification, and virtual-currency foundations, but lifecycle state transitions, genuine provider authentication, cross-service export, retained-record handling, complete account lock/platform-ban enforcement, purchased-entitlement fulfillment, and complete billing/subscription follow-through remain partial. The current account export returns only Account and profile rows, the tenant export includes excessive global Account fields, and successful deletion immediately removes Account-owned rows including payment transactions. Those behaviors directly conflict with [ADR 0050](../../decisions/adr-0050-versioned-export-retention-and-erasure-policy.md) and the bounded erasure workflow below.

Hosted Creator Party/operator identity, terms catalog, acceptance/currentness evidence, changed-term status, and future financial-reconciliation boundaries are target-only. No current party/signer or terms storage, acceptance/transfer route, creator UI, or Game Design evidence binding is implemented or proved; the local retention/export treatment must follow [ADR 0050](../../decisions/adr-0050-versioned-export-retention-and-erasure-policy.md) when the owner contract is implemented. Organization support, marketplace enrollment, and creator-player entitlement issuance remain disabled rather than implied by this data model.

The live runtime membership response currently carries only `accountId`, `tenantId`, `membershipExists`, `gameplayAdmissionAllowed`, `membershipVersion`, and `evaluatedAt`. The target `membershipLifecycleState`, `roles`, `membershipAuthorityGeneration`, complete `authorityTuple`, and `outboxCheckpoints` evidence remain absent from both the proto and producer and must be introduced only with one authoritative snapshot implementation and fail-closed consumer proof; no partial response shape claims lifecycle state today. Account-first bootstrap now authenticates before tenant selection and accepts configured password or verified-email OTP modes. `GetTenantMembershipForRuntime(accountId, tenantId, requestId)`, `GetTenantEntitlementsForRuntime(tenantId, requestId)`, and `GetAdmissionPointer(tenantId, worldSlug, realmSlug)` are present. Current connect-token issuance and text `PLAY` consume `membershipExists`, `gameplayAdmissionAllowed`, `membershipVersion`, and `evaluatedAt`, and admit only when the two admission fields are true; an eligible public-production request with `membershipExists=false` may return `JOIN_REQUIRED`, while an existing non-admitting response cannot be classified as `INACTIVE`. They do not create or restore membership. The obsolete implicit membership-writer surface has been removed, and explicit `JOIN`/`Join & Play` remains target-only and unimplemented. `GetAdmissionPointer` is a live `game_session.v1.GameSessionService` RPC served by the Game Session runtime owner and consumed by Account through `GameSessionClient`; its current response shape and the target routing semantics owned by Multi-Tenancy are described below. Broader reconnect/cutover consumption of admission-pointer truth and operator-facing pointer tooling remain incomplete. The entitlement producer also remains implementation drift until nullable `provisioningStatus`, opaque `tenantAuthorityGeneration`, caller/verified-target binding, source-event evidence, event delivery, and focused proof are implemented together. `CommitTenantCapacityAdmission` now has one synchronized target request/replay contract, including `mutationDigest`, but its proto RPC, callers, durable Account-owned authority/usage persistence, and reservation lifecycle remain unimplemented.

The direct-text `REALMS`-to-`JOIN` scope handoff is also target-only and unimplemented. Account-local remaining work is to validate the authenticated `PlayerExecutionContext` separately from the short-lived target-bound scope, issue that scope during authenticated realm resolution, and validate its exact routing snapshot at `JoinPublicProductionMembership`; Game Session transport retention and server-generated join-attempt identity are defined by [Authentication](../../system-architecture-authentication.md#direct-text-realms-to-join-scope-normative).

The target capacity retry contract uses stable outer `(tenantId, requestId, admissionId)`, fresh per-attempt `admissionAttemptId`, current `capacityRearmFence`, exact complete entitlement evidence, bounded `capacityDelta`, and a versioned `mutationDigest` over that attempt identity, fence, and complete payload. Exact retries replay the stored result. A new attempt requires an Account-owned atomic CAS over the same outer tuple, terminal `OWNER_EXECUTION/NOT_EXECUTED_AFTER_AUTHORIZATION` plus durable no-commit evidence, current fence, and prior digest; the CAS advances the fence without creating a second reservation. Changed fields without a matching rearm return `IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT`; stale or losing fences return `FENCE_REJECTED` only with durable no-accept/no-runtime-mutation proof. This remains target-only and follows the canonical [capacity-attempt proof obligations](../../system-architecture-versioning-runtime.md#focused-capacity-attempt-proof-obligations).

## Architecture and Runtime Notes

- Stateless authentication uses exact short-lived JWT profiles for meta/control and gameplay bootstrap:
  - `control-ui` JWTs for first-party admin/creator web UIs via `/auth/login`, with audience `control-ui` and short lifetime.
  - `player-bootstrap` JWTs for first-party gameplay bootstrap, with audience `player-bootstrap` and only the bounded claims needed for bootstrap routes.
  - Receiver-specific private player-delegation JWTs for backend services via internal authentication flows (for example, `Authenticate`), currently `game-session-account-delegation` with audience `account-service`.
  Raw Telnet and other non-WebSocket text streams never see or transmit these JWT profiles. First-party browser/mobile gameplay clients use only the bounded `player-bootstrap` profile for HTTPS bootstrap; the one-use WebSocket connect token is a separate edge credential. Generic `Service JWT` and `aud=internal` profiles are forbidden. Workload-only calls use mTLS and exact method allowlists without a bearer token. Gameplay protocol clients (Telnet and WebSocket) never see or transmit private delegation tokens.
- The live service hashes and verifies raw passwords with `argon2-jvm` Argon2 using `iterations=2`, `memory=65536 KiB`, and `parallelism=1`, with unique salts before PostgreSQL storage.
- **Target state:** issued-token registry records are stored in Redis as described in [JWT and Token Contracts](../../system-architecture-jwt-and-token-contracts.md); gameplay session bindings are owned by the Game Session Service and are not managed directly here. **Current runtime:** authentication still uses the legacy Account session key rather than the ADR 0035 registry record.
- Creation events are logged to the Logging & Admin Service via a saga step.
- Safety-restriction and recovery events may be sent to Logging & Admin for auditability, but Account remains the enforcement owner for `account_security_lock` and `platform_access_ban`; Logging & Admin does not own or mutate Account restriction state.
- Account may consume bounded relationship/projection data for Entity-owned actor discovery across games; Entity Management owns persisted actor rows, actor identity, playable-state namespace, creation/provisioning, and gameplay state. Account is not the actor writer or ownership authority. See [ADR 0140](../../decisions/adr-0140-realm-authored-controllable-actor-entry.md).
- The core `account` record represents a global platform account identified by `accountId`. Tables that represent per-game state or billing attach to tenants via a `tenantId` column so the same platform account can join multiple games without data leakage. Every tenant-scoped query enforces this filter as described in [Multi-Tenancy](../../system-architecture-multi-tenancy.md).
- Provides a JWKS endpoint for other services to validate tokens. Account Service owns signing-key lifecycle authorization, token issuance/rotation, desired key-ring state, and JWKS publication. In target non-exportable-signer mode, Account delegates only explicitly authorized private-key operations to the approved signer boundary; signer custody does not transfer lifecycle or publication authority. In the interim Account-only mounted fallback, the materialization controller is the sole Kubernetes API reader, writer, and generator for the fixed `jwt-signing-keys` Secret and custodian of that interim private material, but it may act only on Account-authorized operations. Account consumes projected key files and has no signing-Secret API authority. Kubernetes Jobs or CronJobs only observe Account-owned rotation, convergence, and retained evidence; they do not request or own rotation, read or write private keys, or publish JWKS, as described in the [Security Architecture](../../system-architecture-security.md).
- All service-to-service communication is protected by mutual TLS.
- Telnet and other non-WebSocket text transports authenticate through the credential-bearing `LOGIN` flow described in [Authentication & Authorization](../../system-architecture-authentication.md) and do not carry public JWTs or gameplay connect tokens. First-party browser/mobile/server clients use the short-lived `player-bootstrap` profile for HTTPS bootstrap and an HttpOnly-cookie connect token for the WebSocket handshake. Explicitly classified non-first-party/public generic WebSocket clients have no current supported connect-token carrier; the dedicated issuance route, secure-storage response, and `X-Firemud-Connect-Token` handshake header remain target-only until their authorization-matrix, replay, signed-context, response, and carrier proofs are complete. The Game Session Service calls the internal `Authenticate` gRPC method (mTLS-protected) to verify credential-bearing login and obtain `game-session-account-delegation` JWTs for Account, while `/auth/login` remains a browser/control-plane endpoint.
- Admin and creator UI authentication is initiated via the `/auth/login` HTTP endpoint, which issues `control-ui` JWTs used only for the exact control-plane audience described in the authentication and frontend architecture designs.
- Owns credential brute-force defense and login abuse handling for every password and verified-email-code path. It combines trusted canonical source context with normalized account-candidate and coarse pressure buckets, applies graduated temporary throttles, and emits stable retry outcomes. These buckets are policy signals, not durable authority; any Cache/Rate-Limit Redis use must preserve one-to-one opaque subject isolation or explicitly declare a coarse heuristic, bounded cardinality, eviction/reset behavior, and outage mode under [ADR 0087](../../decisions/adr-0087-isolated-subject-rate-limits-with-explicit-loss-semantics.md). Ordinary failed attempts never transition the account to `security_locked`; that durable state requires verified or high-confidence compromise, explicit security policy, or audited operator action. Account is also the sole canonical resolver and publisher of the effective versioned security policy; callers do not merge deployment or tenant overrides or fall back to cached/default values, and an unavailable or contradictory policy resolution fails sensitive actions closed. Suspicious activity triggers notification emails and audit events as described in [Security Architecture](../../system-architecture-security.md#brute-force-defense-and-abuse-handling) and [ADR 0034](../../decisions/adr-0034-layered-abuse-controls-without-attacker-triggered-account-locks.md).
- Non-gameplay workflows such as account creation or billing updates are orchestrated using the Saga pattern outlined in [Transaction Strategies](../../system-architecture-transactions.md).
- Leverages the [Shared Libraries](../../system-architecture-shared-libraries.md) for common DTOs, logging interceptors, and Micrometer metrics.

## Data Model

- `account` table stores username, password hash, email, and status flags for the global platform account.
- `profile` table captures optional user details and preferences.
- `achievement` table records earned achievements keyed by account and game.
- Target `external_account` records link a server-verified canonical `{provider, issuer, subject}` to one global platform account without tenant scope. The current tenant-scoped, caller-asserted row shape is implementation drift.
- Tenant-bound billing tables such as `subscription` and tenant-bound `payment_transaction` records associate `accountId` with `tenantId` for hosted games and hosting plans. Account-scoped transactions, grants, and donations carry no fabricated `tenantId`.
- Account-owned safety records persist separate `account_security_lock` and `platform_access_ban` revisions keyed by global `accountId` and category. Their current projection, monotonic enforcement epoch, idempotency/result records, source case/security-event identity, and safe notice support auth-generation and player-safe denial decisions without a Logging & Admin read. The complete schema/command implementation remains a target gap.
- Account-owned Creator Party, identified operator/terms versions, acceptance/currentness evidence, and future financial-reconciliation records are target-only. Game Design records only the local tenant/evidence binding needed for covered creator writes; it does not copy the party or terms catalog. No current storage or focused proof implements this boundary; see [Account API Contracts](./api-contracts.md#account-owned-hosted-terms-and-creator-party).
- Durable purchased-product grants are reserved for deferred future commerce and are separate from raw payment attempts. A future tenant-bound one-time purchase would store bindings to `accountId`, `tenantId`, an optional Entity-resolvable actor reference `{tenantId, playableStateNamespaceId, characterId}`, and `productCode`; those association fields are not fulfillment-uniqueness inputs. The sole fulfillment uniqueness pair is the immutable `(purchase_operation_id, product_grant_key)` defined by [Stripe Integration Design](./stripe-integration.md). Server-derived `playableStateScope` travels as separately validated policy/routing/authorization evidence for that actor and active instance, not as a durable actor key or uniqueness dimension. `gameInstanceId` may be added only as an explicit replaceable runtime fence when the operation requires it. Account-scoped grants use Account-owned records without `tenantId`. No v1 hosting-billing PaymentIntent, donation, or receipt creates this entitlement. Applying an account-scoped grant to a tenant-scoped feature requires an explicit tenant binding or consumption record. Payment rows remain financial/provider evidence, not entitlement authority.

Google, Discord, and Steam are optional provider-specific HTTPS integrations, not simultaneously promised launch capabilities. Initial delivery links a verified provider subject to an already authenticated global account; provider-first account creation is deferred. Provider email or display-name matches never merge accounts, every account retains Account-owned verified-email recovery and an ordinary login mode, and provider outage does not invalidate existing FireMUD sessions.

## Account Lifecycle State Model

The Account Service owns one canonical account lifecycle state machine for the global platform account. Tenant billing state can block gameplay for one tenant, but it does not change the global account state by itself.

| State | Login and bootstrap | Control-plane access | Billing/export access | Recovery | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `active` | Allowed subject to credentials, 2FA, and abuse controls | Allowed by route authorization | Allowed by route authorization | Allowed | Normal state. |
| `security_locked` | Denied with `AUTH_ACCOUNT_LOCKED` after sufficient identity proof; arbitrary failed attempts retain non-enumerating behavior | Existing sessions are revoked; new control-plane login is denied | The canonical `POST /accounts/{accountId}/exports` plus its matching status/content resources may be used only with an Account-issued, expiring `security-lock-export` recovery credential bound to that exact global account and export job after the Account-owned recovery authorization succeeds. The credential authorizes only that export lifecycle; it cannot cancel deletion, log in, bootstrap, mutate billing, or enter gameplay. `pending-deletion-access`, ordinary account sessions, and an unrestricted support credential are rejected | Recovery is allowed only through the Account-owned account-security recovery workflow. Support may initiate or attest only the explicitly audited recovery case; it cannot read export content or bypass Account's identity proof and credential issuance | Used for verified or high-confidence compromise, explicit account-security policy, or audited operator action. A failed-password threshold alone cannot enter this state. It is not a tenant gameplay ban. |
| `deactivated_pending_delete` | Denied for gameplay and normal control-plane login | Only the exact `pending_deletion_scoped` status, cancellation, export, and billing-settlement routes accept the dedicated Account-owned credential; the pending-deletion export is the full-subject `POST /accounts/{accountId}/exports` lifecycle with its status and content routes | Account-scoped portable-data export and billing-settlement reads remain available only through that bounded workflow credential; new purchases/subscriptions are denied | Allowed through the exact cancellation route before the retention window expires | Entered after a confirmed deletion request when asynchronous retention/settlement work remains. There is no implicit support or ordinary account-session bypass. |
| `deleted` | Denied | Denied | Only retained audit, tax, fraud, and settlement records remain available to platform-admin/reporting flows | Denied | Terminal account state; usernames/emails may be tombstoned or anonymized according to the retention policy. |

Transitions are explicit: `active` may move to `security_locked`, `deactivated_pending_delete`, or remain `active`; `security_locked` may return to `active` after successful recovery or move to `deactivated_pending_delete` only through a support-reviewed path; `deactivated_pending_delete` may return to `active` before the published cancellation window expires or move to `deleted`; `deleted` has no recovery transition. Every transition must emit an audit event and advance the Account-owned account authority generation, projecting `session:auth:generation:account:<accountId>` set-if-greater, so existing control-plane and player-bootstrap tokens stop working immediately when the new state denies access.

`security_locked` is the lifecycle projection for the protective `account_security_lock` category only. The punitive `platform_access_ban` is an independent Account-owned restriction projection that may coexist with every non-deleted lifecycle state. Recovery can clear the security lock after its required evidence but must leave a platform-access ban unchanged; a platform-access-ban removal requires its own authorized moderation revision. Account evaluates both projections at login, bootstrap, token/session authority, and other account access boundaries. When both deny the same boundary, it returns only the `platform_access_ban` safe outcome and does not expose the lower-priority security lock; both records remain independently active. A missing or unreadable restriction projection fails closed for the protected account operation.

The target restriction command commits the immutable category revision, current projection, account authority-generation effect, and idempotent result in one Account transaction. Logging & Admin may submit a digest-bound command for a punitive platform restriction or appeal outcome, but it remains the policy/case/audit owner and never writes Account tables. The current service does not yet prove this separate platform-ban table/command, recovery isolation, monotonic ordering, or end-to-end generation enforcement.

Account deletion is global, not tenant-scoped. A self-service request requires recent authentication, explicit confirmation, and a clear portable-data export option within the defined account scope. `DeleteAccount` must fail with `ACCOUNT_DELETE_ACTIVE_BILLING_OWNER` until billing authority proves that the account has no owned subscription in `pending`, `provisioning`, `trialing`, `active`, `past_due`, `grace`, or `suspended`, no nonterminal billing-owner transfer naming it as old or proposed owner, and no unresolved customer, subscription, webhook, outbox, cancellation, or provider-reconciliation work. The response returns all safely disclosable tenant and blocker categories. The caller must complete transfer or terminal cancellation and allow every related durable/provider item to reconcile before retrying deletion. The current implementation proves only the nonterminal-subscription subset of this target precondition.

An eligible confirmed request enters `deactivated_pending_delete`, immediately revokes ordinary account and bootstrap authority, and hides normal account/profile surfaces. Only explicit deletion cancellation, export, and necessary billing-settlement routes remain during the published cancellation and retention window. A durable, idempotent cross-service workflow then deletes, anonymizes, tombstones, or schedules policy-allowed retention for each owning domain. Failed work leaves the account pending with retry and operator diagnostics; a durably scheduled billing/provider step is intermediate pending evidence only and never terminal deletion. The account becomes terminal `deleted` only after every required non-retained step has completed and every permitted retained record has been durably finalized under its approved retention policy.

A billing-owner transfer is not cleared merely because its effective date passed: deletion requires terminal reconciliation as completed, or terminal cancellation with provider absence or old-subscription cancellation, plus clearance of every related customer, subscription, webhook, outbox, cleanup, cancellation, and provider-reconciliation blocker.

Deletion must not blindly hard-delete billing, payment, refund, Stripe customer, tax, fraud, moderation, security, or audit records that have an approved retained purpose. Under ADR 0050, every persistent subject-related category declares its owner, export behavior, terminal action, retention trigger, exact finite maximum, approved purpose, permitted readers, and backup treatment. Retained records keep only the minimum account correlation and access needed for that purpose; generic or indefinite “audit/compliance” retention is forbidden.

Account-owner portable-data export and tenant-admin export are separate contracts. `ExportAccount` is the ordinary account-owner export contract: the current implementation is the synchronous `GET /accounts/{accountId}/export`, while the target full-subject lifecycle is `POST /accounts/{accountId}/exports`, `GET /accounts/{accountId}/exports/{exportId}`, and `GET /accounts/{accountId}/exports/{exportId}/content`. When the account is `deactivated_pending_delete`, those target full-subject routes are explicitly allowlisted under `pending_deletion_scoped`; they are the pending-deletion export route and do not use ordinary account-session authority. The operation gathers every required owning-service contribution of portable subject data within the defined account scope that the caller is entitled to receive. Status/progress and an explicitly nonterminal versioned manifest remain readable while owner fan-in is partial or has retryable, failed, or omitted owner outcomes; only content download and any terminal artifact are gated on the required fan-in reaching its terminal contract. Missing, omitted, failed, or separately retained categories remain explicit; the manifest is not a promise to return every account-related record, secret, retained evidence, or provider object. Tenant billing-safe export is a separate tenant-scoped route for `tenantAdmin` recovery while a tenant is billing-blocked; it returns only tenant-owned exportable records and minimum subject references and must not expose global email, credentials, external identities, security state, or unrelated account data from other tenants.

For `security_locked`, the canonical account export route set uses only the distinct Account-issued `security-lock-export` credential produced by successful Account-owned recovery authorization. It binds the path account and export job to the same global subject, accepts no tenant selector, is single-use for export initiation and expiring for bounded status/content reads, and rejects pending-deletion credentials, ordinary sessions, and support-only access. The credential authorizes only that export lifecycle; it cannot log in, bootstrap, cancel deletion, mutate billing, or enter gameplay. Account records the recovery case and exact export authorization, while support cannot authorize or retrieve the artifact outside that workflow.

## Redis Role and Prefixes

- **Coordination Redis**
  - The Account Service does not participate in tick or gameplay coordination and never touches `tick:*`, `timer:*`, `retry:*`, or other tick-related prefixes on Coordination Redis; those responsibilities remain with the Game Session and Automation & Scripting services as described in [Redis Architecture](../../system-architecture-redis.md).
  - It does, however, use auth/session keys as documented in [Authentication & Authorization](../../system-architecture-authentication.md):
    - `session:auth:token:<tokenHash>` – the **target** one versioned issued-token record for a registry-backed `control-ui`, player-bootstrap, or receiver-specific private player-delegation JWT, consulted for control-plane/admission validity and immediate per-token revocation. The current runtime uses the legacy Account session key instead.
  - These records live on Coordination Redis so auth sessions share the same AOF and reset semantics as gameplay sessions. They are short-lived but reset-sensitive: coordination resets that drop `session:auth:*` force re-authentication and token re-issuance.
- **Cache/Rate-Limit Redis**
  - The Account Service does not maintain its own Cache/Rate-Limit Redis prefixes today; any future caches for account or profile lookups must use Cache/Rate-Limit Redis and the key naming/TTL/versioning rules in [Redis Cache & Rate Limiting](../../system-architecture-redis-cache.md), not Coordination Redis.
  - Account does not currently claim a Cache/Rate-Limit Redis bucket or cache as an authorization, financial, durable-quota, or security-lock authority. Any future abuse signal remains a bounded heuristic unless an authoritative owner contract is added.
  - When introducing new Redis usage here, follow the [Redis Design Checklist](../../system-architecture-redis-design-checklist.md) so auth/session keys, roles, and observability remain consistent with the global design.

If you change Redis usage for this service, you must read and apply:

- [Redis Architecture](../../system-architecture-redis.md)
- [Redis Cache & Rate Limiting](../../system-architecture-redis-cache.md)
- [Redis Operations & Migrations](../../system-architecture-redis-operations.md)

## Session and Token Model

**Target state:** Authentication generates a `control-ui`, player-bootstrap, or receiver-specific private player-delegation JWT and establishes exactly one server-side `session:auth:token:<tokenHash>` registry record before returning it, following [ADR 0035](../../decisions/adr-0035-single-record-issued-token-registry.md). The record proves exact issuance and active state; signed claims plus Account-owned revocation/version state determine tenant and global authority without scope-duplicated token keys. **Current runtime:** it still uses the legacy Account session key and does not yet provide this registry proof.

**Target state:** `tokenHash` is a fixed-length digest (a hex-encoded SHA-256 of the complete compact JWT), and the one registry record's TTL is derived from that token's own `exp` claim, not from a global session lifetime:

- `registry_ttl_ms = max(0, token_exp_ms - now_ms) + FIREMUD_AUTH_SESSION_SAFETY_MARGIN_MS`

The record therefore expires at the actual token expiry plus the cleanup margin; client activity does not extend it, and a newly issued or refreshed token gets its own calculation.

Gameplay continuity uses a separate `session_expiration_ms` setting. Its effective value is capped independently at `300000` ms (five minutes); changing the registry cleanup margin must not extend the continuity anchor.

See [Environment & Secrets](../../infrastructure/environment-and-secrets.md#authentication) for configuration details.

The exact JWT profiles share the base claim schema (`iss`, `sub`, `jti`, `accountId`, `aud`, `iat`, `nbf`, `exp`, optional profile-specific roles) but differ in audience and issuance flow. The approved Game Session-to-Account control path uses profile `game-session-account-delegation` with exact audience `account-service`. New endpoints must document the exact profile they issue or accept and validate the exact audience before trusting a token.

The `player-bootstrap` token profile is distinct from both `control-ui` and private player-delegation JWTs:

- Audience is `player-bootstrap`.
- It is issued only by `POST /auth/player-bootstrap` / `IssuePlayerBootstrapToken`.
- `POST /auth/player-bootstrap` is the canonical first-party browser/mobile player-login endpoint. It authenticates player credentials directly under the same password, OTP, brute-force defense, and account-lock policy used for gameplay login rather than deriving bootstrap from a pre-existing admin/creator session.
- It establishes account identity for first-party gameplay bootstrap only. Target behavior is that public-game membership is created only by explicit `/auth/bootstrap/join`; character creation and `POST /auth/connect-token` later require that membership and recheck applicable grants, entitlements, and the discovery-selected runtime target. The current runtime requires existing caller-bound membership for connect-token issuance and text `PLAY` and uses only `membershipExists`, `gameplayAdmissionAllowed`, `membershipVersion`, and `evaluatedAt`. For a publicly visible public-production target whose joining policy permits entry, `membershipExists=false` may return `JOIN_REQUIRED` without creating or restoring membership; an existing non-admitting response is not lifecycle-aware. Non-public or non-visible targets remain subject to their realm-access and grant checks rather than using public onboarding semantics.
- It is stored in memory only by first-party gameplay UIs and is accepted only on gameplay-bootstrap surfaces such as bootstrap discovery and `POST /auth/connect-token`.
- For first-party `/ws/game/**`, subsequent gameplay `LOGIN` must complete from the bootstrap/connect context already established for that socket; browser clients must not be required to replay account credentials after bootstrap.
- If the token is lost because the page/app process is restarted, the client must restart bootstrap from `POST /auth/player-bootstrap`; the current architecture does not define a hidden silent-bootstrap refresh mechanism.
- It is backed by one `session:auth:token:<tokenHash>` record so per-token logout and account-level revocation semantics remain consistent.
- `POST /auth/logout` and `POST /auth/logout-all` must accept this profile so first-party gameplay UIs can explicitly revoke bootstrap capability on sign-out rather than waiting for expiry.

Gameplay clients never hold control-plane `control-ui` JWTs or private player-delegation JWTs. The only JWT profile first-party gameplay clients may hold directly is this short-lived `player-bootstrap` token.

The Account Service is the sole owner and writer of issuer, account, tenant, and membership authority generations and their `session:auth:generation:*` projections. Other services request authority changes via events or APIs and must not write Account authority state or projection keys directly.

Token refresh, replacement, generation, revocation, fencing, and logout are canonical in [JWT and Token Contracts](../../system-architecture-jwt-and-token-contracts.md); this document does not restate their field or ordering contract. Account is the authority and transaction boundary for those operations and maintains the local registry, durable authority, audit, and outbox records required by that contract.

Game Session orchestrates binding-refresh installation and reconciliation through Account-owned operations; it never writes Account-owned registry or authority state. Current runtime still uses the legacy Account session keys rather than the target issued-token registry, so the target refresh, replacement, fencing, and logout proof remains unavailable.

## Membership and Entitlement Authority

Billing-safe mutation authority contract:

- The Account Service provides an authoritative membership API for live billing-safe checks: `GetCallerTenantMembership(tenantId)` (REST equivalent `GET /tenants/{tenantId}/memberships/me`), where subject account is bound from caller auth context.
- Cross-tenant membership reads use a separate API: `GetTenantMembershipForAccount(tenantId, accountId)` (REST equivalent `GET /tenants/{tenantId}/memberships/{accountId}`), restricted to `billingAdmin`/`platformAdmin`.
- The Account Service provides a separate internal membership API for gameplay/runtime callers: `GetTenantMembershipForRuntime(accountId, tenantId, requestId)` (or protocol-equivalent). Game Session must use this API for `PLAY`, reconnect/resume validation, and membership-gap reconciliation rather than reusing billing-safe caller-bound routes.
- Membership responses include authoritative `membershipExists`, `evaluatedAt`, and `membershipVersion` fields so callers can distinguish absence from denied admission, verify freshness, and detect stale reads. The target response additionally carries `membershipLifecycleState`, `authorityAvailability`, `roles[]`, the complete applicable `authorityTuple`, an opaque `membershipAuthorityGeneration`, and the matching committed Account authority-outbox checkpoint; callers compare Account-owned generations only for exact equality and never interpret them.
- If authoritative membership data is unavailable, callers must fail closed for billing-safe mutations rather than relying on JWT claims alone.

Membership freshness evidence contract:

- Account reads the membership state, `membershipVersion`, the complete applicable `authorityTuple` (including the Account-owned membership authority generation), `evaluatedAt`, and the applicable committed `outboxCheckpoints` set from one authoritative snapshot/transaction or equivalent Account-owned fence. It returns those values together, or returns Account-authenticated evidence over exactly those values; callers must not assemble them from separate membership, generation, projection, or event reads.
- The target membership response minimum therefore includes `accountId`, `tenantId`, `roles[]`, `gameplayAdmissionAllowed`, `membershipVersion`, `membershipAuthorityGeneration`, `authorityTuple`, `evaluatedAt`, and `outboxCheckpoints[]`. The checkpoint set uses `{outboxStreamKey, outboxSequence, sourceEventId, sourceEventDigest}` for every positive sequence and must contain the exact entry for `account:auth-authority:v1:membership/<accountId>/<tenantId>` that covers the returned membership and tuple snapshot. `membershipAuthorityGeneration` is the explicit membership member of that tuple; any separately carried value must authenticate the same value and is not an independent authority.
- `evaluatedAt` is the UTC evaluation time of that complete authoritative snapshot and is not restamped when an older projection or cached row is read. A response is invalid when any required field or checkpoint is absent, when the checkpoint does not cover the tuple and membership version, or when the values cannot be proven to come from the same snapshot. Reachable contradictory, stale, regressed, or mismatched evidence is denied; an unavailable or timed-out Account evidence dependency remains retryable `AUTH_UNAVAILABLE`. A distinct authorization outcome may be defined only by the owning API contract for a different operation, not as an alternative code for this runtime membership dependency.

The token `jti` remains only in the dedicated issuance/replay record and access-controlled security audit evidence; it is not returned to clients.

Browser/mobile clients and first-party non-browser clients with a protected cookie jar retain and use the cookie normally. - Bootstrap discovery surfaces must return the target snapshot `connectScopeId`, which cryptographically binds the exact `tenantId`, `worldSlug`, `realmSlug`, `playableStateNamespaceId`, `playableStateScope`, `gameInstanceId`, `catalogRevision`, `pointerVersion`, and `connectScopeExpiresAt`, together with `evaluatedAt`; every non-public target additionally binds exact `playtestLifecycleId` and positive `playtestStateGeneration`, while a non-grant public-production target omits both fields.

Account must not silently re-resolve or select a newer target. Missing proto or caller support for carrying and proving this complete bound snapshot through that gate is implementation drift.

This target producer path remains unimplemented.

If Gateway may already have consumed the token, the caller must rediscover and use a new `requestId` rather than replaying the prior issuance key.

An exact retry replays `JOIN_REQUIRED` even if membership is created later; the caller must use a new request ID after the explicit join. The target replay outcome is durable for the complete issuance-idempotency window. The current implementation provides only the bounded Session Service replay record, so durable database-backed failure replay remains an implementation gap. A private or playtest target additionally requires the current Account-owned realm-access grant; a grant never substitutes for membership or public onboarding.

The internal boundary authenticates the exact Game Session mTLS workload and validates its typed caller-bound `PlayerExecutionContext`, including `accountId`, before using the scope. Connect-token issuance and public-production `PLAY` must require its result but never invoke it implicitly. Explicit `JOIN`/`Join & Play` remains target-only and unimplemented; no implicit membership writer is exposed by connect-token issuance or `PLAY`.

A newer target is not silently selected. It binds this exact freshness baseline to the same `connectScopeId` and `requestId` through issuance and commit. Membership-change producer contract:

- The target Account producer commits the membership change, applicable `authorityTuple`/`issuanceFence`, `membershipVersion`, and the outbox row in one Account transaction or composite CAS/fence. It emits membership-change events with `schemaVersion`, `eventId`, `eventType`, `outboxStreamKey`, `outboxSequence`, `sourceScope`, `accountId`, `tenantId`, `membershipVersion`, the Account-committed opaque `membershipAuthorityGeneration` for that exact `{accountId, tenantId}` scope, the complete applicable `authorityTuple`, `issuanceFence`, changed roles, a flag indicating whether gameplay admission remains allowed, and the Account-committed boolean `callerBoundAuthorityInvalidated` from that same snapshot/checkpoint.
- The membership event stream is exactly `account:auth-authority:v1:membership/<accountId>/<tenantId>`. Its `sourceScope` is exactly `membership/<accountId>/<tenantId>` and must decode to the scope encoded by `outboxStreamKey`; a mismatch is malformed evidence. `outboxSequence` starts at `1` independently for this exact stream and is allocated in the same Account commit as the membership authority change. `membershipVersion` remains the separate monotonic membership projection version and is never substituted for `outboxSequence` or `membershipAuthorityGeneration`.
- `membershipVersion` must advance on any membership or role change that can affect gameplay admission or caller-bound tenant authority.
- When a membership or tenant-role change invalidates existing caller-bound tenant authorization, the Account Service must also advance the `{accountId, tenantId}` membership authority generation and project `session:auth:generation:membership:<accountId>:<tenantId>` so previously issued control-plane tokens lose tenant authority immediately rather than waiting for expiry.
- Each consumer keeps a `watermark` (`lastAppliedOutboxSequence`) and the applied event identity/digest per exact `outboxStreamKey`, never one global watermark. `contiguous` is true only when `outboxSequence == watermark + 1`; a first event is initialized from the matching Account checkpoint and is not treated as a false gap. A sequence at or below the watermark is a no-op only when its event identity/digest and all authority evidence match. The same sequence with conflicting identity, digest, `sourceScope`, `authorityTuple`, `issuanceFence`, `membershipVersion`, or membership payload is a stream conflict and must quarantine the affected scope and fail closed. A higher sequence is a gap only for that same stream; an unrelated account, tenant, issuer, or grant stream never creates a membership gap. On a gap, non-contiguous delivery, or conflict, consumers stop affected admission and reconnect/validation, reconcile the exact Account snapshot/checkpoint, and advance the watermark only after the missing range or conflict is proven resolved. Unavailable reconciliation is retryable unavailable; reachable contradictory or revoked evidence remains denied.
- Consumers retain only a finite, deployment-configured replay-evidence window per exact `outboxStreamKey`, with a maximum sequence count and retention TTL, keyed by `outboxSequence`; each retained entry includes `eventId`, the canonical event digest, `sourceScope`, the complete applicable `authorityTuple`/`issuanceFence`, and the relevant membership/payload evidence. When a duplicate at or below the watermark falls outside that window, the consumer must use an Account-owned authoritative lookup for the exact `(outboxStreamKey, outboxSequence)` and compare the supplied evidence for exact equality. Matching evidence is accepted as a no-op; unavailable lookup is retryable unavailable and keeps the affected scope fail closed; reachable missing or conflicting evidence is quarantined for reconciliation and fails closed. Advancing a watermark never makes an unverified older duplicate acceptable.
- The focused producer proof must show that the event's `membershipAuthorityGeneration`, `membershipVersion`, complete `authorityTuple`, and checkpoint are the values committed for the same membership snapshot; none may be reconstructed from `membershipVersion`, arrival order, or a projection read after commit. Consumers track membership-version ordering separately from the opaque authority generation, compare that generation only for exact Account-authorized equality, and must not use either value as the event dedupe key. Any mismatch requires reconciliation through Account or an Account-owned comparison API before the consumer admits or retains authority.

### Playtest Grant Persistence and Authority Consequences

The complete Account-owned grant mutation and replay contract is defined in [Account API Contracts](./api-contracts.md#account-owned-playtest-grant-contract). This runtime document owns only its data and projection consequences: persist one grant scope `{accountId, tenantId, worldSlug, realmSlug, playtestLifecycleId}` with active/revoked state, `grantRevision`, explicit `grantExpiresAt`, fork-expiry reference, grantor/audit metadata, request outcome, and a retained revocation tombstone. The grant row must never be hard-deleted while delayed request replay could still arrive. Each mutation also persists a separate durable grant-mutation operation/replay record—not a second grant row—with `requestId`, ADR 0047's exact seven-field `mutationDigest/v1`, normalized operation, complete lifecycle-bound target, requested expiry, conditional `expectedGrantRevision` mapped to typed `expectedVersion` (absent only for the initial `CREATE` precondition), pending or terminal outcome, and the configured retention/deduplication horizon. Exact request/digest retries replay that operation result and changed inputs conflict before grant mutation; the operation record is distinct from both the current grant row and its retained revocation tombstone.

Account's grant write transaction compares `expectedGrantRevision` against the current active grant for `EXTEND`/`REVOKE`, against the current grant or retained tombstone for `REGRANT`, or verifies the absent-grant-and-tombstone precondition for `CREATE`; on success it commits the next revision, grant/tombstone projection, durable result, and authority event atomically. A stale or failed precondition is a deterministic `GRANT_REVISION_CONFLICT` with no grant, tombstone, result, or event mutation. Concurrent operations sharing one expected revision have exactly one commit; an exact request retry replays its stored result without another revision or event.

Account evaluates effective access as `min(grantExpiresAt, forkExpiresAt)` and emits a monotonic grant authority event on create, extension, revocation, expiry, and deliberate re-grant as part of the successful mutation commit. The stream is scoped to the exact grant, with the same per-stream sequence, event identity/digest, checkpoint, and gap/conflict fail-closed rules as other Account authority streams. Consumers do not reconstruct grant state from a JWT, realm slug, cached discovery result, or a separate fork read. A missing, stale, contradictory, or unavailable grant projection denies non-public discovery/admission and requires exact Account reconciliation.

Grant `scopeId`, `outboxStreamKey`, and event `sourceScope` are produced and parsed through the [canonical grant authority-stream scope codec](../../system-architecture-redis.md#canonical-grant-authority-stream-scope-codec). This document owns only the persistence and projection consequences; it does not define another stream grammar or fallback identity.

Current `AccountRealmAccessGrant` persistence and internal grant reads are limited to the legacy four-field identity, with only limited text `PLAY` denial; no lifecycle-bound read, expiry, fork-expiry evaluation, tenant-admin mutation surface, expected-revision CAS, request-replay record, durable tombstone, revocation propagation, consumer reconciliation, active-binding ejection, or complete grant event proof exists. During the direct pre-v1 cutover, records lacking `playtestLifecycleId` are invalidated or denied and must be regranted under the exact lifecycle; this local status does not add an alias or compatibility path. The gap statement is intentionally local; endpoint ownership and mutation outcomes remain in [Account API Contracts](./api-contracts.md#account-owned-playtest-grant-contract) and [ADR 0138](../../decisions/adr-0138-expiring-playtest-grants-with-bounded-active-revocation.md).

### Runtime Implementation Status and Drift

- Account-first bootstrap now authenticates before tenant selection and accepts configured password or verified-email OTP modes. `GetTenantMembershipForRuntime(accountId, tenantId, requestId)`, `GetTenantEntitlementsForRuntime(tenantId, requestId)`, and `GetAdmissionPointer(tenantId, worldSlug, realmSlug)` are present. Current connect-token issuance and text `PLAY` consume `membershipExists`, `gameplayAdmissionAllowed`, `membershipVersion`, and `evaluatedAt`, proceed only when the two admission fields are true, and may return non-actionable `JOIN_REQUIRED` for an eligible public-production request with `membershipExists=false`; an existing non-admitting response cannot be classified as `INACTIVE` because lifecycle state is target-only. Current non-public missing or non-admitting membership behavior remains the existing `WORLD_ACCESS_DENIED` path; target-only `NON_PUBLIC_ENROLLMENT_REQUIRED` is not current behavior. Non-public or non-visible targets remain grant-gated and do not use public onboarding semantics.
- Explicit `JOIN`/`Join & Play` and the membership-authority-generation reread at connect-token issuance remain target follow-through. Broader reconnect/cutover consumption of admission-pointer truth and operator-facing pointer tooling are also incomplete.

Runtime and data consequences remain local to this document: `payment_transaction` is the financial/provider audit record, while `purchase_entitlement` is reserved for a separately accepted future marketplace and is not a v1 hosting-billing authority. Account must not infer gameplay value from payment status alone; a future paid-but-unfulfilled grant would remain non-active and therefore non-authorizing, and its durable operation state would remain visible to reconciliation and deletion guards. Existing donation rows and virtual-currency/premium-hosting code remain implementation substrate unless a supported product contract explicitly admits them; creator donations/tips are deferred.

### Deferred Creator Monetization

Creator-directed player purchases, donations/tips, platform fees, revenue sharing, payouts, and settlement are deferred and unsupported. Existing `currency_balance`, donation, creator-share, and generic payment code is substrate only; it cannot create gameplay entitlements or establish Creator Party authority. Under future direction [ADR 0179](../../decisions/adr-0179-firemud-managed-creator-commerce-boundary.md), a separately opted-in, FireMUD-managed path would use Account for financial reconciliation and entitlement authority, but its routes, schemas, fee, payout, and settlement behavior remain unselected and unimplemented. Hosted-content acceptance, payment, tenant roles, or billing ownership do not imply marketplace enrollment.

### V1 Hosting Billing

Account owns the sole supported v1 Stripe integration for FireMUD hosting-plan billing. A tenant's hosting subscription, provider event/reconciliation state, billing-owner binding, and derived entitlement/availability outcome remain Account authority. Game Session, World Management, and lifecycle/admission consumers use Account's billing outcomes and never call Stripe or copy provider logic. Billing-safe account and tenant management remains reachable when gameplay is denied for billing reasons. The complete provider webhook, reconciliation, and end-to-end entitlement enforcement proof remains partial as recorded in [Stripe Integration Design](./stripe-integration.md).

### Email and Notification Design

This service sends verification and password reset emails using a configured SMTP provider. Notifications for suspicious logins or account events are queued for asynchronous delivery via a gRPC `NotificationService`. Sample templates live under `resources/templates/` and environment variables configure `spring.mail.*` along with `firemud.mail.from`, `firemud.mail.verification-url`, and `firemud.mail.reset-url`. The gRPC API is defined in [`notification_service.proto`](../../../../protos/account/v1/notification_service.proto).
