# Microservices Responsibility Matrix

Checkmarks in this table indicate **participation** in a workflow. Rows prefixed with `Authoritative owner:` identify the sole invariant-enforcement or policy-of-record authority for that function. Cross-cutting contracts such as Multi-Tenancy are named explicitly in the function label; service checkmarks on those rows show runtime serving or participation and do not transfer the cross-cutting authority.

Platform Operations is a cross-cutting operational authority rather than a microservice column in this matrix: it owns object-storage, CDN, registry, and promotion-evidence infrastructure and delivery. Game Design remains the authority for publication coordination, release descriptors, asset lifecycle, CAS state, and purge eligibility; domain services retain participant data and digest authority. The first-party frontend is likewise not a microservice column: [FRONT-01](./decisions/adr-0144-stateless-first-party-frontend-application-boundary.md) assigns it an independently released static presentation artifact with no durable gameplay, session, authorization, or domain authority.

## Target Responsibility Model

The matrix below is the target participation and ownership contract. `Authoritative owner:` rows identify the sole invariant or policy authority; other participants coordinate or provide bounded evidence through documented contracts and do not create competing state authority.

## Implementation Status

- Live external operator availability includes the Gateway edge plus Logging & Admin domain ingress for reads, investigation, admission-pointer reads/audit, and prepared-upgrade proof reads. TCP Proxy does not participate in this HTTP operator read/proof-read path. `EvaluateModerationPolicy` is a separate live internal owner-boundary read consumed by Game Session and Social & Groups, not external operator availability. The target mutation rows below are normative contracts, not claims that every listed mutation is currently routable.
- The service-local `/moderation/actions` and `ApplyModerationAction` path currently persists the generic legacy `moderation_actions` row, but it is unavailable at external Gateway ingress, persists no separate fixed-category audit evidence, and performs no owner-side enforcement. The target path remains gated pending the action-family schema, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` golden vectors, Account authorization-reference issuance, and Logging & Admin receiving-service validation/redemption. Synchronous `EvaluateModerationPolicy` consumption at `GAMEPLAY_ADMISSION` and `CHAT_SEND` remains a separate live owner-boundary read for Game Session and Social & Groups; those services own gameplay/chat enforcement, while Logging & Admin retains policy-input responsibility rather than consuming those enforcement decisions.
- Feature-flag overrides, version-upgrade preparation, scoped per-instance tick pause/resume, and admission-pointer CAS/cutover have implementation or owner contract seams but remain externally gated pending their action-family schemas, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` golden vectors, and Account-issued authorization-reference issuance/redemption flow and, where applicable, the target-only `privileged_control` issuance flow. Admission-pointer CAS/cutover is implemented-participating ownership, not target-only capability; it is not currently exposed as an externally available mutation. Broader guarded tick enumeration, regional mutation, and reset/remediate orchestration remain target-only.
- Game Session `/sessions*` routes are current owner-local hooks; direct owner edge exposure is denied. The target external lifecycle ingress is `Gateway -> Logging & Admin -> Game Session` owner RPCs, but lifecycle forwarding is target-only and non-routable until its owner contract, action-family schema, shared digest vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption flow exist. No complete current external `/sessions*` family is claimed.
- Versioned moderation propagation, hypothetical quota overrides, and broader tick remediation remain target-only/non-routable coverage without current executable owner routes. They cannot be exposed through Gateway or treated as Logging & Admin forwarding routes until the owning service contract, action-family schema, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` golden vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption flow exist. The currently supported ingress remains limited to the live reads, investigation, admission-pointer reads/audit, prepared-upgrade proof reads, and internal owner-boundary moderation evaluation listed above; this target-only status does not remove or widen those current paths.
- The causal-read fence/composite room-view row is target-state only: current proto/request paths remain floor-free, and implementation/proof are unimplemented. See [ADR 0059](./decisions/adr-0059-causal-floor-cross-service-presentation-reads.md) and the [world-runtime-and-movement tracker](../project-management/implementation-tracking/world-runtime-and-movement.md) for current status. The accepted responsibility split remains Game Session allocation, Game Logic composition, and World Management/Entity Management participant provision.
- Plain-text gameplay remains the universal first-party WebSocket, generic text WebSocket, and Telnet/bridge representation. The versioned `PlayerOutput` projection is a Game Session-owned presentation contract consumed by first-party WebSocket clients; the frontend is only a presentation consumer. Classic-client semantic negotiation is deferred and unadvertised under [MCP-01](./decisions/adr-0145-plain-text-gameplay-and-deferred-classic-client-extensions.md); if later selected, Game Session owns semantics and TCP Proxy remains a generic bounded transport bridge.

The `<tenantId, gameInstanceId, regionId>` tuple in the tick and coordination health metrics row is the diagnostic scope Game Session must support through control-plane status, structured logs, and audit records. It is not a Prometheus label tuple: metric series must use bounded `scope`, `scope_bucket`, `region_class`, or equivalent operational buckets under the cardinality policy, while exact identities remain in diagnostic records.

For the edge-routable services in this matrix, participation does not imply that every mutation may be called directly by external tools. Per the overview’s canonical operator write ingress policy, external mutating operator workflows for moderation, quota overrides, runtime feature-flag overrides, admission control, session lifecycle, tick remediation, and version-upgrade preparation must enter through Logging & Admin. Direct external writes on other edge-routable services require an explicit bypass-safe designation in the owning service contract. Game Design tenant-scoped asset and template creator writes are the current architecture-level bypass-safe write class delegated to an owning service contract.

Service docs may not create new external bypass-safe write classes on their own. If a workflow is not explicitly allowlisted by the overview or this matrix, treat it as non-bypass-safe until the architecture docs are updated.

Route-review procedure:

1. Classify the proposed route using the [canonical route-review examples and traffic split](./system-architecture-overview.md#external-admin-traffic-split-canonical).
2. Confirm the matching participation and ingress rows below; participation alone does not authorize a direct external mutation.

- The exact current matrix rows are **External operator read/proof-read ingress** and **External operator write ingress**; their current availability and drift are recorded in **Implementation Status**, while the rows remain the canonical participation contract. Version-upgrade preparation is a mutation and belongs only to the gated write-ingress row; the read row covers proof reads, not preparation writes. Admission-pointer CAS/cutover is implemented but externally gated, so its ownership row must not be described as target-only.
- Proposed route: `POST /api/session/game-sessions/{id}/feature-flags/{flagKey}:toggle`. Matrix check: `Game Session` participates in `Admin/creator API participation`, but the **External operator write ingress** row routes this workflow through `Logging & Admin`, so the direct external Game Session route is not allowed without a design update; current availability is governed by **Implementation Status**.
- Proposed route: `POST /api/design/templates`. Matrix check: `Game Design` participates in `Admin/creator API participation`, and `Bypass-safe Game Design creator writes for tenant-scoped assets and templates` delegates this domain-local creator write to the Game Design service contract, so the route may be edge-routable when Game Design documents tenant access, validation, and audit behavior.
- Proposed route: `GET /api/account/accounts/{id}`. Matrix check: `Account Service` participates in `Admin/creator API participation`, and the request is an external admin read rather than an operator mutation covered by the **External operator write ingress** row, so the route may be edge-routable when the owning service documents it as a bypass-safe read contract.

The gated moderation policy-input row assigns target `POST /moderation/actions` policy-input persistence and audit to Logging & Admin; its separate live `EvaluateModerationPolicy` read supplies runtime decisions to Game Session and Social & Groups. Game Session owns `GAMEPLAY_ADMISSION` enforcement and Social & Groups owns `CHAT_SEND` enforcement. Currently, the service-local `/moderation/actions` and `ApplyModerationAction` path persists only the generic legacy `moderation_actions` row; it persists no separate fixed-category audit evidence, performs no owner-side enforcement, and has no external Gateway route. Target owner-side enforcement, quota, runtime, admission-control/pointer-write, tick, and lifecycle mutations use the canonical Logging & Admin ingress and remain individually gated by their owning action, digest, and authorization contracts.

## Notes on Redis Ownership and Participation

- **Independent version and admission CAS domains** – Game Design Service owns the version lifecycle state and `versionStateEpoch` CAS contract used for activation and rollback. Game Session and Logging & Admin may invoke that typed lifecycle API through their documented control-plane paths, but neither owns the version epoch. Game Session owns the admission-pointer read/preparation implementation and the implemented-but-externally-gated `pointerVersion` CAS and cutover contract for `{tenantId, worldSlug, realmSlug}`. Gateway participates at the edge for operator/proof reads and write ingress, while Logging & Admin is the domain ingress that invokes the gated operation. Neither edge participation nor operator ingress transfers authority from Game Session. The two CAS tokens are independent and must not be substituted for one another.

- **Authoritative owner: Coordination Redis gameplay sessions (`session:game:*`)** – Game Session Service owns gameplay session bindings, lifecycle, and reset scope expectations for these keys. Other services participate only through documented shared helper libraries and key contracts; they do not introduce new gameplay session prefixes or modify TTLs/payload semantics without Game Session ownership and Redis design review.
- **Authoritative owner: Coordination Redis gameplay coordination keys (`tick:*`, `timer:*`, `retry:*`, `tick-executor-lease:*`)** – Game Session Service owns gameplay coordination schema and lifecycle for these prefixes. Other services participate only through documented shared helper libraries and key contracts; they do not introduce new gameplay coordination prefixes or modify TTLs/payload semantics without Game Session ownership and Redis design review.
- **Authoritative owner: Coordination Redis auth sessions (`session:auth:*`)** – Account Service exclusively owns the issued-token registry and revocation/version semantics, including lifecycle, revocation, and scope contracts consumed by downstream services; Game Session does not mutate this keyspace.
- **Authoritative owner: Coordination Redis connect-token replay (`gateway:connect-token:jti:*` and replay-readiness fence)** – Spring Cloud Gateway owns only this narrow edge replay-consumption keyspace and its readiness fence. It does not own general gameplay sessions, Account auth state, or broader coordination policy.
- **Redis-backed automation ownership split** – Automation & Scripting Service owns:
  - Coordination Redis scheduler/timer keys such as `automation:timer:*` and `script-scheduler:*`.
  - Cache/Rate-Limit Redis `automation:queue:*`, `automation:quota:*`, `automation:tenant-budget:*`, and `automation:test:capacity:*` best-effort queues/counters.
  Game Session and other services interact with automation via gRPC APIs, not by writing `automation:*` keys directly.
- **Cache/Rate-Limit Redis usage (caches, quotas, rate limiting)** – World Management Service, TCP Proxy Service, Spring Cloud Gateway, Entity Management Service, Automation & Scripting Service, and Social & Groups Service all use shared cache and rate‑limit helpers backed by Redis (for example, `cache:*` and `ratelimit:*` prefixes). World Management is authoritative for invalidation semantics of `room:*` and `world-dynamic:*` world caches; the schema, TTL policies, and correctness guarantees for these prefixes are defined in the shared cache/rate-limit library and in the Redis Cache & Rate Limiting design.

These ownership boundaries are normative per `design/architecture/decisions/adr-0009-coordination-redis-ownership-boundary.md`.

## Notes on Movement and Moderation Contracts

- **Movement/location write contract orchestration** – Game Session orchestrates movement under tick/effect identity and owns per-session sequencing plus the current execution-region pointer; Game Logic computes deterministic movement outcomes; World Management commits authoritative room occupancy/location and validates its targeting token, while Entity Management applies entity-side consequences without owning occupancy indexes. The canonical stale-evidence and executor-fence mutation/barrier contract is [ADR 0054](./decisions/adr-0054-split-spatial-authority-with-causal-read-composition.md). For `ResolveLook` presentation reads, Game Session allocates the `CausalReadFence` from durable region commit authority and Game Logic propagates it unchanged to World and Entity.
- **Movement hot-path shape** – Under [ADR 0056](./decisions/adr-0056-one-hot-path-fan-out-owner.md), Game Session dispatches the fenced operation to Game Logic as the one fan-out owner, and Game Logic coordinates World Management plus Entity Management as its at-most-two authoritative participants. Neither participant may recursively create synchronous fan-out, and movement has no separate participant-count exception.
- **Canonical room-state causal floor** – The [Identifier Glossary causal-read fence contract](./system-architecture-identifier-glossary.md#cross-service-causal-read-fence-identity) and [ADR 0059](./decisions/adr-0059-causal-floor-cross-service-presentation-reads.md) own the requested floor and composite identity. Game Session allocates the fence from one Game Session-owned durable region-status snapshot after authority validation, using its operational `regionId` and `regionEpoch`, with `committedTickId` set to that snapshot's `lastCommittedTickId`; an accepted epoch starts at `-1` before tick `0`, and allocator state does not cross an epoch boundary. It passes the fence on `ResolveLook`; Game Logic propagates it unchanged to World and Entity, composes same-scope/epoch responses using served-through validation and opaque component versions, and rejects or retries responses behind the requested floor. Game Session owns request initiation, ordering, and transcript rendering/cache behavior, but it is not the downstream read orchestrator for `GetRoomSnapshot` plus `ListRoomEntities`. See the [RegionStatus timeline contract](./system-architecture-ticks.md#bootstrap-vs-stream-authoritative-timeline-source) and canonical room runtime contract in `design/architecture/system-architecture-overview.md`.
- **Item command runtime split** – Game Session owns text-session ingress and transcript rendering for player item commands; Game Logic owns the gameplay-facing item command RPC seam; Entity Management remains authoritative for item/container/equipment persistence, holder mutation, validation, and transfer audit writes.
- **Operator-action coverage split** – Admission-pointer `GET` reads, audit, and prepared-upgrade proof reads are the live external operator read ingress through Gateway and Logging & Admin. Version-upgrade preparation is an implemented operator-facing mutation but remains externally gated and unavailable pending its action-family schema, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` golden vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption. Runtime feature-flag overrides, admission-control/pointer writes, and scoped per-instance tick pause/resume remain unavailable pending their action-family schemas, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` golden vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption; `/moderation/actions` and `ApplyModerationAction` are unavailable for policy-input/audit persistence pending their action schema, shared vectors, Account authorization-reference issuance, and Logging & Admin receiving-service validation/redemption, and remain unavailable for owner-side enforcement. Game Session's `/sessions*` lifecycle routes are current owner-local hooks, not a complete external ingress family; target lifecycle forwarding enters through Gateway to Logging & Admin and then Game Session owner RPCs. Admission-pointer open/close/retarget CAS and cutover are implemented but externally gated and unavailable through the external mutation ingress pending the same action-family, digest, and authorization-reference prerequisites. Current gameplay/chat enforcement synchronously consumes the live `EvaluateModerationPolicy` read; versioned propagation, quota override, and broader reset/remediate actions remain target families.
- **Tick remediation split** – Scoped per-instance pause/resume is implementation-participating but externally gated: Logging & Admin owns its ingress, target-state remediation API, automation policy, and audit trail, while Game Session owns the mutation in its gameplay coordination keyspaces. No live admin UI or automation is claimed until the action-family schema, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption exist. Broader guarded enumeration, regional mutation, and reset/remediate orchestration remain target-only until their owner APIs exist. Game Session owns mutation only within its gameplay coordination keyspaces, including `tick:*`, `timer:*`, `retry:*`, `session:game:*`, and `tick-executor-lease:*`; Account exclusively owns `session:auth:*`, and this explicitly excludes both the Account auth family and Automation & Scripting-owned `automation:tick:*`, `automation:timer:*`, and `script-scheduler:*` families.
- **Replacement-instance compatibility preflight (External operator read/proof-read ingress)** – Game Session owns `ValidateInstanceCutoverCompatibility` orchestration and result semantics; Game Design, World, Entity, Automation, and Logging/Admin participate as dependency and policy providers for checks. Version-upgrade preparation remains part of the separate external operator write ingress.
- **Moderation policy propagation** – Logging & Admin solely owns gameplay-ban/chat-mute/chat-ban policy definitions, policy-input/audit records, and the gated moderation mutation ingress; Social & Groups does not own those records or ingress. `POST /moderation/actions` and `ApplyModerationAction` do not currently persist or perform owner-side enforcement; they remain unavailable pending the action-family schema, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` vectors, Account authorization-reference issuance, and Logging & Admin receiving-service validation/redemption. The separate live `EvaluateModerationPolicy` read supplies Game Session and Social & Groups at their owner boundaries; those services retain gameplay and chat enforcement respectively. Versioned policy snapshot/event propagation, pull-on-miss refresh, and broader enforcement remain missing; the applicable high-risk decisions fail closed when fresh policy evidence is unavailable. See the canonical moderation propagation contract in `design/architecture/system-architecture-overview.md`.
- **Ban taxonomy** – Account owns `account_security_lock` security/recovery and `platform_access_ban` enforcement, including the required account authority-generation advances; Logging & Admin owns the gated gameplay/chat moderation policy-input and audit records. The live `EvaluateModerationPolicy` read is consumed only at the Game Session and Social & Groups owner boundaries. Game Session and Social & Groups enforce gameplay and chat decisions respectively. The gated moderation enforcement mutation endpoint is unavailable and does not directly perform runtime enforcement.
- **Admin/creator API allowlist policy** – Gateway owns the edge-route allowlist policy; domain services own only the API contracts behind allowlisted routes.
- **External operator read/proof-read ingress** – Gateway is the external edge ingress and Logging & Admin is the domain ingress for admission-pointer reads/audit/prepared-upgrade proof reads; TCP Proxy does not participate in this HTTP path. Version-upgrade preparation is an implemented operator-facing mutation, but it is not part of this read/proof-read ingress: it remains externally gated and unavailable pending its action-family schema, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` vector conformance, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption under the external operator write ingress. Game Session remains the authority for the underlying read/proof-read contract. Game Session's current `/sessions*` lifecycle routes remain owner-local rather than current external ingress.
- **External operator write ingress** – Gateway is the external edge ingress and Logging & Admin is the mandatory domain ingress for implementation-participating runtime feature flags, version-upgrade preparation, admission-control/pointer writes, and scoped per-instance tick pause/resume, but those mutations remain externally gated pending their action-family schemas, shared `mutationDigest/v1` vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption. Lifecycle forwarding, versioned moderation propagation, hypothetical quota overrides, and broader tick remediation are target-only/non-routable participation contracts, not current routes; they may enter through Gateway to Logging & Admin only after the owning service contract, action-family schema, shared vectors, and Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption flow exist (plus any Logging & Admin receiving-boundary validation required by the family). Admission-pointer open/close/retarget CAS and cutover remain implemented-participating but externally gated under the same prerequisites. The live read/proof-read ingress and current owner-local/internal paths remain unchanged.
- **Edge admin/creator protocol** – External admin/creator APIs are HTTP(S) only at the Gateway edge unless a dedicated design update explicitly adds an edge gRPC contract. Internal service-to-service gRPC remains direct. External mutating operator workflows defined in the overview’s canonical operator action table must enter through Logging & Admin rather than directly through another edge-routable service.
- **Edge exposure default** – Unless a service is explicitly marked as participating in edge-routable domain APIs, its APIs are internal-only and reached through service-to-service contracts, not directly from external tools via Gateway.
- **Gameplay hot path policy** – Service APIs follow [ADR 0056](./decisions/adr-0056-one-hot-path-fan-out-owner.md): one ingress/dispatch step, one synchronous fan-out owner, and at most two authoritative participants across the transitive critical path. Every RPC stage, repeated call, retry, timeout, p95/p99 budget, fallback, and fail-closed outcome remains inside the measured contract; a third authoritative participant requires a new architecture decision.
