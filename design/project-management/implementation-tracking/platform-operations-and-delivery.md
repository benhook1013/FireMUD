# Platform Operations and Delivery

## Target-State Authority

This is a non-normative implementation tracker for platform operations and delivery. Canonical target-state infrastructure, deployment, security, and operational contracts remain authoritative in [design/architecture](../../architecture/README.md) and the linked design sources below; this tracker owns current implementation status, proof evidence, gaps, and handoffs, not normative policy.

## Current Status

The implementation record is consolidated by live capability. Runtime identity and logging, bounded metrics, player-experience smoke, hosted delivery, public-edge routing, deployment preflight, bounded component reset/recovery helpers, gameplay proof, and operator control surfaces exist at the bounded boundaries stated below. The `coordination-maintenance` CLI/control-plane, including public `recover` scope, is not currently implemented or proven; environment-wide player-facing recovery, immutable erasure high-water replay, and the durable recovery-controller continuation contract remain unimplemented. Remaining work also includes preview lifecycle/admission cleanup, the public Telnet TLS deployment boundary, richer live-environment proof, credential rotation, deferred observability context, and higher-scale gameplay evidence.

Packet 5 accepted decisions are reflected here as status only: [ADR 0141](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0141-fixed-safety-restriction-categories-and-independent-lifecycles.md) fixes the five safety categories and owner-local enforcement direction, [ADR 0142](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0142-bounded-moderation-appeal-cases.md) assigns bounded appeal cases to Logging & Admin with Account browser authentication/handoff, and [ADR 0143](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0143-stripe-v1-hosting-billing-and-deferred-creator-monetization.md) limits supported v1 commerce to Account-owned Stripe hosting billing. These decisions do not upgrade implementation or proof status.

### Script-transition operator evidence

Platform operations records the operator-facing consequence of the script transition family without becoming a domain owner. **Target:** Game Session owns the durable exact `{scriptPatchVersion, scriptPinEpoch}` and append-only rollout history; Logging & Admin must compose that authority with Automation readiness, projection freshness, plugin policy, timer, and dead-letter diagnostics. That composed readback is diagnostic/audit evidence only: it does not admit work, choose between authorities, or bypass owner-side fences. Game Session retains request/digest idempotency, exact tuple/history, and final execution fencing; Automation retains owner-read recovery plus producer epoch/version/cursor-aware projection refresh and bounded-fresh admission checks; Logging & Admin forwards, audits, and composes without becoming a second authority or deduplication ledger. `ScriptPatchPinChanged` is an advisory reread wake-up after the owner state/history commit, while `ScriptPinConvergenceTimedOut` remains a named durable producer consequence whose downstream delivery is advisory. See [ADR 0108](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0108-no-degraded-script-admission-without-authoritative-pin.md), [ADR 0109](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0109-game-session-owned-script-rollout-history.md), [ADR 0120](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0120-owner-read-first-control-plane-notifications.md), and the [notification and recovery contract](../../architecture/system-architecture-scripting-control-plane-events.md#notification-and-recovery-contract). For new instance-scoped scripted admission, missing, stale, or mismatched exact authority fails closed; tenant-readiness `onLoad` remains on its declared pre-instance-pin identity and does not require or fabricate `gameInstanceId` or `scriptPinEpoch`. This is not a stale-pin emergency override. **Current:** complete Game Session + Automation composed readback remains unavailable/unproven: current routes expose bounded Game Session pin/convergence reads, while Automation's projection/freshness evidence remains separate and non-authoritative; exact epoch/freshness composition and end-to-end fail-closed admission proof remain partial/unproven. The live handoff still omits `scriptPinEpoch`, so same-version old-epoch rejection and an end-to-end fail-closed result are not implemented/proven; current rollback and tick-pause seams do not prove the integrated rollback workflow or routine gameplay availability. **Target, unproven:** routine rollback keeps unrelated player-command admission and ordinary gameplay available while scoped Automation work reconciles. A full gameplay pause is valid only for a declared unfenced effect family, migration, or compatibility transition that cannot enforce the final `scriptPinEpoch` fence. The [Game Session tracker](./game-session-runtime-and-tick-coordination.md#capability-status) and [Automation tracker](./automation-and-scheduler-runtime.md) retain the implementation and proof gaps.

## Operator Write Boundary Status

The tracker separates implemented transport-forwarding paths from supported durable operator workflows and the target external ingress contract. Game Session `/sessions*` routes remain internal/owner-local. Logging & Admin has implemented forwarding paths for runtime feature-flag and scoped tick pause/resume requests, but those paths do not prove durable intent, Account authorization, owner idempotency/result persistence, or reconciliation; external enablement remains gated pending those contracts. The separate live `EvaluateModerationPolicy` read is an internal owner-boundary dependency, not external operator availability. `/moderation/actions` currently persists neither the `moderation_actions` record nor audit evidence and does not perform owner-side enforcement or forwarding. The target responsibility split is Logging & Admin policy ingress/readback, moderation cases, bounded appeals, protected evidence, and audit; Account, Game Session, and Social & Groups retain their restriction state and enforcement commands. Quota overrides, broader remediation, external session-lifecycle forwarding, and any owner mutation without a complete route, authorization, idempotency, and proof contract remain gated or target-only.

## Consolidated Implementation Record

### Runtime Identity and Structured Logging

`common-platform-core` provides the shared runtime identity model. `service` remains the logical `spring.application.name` and is stable across replicas; `serviceInstanceId` identifies one running instance, preferring platform identity and otherwise using a unique startup-generated value rather than static operator configuration. Startup lifecycle logs include runtime identity and available build metadata such as hostname, build SHA/version, image tag, and boot time. Services using the shared web stack expose identity and build metadata through `/actuator/runtime`.

The baseline request fields are `service`, `serviceInstanceId`, `traceId`, and `correlationId`. The shared servlet HTTP, reactive HTTP, gRPC, custom WebSocket edge, and Telnet edge paths now carry that baseline. Custom edge handlers use `RuntimeLoggingContext`; Gateway handshake-rejection logs open that context, bridge stall logs reuse the downstream connection correlation id, and Gateway/TCP Proxy development echo handlers no longer bypass the baseline. The consistency audit is intentionally bounded and does not replace every service-local log or vendor pipeline.

Game Session uses a shared `GameplayLoggingContext` on admission, `PLAY`, `LOOK`, movement, communication, reconnect restore/replay, recipient delivery, tick enqueue/query/process, and TCP disconnect coordination when identity is already known. Game Logic applies the same bounded fields to high-value LOOK, MOVE, and communication logs when the gRPC request carries them. The gameplay fields are `tenantId`, `gameInstanceId`, and `characterId` where known, with `regionId` part of the contract but deferred because these paths lack one canonical current-region source. Broad reactive MDC propagation outside shared filters remains optional follow-through.

### Metrics and Observability Policy

The canonical metrics policy has no approved `serviceInstanceId` or other per-instance exception. Raw `tenantId`, `gameInstanceId`, `regionId`, `sessionId`, `playerId`, `characterId`, script/plugin version identifiers, and similar unbounded runtime identifiers belong in logs, traces, or audit records, not ordinary Prometheus labels. Bounded semantic labels such as command type, normalized application error code, reason, role, fixed gRPC method, transport status, disconnect status, and shutdown classification remain allowed. No bounded tenant-bucketing exception is approved; a future scoped drilldown must introduce a bounded `scope` label through an architecture update.

The implemented changes remove raw tenant labels from gameplay invocation/failure, session resume/takeover/fallback, and tick follow-up metrics; remove `script_patch_version` from `game_sessions_started_total`; aggregate retry-queue depth rather than exposing tenant/target pairs; and use bounded `classification` for TCP Proxy shutdown. The reviewed scheduler, readiness, proxy, gRPC, delivery, aggregation, account, logging-admin, social-groups, world-management, game-design, cache, queue, saga, and entity surfaces remain untagged or bounded. In particular, `ReadinessTransitionTracker` uses bounded component/status/dependency values, `TickScheduler` keeps tenant/session keys out of metric labels, `MetricsInterceptor` uses fixed descriptors and transport status, and `grpc.app_error` uses normalized error codes.

`check-metrics-cardinality.py` scans service metric-building code, canonical observability documentation, metrics catalogs, and shipped Prometheus rules. It rejects forbidden raw identifier labels in metric examples as well as PromQL `sum by (...)` and `on (...)` clauses. Canonical Redis and scripting examples use bounded `scope`/`scope_type` and the documented bounded vocabulary (`script_category`, `plugin_family`, `plugin_version_family`, `component_class`, and `tier`); tick/coordination-exposure recording rules aggregate and join on bounded `scope`.

Gateway gameplay-handshake rejection and public-versus-bridge close metric semantics are canonical in [Logging and Monitoring](../../architecture/system-architecture-logging-monitoring.md) and its linked Gateway owner. Current `GameplayWebSocketObservability` still emits the bridge-only shutdown-class label for public closes and uses the legacy attribution vocabulary, so the target metric split and neutral valid-upstream classification remain implementation gaps. Outbound bridge-buffer pressure closes and their slow-client subset meter are implemented; arbitrary upstream close text is not exposed as a metric label.

### Player-Experience Smoke and Retained Evidence

`dev-tools/observability/run-player-experience-smoke.py` is the canonical operator harness. It runs one dedicated non-player identity, marked as synthetic in authoritative account/session state, per prod-like environment through the first-party public bootstrap and connect-token path, then proves WebSocket `LOGIN -> PLAY -> LOOK`; it also runs independent WebSocket and Telnet blackbox probes, emits the mirrored deadman heartbeat, writes retained evidence, and can emit OpenMetrics-style mirrored signals. The WebSocket flow uses `POST /auth/player-bootstrap`, bootstrap-backed world/realm/character discovery, `POST /auth/connect-token`, the Gateway `Firemud-Connect-Token` cookie, and the canonical Gateway route family (including the public `/api/account/auth/player-bootstrap` and `/api/account/auth/connect-token` inventory). The Telnet probe proves `WORLDS`; TCP port-open alone is insufficient. Shared gameplay smoke helpers keep the runner aligned with the existing local WebSocket and Telnet drivers.

The bounded signal contract uses `playerflow_canary_success{flow,path,target}`, `playerflow_canary_latency_ms{flow,path,target}`, `entrypath_blackbox_probe_success{path,target}`, and `observability_deadman_heartbeat_timestamp_seconds{source}`. The alert substrate includes `PlayerFlowCanaryLoginFailed`, `PlayerFlowCanaryCommandFailed`, `PlayerFlowCanaryLatencyHigh`, existing WebSocket/Telnet blackbox alerts, and `ObservabilityDeadmanHeartbeatStale`, with the deadman freshness threshold documented as `3 * 60s`. Alert routing labels remain bounded. Controlled failure injection is supported only for non-production paths and cannot exercise production paging destinations through ordinary proof.

The shared player-experience alert snippets and canonical Kubernetes `PrometheusRule` carry those alert families, and the observability contract validator requires the canary, blackbox, and deadman alerts so alert assets cannot silently drift from the smoke contract. Gateway anonymous bootstrap calls are rate-limited by canonical client-IP key, preserving public usability for the bootstrap and connect-token endpoints without adding identity-bearing metric labels.

Non-simulated runs require retained external-authority evidence through `--external-authority-evidence` or `PLAYER_EXPERIENCE_EXTERNAL_AUTHORITY_EVIDENCE`; only `--simulate` may synthesize external-authority state. Retained evidence records authoritative deadman and observability-entrypoint check objects with explicit `evidenceRef`, `target`, and `checkRef`, rather than a synthetic incident-opened boolean. The repository validates evidence shape and required green-state semantics, not freshness against the smoke execution window, because those external handles are opaque. Operators or automation must select and retain contemporaneous evidence for first-live, reopen, and post-restore traffic-open events.

### Canonical Demo and Runtime Seed State

Smoke and hosted preview seeders reassert canonical state by stable business identity instead of relying on empty tables. Preview/dev-demo bootstrap uses the canonical Account bootstrap flow and the same demo runtime-seeding contract as local Compose. Account reasserts the demo account and membership; Game Design reasserts the published game/template/version/revision/release-bundle/asset-artifact proof rows; Game Session reasserts manifest, feature-flag, and running-instance rows; Logging & Admin reasserts smoke-facing log/report/moderation rows; and World Management reasserts authored starter topology by natural demo identity before layering runtime topology on it.

World Management runtime-target seeding is isolated behind `SmokeDemoRuntimeSeedProperties` and consumes explicit `{tenantId, gameInstanceId}` targets. It no longer imports `GameplayCatalogProperties` in main-code smoke seeding, declares canonical demo and sandbox targets explicitly, and honors the shared `FIREMUD_BOOTSTRAP_*` overrides. The fresh-bootstrap script invokes helper scripts explicitly so file-mode drift cannot break the repo-owned proof path. The seed boundary does not attempt to become a general fixture framework or seed authored content beyond the smoke/demo contract.

### Hosted Preview, Dev-Demo, and Deployment Delivery

Hosted preview deploys real Helm releases into a prod-like stack, exposes a deliberately small preview-only TCP port range alongside HTTPS, seeds the minimum state for `LOGIN -> PLAY -> LOOK`, and proves TCP/Telnet access before browser convenience work. Preview is not a separate gameplay architecture and should not put product-specific browser helpers into Gateway. The current preview TCP contract requires a bootstrap gameplay session before `LOGIN`, supplied by preview-only default bootstrap metadata and workflow-created seed state; this is temporary operational glue, not the long-term TCP admission model.

Hosted preview uses the canonical bundle-based internal gRPC mTLS contract. Service runtime configuration uses `spring.ssl.bundle.pem.firemud-grpc` with `spring.grpc.server.ssl.enabled=true`, `spring.grpc.server.ssl.bundle=firemud-grpc`, and `spring.grpc.server.ssl.client-auth=REQUIRE` where client certificates are mandatory. The legacy top-level `grpc.server.*` server-TLS pattern is absent from service runtime config. `check-grpc-transport-config.sh --enforce` is the direct regression guard, and `checkGrpcTransportConfig` is the Gradle entrypoint. The guard is not a substitute for runtime handshake proof.

Preview and dev-demo have shared rollout diagnostics through `dev-tools/hosted/shared/show-rollout-diagnostics.sh`; the preview and dev-demo wrappers and workflows invoke it after cluster access succeeds. It reports target metadata, blocked readiness reasons, service/target ports, safe ConfigMap values, secret/TLS summaries, recent events, unavailable workload descriptions, and current/previous logs for problematic containers. This makes the first blocked dependency visible instead of waiting for a Helm timeout.

The fixed `develop` environment is a separate `dev-demo-cluster`, not a PR preview: namespace `dev`, release `dev`, hostname `dev.preview.firedevops.net`, and TCP port `32016`. Its workflow and scheduled reconciler deploy the current `develop` head, reset the namespace for reproducibility, and prove `LOGIN -> PLAY -> LOOK`; it is not a promotion source, rollback-evidence source, or DR-attestation source. PR janitor/capacity logic applies only to `pr-*` namespaces. Player-facing staging and production use the canonical Kustomize overlays, while hosted Helm retains preview/dev-demo ownership.

### Public Edge and Transport Contracts

Gateway is a curated public edge with explicit route inventory and deny-by-default behavior, not permissive service fan-out. Canonical route families include `/api/session/**`, `/api/admin/**`, `/assets/**`, WebSocket gameplay, Telnet, and `gateway.v1` management. The current public session HTTP inventory is only `GET /api/session/ping`; remaining session/admin routes are privileged external contracts. Gateway blocks `/api/{public-family}/internal/**`, and undocumented paths under the explicit families are denied rather than forwarded by a coarse family matcher. The canonical namespace is `/api/{service}/**`, not the obsolete `/admin/...` examples.

Owner-side enforcement is part of the boundary: Game Session enforces the documented public/internal/operator split, and Logging & Admin exposes only published privileged routes behind its own authorization. Gateway route configuration, route predicates/filters, owner controllers, fixtures, and docs are intended to describe the same inventory. Gateway management uses the canonical `gateway.v1` package and shared in-band application-error conventions; it does not replicate service authorization policy locally. New public routes must be added to the explicit inventory with owner-side enforcement.

Spring Cloud Gateway has dynamic route-management surfaces, but runtime changes are ephemeral overrides layered over canonical `routes.yml`. Until shared persistence, multi-pod convergence, route-change auditing, and readiness enforcement exist, the intended policy is that those overrides are dev/test-only; current runtime does not yet enforce that isolation, and a full Gateway restart returns to baseline routes. The explicit public route inventory and owner-side enforcement are implemented, while this dynamic-management lifecycle is not a production control plane.

### Preflight, Secrets, and Kubernetes Binding Authority

`design/operations/environments/<environment>/expected-bindings.yaml` is the authority for player-facing binding identity, and `dev-tools/deploy/preflight.py` consumes it and emits `expectedBindingsRef`. Every report carries a generated unique `deploymentEventId`, event applicability, and a boolean `required` plus `pass`, `fail`, or `not_applicable` for every implemented policy. Consumed operator reports reject synthetic all-pass applicability, noncanonical staging paths, event mismatches, post-apply completion, and completion more than 30 minutes before apply. Waiver execution and consumed waived reports remain fail-closed pending trusted one-time consumption authority. Canonical binding references fail closed: internal secrets use `secret://<namespace>/<name>`, certificates use the documented `cert-manager://...` forms, and external bindings use `<scheme>://<namespace>/<binding>`.

Preflight validates exact rendered bootstrap Secret and registry pull-secret identity from the expected-binding manifest, including environment-specific `ghcr-pull-*` bindings on the staging/production `firemud-app` ServiceAccount. Rendered Kubernetes resources are parsed to prove mounted `jwt-signing-keys` on primary workloads and mounted `jwt-jwks` for Account Service. This is legacy Secret-backed wiring evidence only: it does not validate the accepted fixed-name ConfigMap, Account-only CAS/RBAC publication contract, or player-facing asymmetric JWT readiness. `common-security` supports file-mounted signing material through `firemud.auth.jwt-secret-path` without an inline secret, and Account Service serves `/.well-known/jwks.json` from `firemud.auth.jwks-path` with a classpath fallback for local/dev. Preview rendering generates namespace-unique signing/JWKS material; the canonical app Secret does not carry inline JWT signing material.

Player-facing external bindings are unique across all expected-binding manifests by default. Backup/object-store, asset-store, SMTP, webhook-target, and operator-credential identity/value collisions fail `PREFLIGHT-EXTERNAL-001` unless every participating manifest declares the field shared with the same non-empty `sharedRationale`; object values may use `value` or `bindingRef` with `shared: true`. In `serviceDiscovery.mode=explicit-overrides`, every rendered `FIREMUD_SERVICES_*` value must be declared in `allowedOverrides` with the exact expected value; undeclared, missing, or mismatched overrides fail preflight.

Preflight can process `FIREMUD_TRAFFIC_OPEN_EVENT=first-live|reopen`. Production `PREFLIGHT-BACKUP-002` and hobby `PREFLIGHT-BACKUP-003` fail closed because the durable environment-wide recovery-controller read is not implemented; checked-in projections and caller-supplied tenant, game-instance, region, or timestamp evidence cannot authorize traffic. `write-traffic-open-evidence.py` retains the post-finalization hobby projection contract, including schema, operator identity, evidence references, and canonical `deploymentEventId` lineage; the projection is not a preflight authorization input. Actual first-live/reopen authority still requires the target-state durable controller and current environment evidence. The staging/production Kustomize path is canonical, renders no placeholder player-facing credentials/TLS resources, supports immutable image/digest control, and uses the same binding contract. Hosted preview/dev-demo render checked-in baseline internal-service `NetworkPolicy` resources through Helm.

### Reset, Flyway, Redis, and Local Proof Tooling

The canonical local toolchain is one-shot and explicitly destructive. `dev-tools/restores/reset-service-db.sh <service>` resets only the known service-owned runtime tables and matching Flyway history, fails for unknown reset contracts, includes shared saga tables for saga-backed services, waits for Postgres readiness, and exports the service schema/history variables (`SERVICE_SCHEMA`, `SPRING_FLYWAY_TABLE`, `FLYWAY_SCHEMAS`, `FLYWAY_DEFAULT_SCHEMA`, and `FLYWAY_TABLE`) plus standard Flyway connection variables. `checkFlywayVersions` runs `check-flyway-versions.sh` locally and in CI to reject duplicate versions without pretending to simulate historical upgrades.

The pre-v1 baseline reset has replaced the local migration chains for `entity-management-service`, `automation-scripting-service`, and `game-session-service` with one canonical `V1__baseline.sql` each, removing superseded archaeology and proving service-scoped reset/rebuild against each baseline. Root Gradle Flyway wiring supports `:service:flywayMigrate` outside Spring Boot startup. Shared Postgres/Flyway test support and every SQL-backed service base configuration use `flyway_schema_history_<service_schema>` rather than bare `flyway_schema_history`; `logging-admin-service` has a Testcontainers Postgres + Flyway + Redis application boot proof. Dead Hibernate/JPA environment knobs were removed from the canonical Docker manifest.

`verify-fresh-bootstrap.sh` proves clean reset, current-stack startup, and `LOGIN -> PLAY -> LOOK`; `verify-restart-state.sh` proves restart against existing state; and `SMOKE_IMAGE_TAG=<tag> dev-tools/verify-smoke-images.sh` is the canonical image-tag smoke entrypoint. These are source-built/image-tag startup and state-retention proofs, not evidence that production TLS lifecycle, certificate rotation, or edge reconnect behavior is complete. Fresh-bootstrap normally uses cached Compose builds but supports targeted freshness through `FIREMUD_SMOKE_NO_CACHE_SERVICES`, validates service IDs, and applies targeted no-cache builds in serial or parallel mode. Source-built smoke images normalize boot-jar ownership/mode for the non-root `firemud` runtime. WSL proof requires the native Linux Docker CLI at `unix:///var/run/docker.sock`, not a Windows `docker.exe` wrapper.

Heavy local Gradle proof uses `dev-tools/validation/run-locked-gradle.sh`, which acquires a service lock for service-scoped tasks and a repo-wide lock for unscoped/mixed tasks, recording lock-owner metadata under `.gradle/firemud-validation-locks/`. `inspect-test-results.sh <service>` is read-only diagnostics for fresh JUnit XML when a run goes quiet after test execution; it does not auto-kill or auto-pass Gradle.

The [canonical Redis coordination reset/recovery sequence](../../architecture/system-architecture-redis-operations.md#canonical-coordination-reset-sequence) is the sole normative command sequence. Incident, reset/recovery, backup/restore, and related runbooks link to it and retain only scenario-specific scope, session-policy, evidence, and abort details. The documented `coordination-maintenance` CLI/control-plane surface is not currently implemented or proven; the sequence and runbook wording are not evidence that this operator surface exists.

### Gameplay Proof, Fixtures, and Operator Control

`GameplayWebSocketDriver` and `GameplayTelnetDriver` are the shared FireMUD-specific transport drivers for chained `LOGIN`, `PLAY`, readiness via `LOOK`, expected server close, unexpected client abort, reconnect, takeover, and multi-actor flows. Telnet retains deliberate multiline block and prompt/partial transcript semantics; raw telnet byte-pipeline and Gateway bridge socket-rebind tests remain low-level exceptions. Shared scenario helpers cover ready pair/trio and actor/target/observer admission, and shared backend assertions cover Entity Management item/equipment and Social Groups whisper/tell request shapes.

`GameplayCrossServiceStack` owns nested service/bootstrap setup, migration-dir resolution, suite-specific room/social fixtures, mutable stub reset, shared live-session seeding, and `freshGameplayBaseline(...)`, including runtime-row reset with `TRUNCATE ... RESTART IDENTITY`. Transcript-block helpers wait for the exact LOOK or move-plus-LOOK boundary. The shared baseline preserves customized friend-presence/social fixtures; only tests where pre-disconnect transcript shape is itself the behavior retain bespoke setup. Smoke remains black-box operator proof, but local and hosted scripts share the command-step catalog, transport-session helper semantics, account validation, and readiness/account-schema helpers. The legacy image-based `TcpProxyCrossServiceIntegrationTest` has been retired in favor of the canonical Telnet Gateway suites and smoke proof paths.

`common-test-support` provides `AccountRuntimeStubServer` for the canonical Account runtime RPC surface used by Game Session and TCP Proxy tests, with knobs for authentication identity, gameplay admission, entitlements, realm grants, and request capture. A compatibility test categorizes the Account method set. Expected TCP Proxy shutdown cancellation/unavailability is classified and debug-logged under an expected-shutdown bucket; active-path transport failures remain warnings and retain the transport-failure metric.

The deterministic multiplayer proof is bounded but real: ten concurrent generated WebSocket clients enter `LOGIN -> PLAY -> LOOK` against nested Game Session/Game Logic/World/Entity services with real Redis/Postgres test containers and bounded Account/world/entity stubs. Generated usernames map deterministically to generated account ids in the Account runtime stub, game instances are inserted programmatically, and isolated containers plus explicit baseline/Redis/session/screen-buffer resets remove any per-player cleanup contract. The proof asserts active same-room session contexts, bounded `gamesession.command.look.invocations`, no unexpected errors or disconnects, synchronized `north` movement plus destination LOOK churn with `gamesession.command.move.invocations`, and a ten-player SAY burst with per-actor transcripts and one `CHAT_TYPE_SAY` dispatch per player plus `gamesession.command.say.invocations`. A mixed-transport proof keeps one Telnet and one Gateway-backed WebSocket client in the same runtime through a shared movement step. Current loose timing bounds are 20 seconds for concurrent entry, 20 seconds for ready-player bootstrap, and 15 seconds for movement plus destination LOOK. The `load-testing` module and higher-volume operator harness remain future work.

Game Session is the persisted runtime feature-flag authority. Logging & Admin retains policy ingress/readback, moderation case/appeal/evidence/audit responsibility, and privileged operator ingress, and has implemented transport-forwarding paths for `ToggleFeatureFlag` and scoped `PauseTicksForScope`/`ResumeTicksForScope`, but those paths are not supported durable operator workflows: action-family schemas, shared cross-language `mutationDigest/v1` golden vectors, Account authorization-reference issuance/redemption, owner-side idempotency/result persistence, and reconciliation remain unproven, so external enablement is gated. Logging & Admin no longer has a duplicate feature-flag table/entity/repository/mapper/migration/seeding path. Current Game Session `/sessions*` routes remain owner-local/internal; target external session-lifecycle forwarding through Logging & Admin is not yet a live route. The separate `EvaluateModerationPolicy` read is a live internal owner-boundary dependency; `/moderation/actions` currently persists neither the `moderation_actions` record nor audit evidence and does not perform owner-side enforcement or forwarding. Account, Game Session, and Social & Groups retain restriction state and enforcement commands; no implementation/proof claim is made for the target moderation command or case workflows. Logging & Admin also exposes the bounded tenant-qualified compatibility readback `GET /admission-pointers/version-upgrades/{tenantId}/{sourceGameInstanceId}/compatibility/{targetVersionId}`, returning the canonical Game Session result, reasons, checked participants, optional remap set, and participant details after tenant authorization, without creating a prepared-upgrade row. Quota-override ingress and the broader tick-remediation `remediate` route remain gated/target-only rather than owner-backed routes. Richer embedded observability and real-time moderation tooling likewise remain future application work.

The current TCP Proxy hardening boundary is intentionally separate from Telnet parity: gameplay ingress, Proxy-to-Gateway WebSocket mTLS, PROXY protocol client-IP preservation, MCP 2.1 control-line handling, connection throttling, and active cross-service parity proof are live. Production follow-through is tuning, additional bounded abuse metrics, certificate-rotation/lifecycle proof, and higher-volume evidence rather than rebuilding those implemented substrates. This does not revive the retired disabled test scaffolding.

## Active Gaps

- Preview clean-redeploy lifecycle and documentation convergence remain open. The current preview-only bootstrap session contract is still in place, and the planned TCP admission cleanup has not selected or implemented its replacement.
- Preflight validates expected-binding manifests, rendered binding identity, report shape, canonical refs, cross-environment external isolation, and exact service-discovery overrides, but does not yet provide richer Kubernetes live-state validation.
- Production and hobby first-live/reopen remain intentionally blocked: `PREFLIGHT-BACKUP-002` and `PREFLIGHT-BACKUP-003` have no durable recovery-controller read and reject checked-in projections or caller-supplied tenant/game-instance/region/timestamp evidence as authorization. The writer retains the post-finalization hobby projection, but it cannot authorize traffic.
- JWT/JWKS file mounting and preview-unique rendering are live, but the executable preflight still expects the legacy Secret-backed `jwt-jwks` resource rather than proving the accepted fixed-name ConfigMap and Account-only CAS/RBAC contract. Automated credential rotation and its operational proof are not live.
- Gateway/TCP edge startup and restart proofs are bounded as described above; they do not close the remaining TLS lifecycle, certificate-rotation, reconnect, or final TCP Proxy hardening work.
- Runtime logging still lacks one authoritative current-region source for broad `regionId` enrichment. Expanded reactive MDC propagation is optional follow-through, not an unimplemented baseline requirement.
- Logging & Admin's richer embedded observability surface remains a design gap, and quota-override plus broad tick-remediation `remediate` ingress is not live.
- Future gameplay suites should adopt the shared drivers and reconnect/takeover helpers when their scenario shape matches; the current shared-harness boundary does not require migrating every future or intentionally low-level test.
- Higher-volume/soak proof, combat and inventory concurrency, broader shared-room fanout proof, and the operator-facing `load-testing` harness remain outside the ten-client proof boundary.
- The accepted [ADR 0058](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0058-class-specific-redis-loss-outcomes.md) Redis-loss contract requires durable intent and class-specific rebuild/terminalization; current preflight and recovery evidence do not establish every class or measured `redis_unreplicated_write_window_slo_ms` value.
- [ADR 0057](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0057-game-session-owned-reconciliation-with-isolated-workers.md) assigns isolated Game Session reconciliation workers; [ADR 0068](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0068-evidence-derived-bounded-tick-ledger-recovery.md) defines recovery SLOs; and [ADR 0076](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0076-failure-class-specific-durable-tick-retries.md) defines retry exhaustion and quarantine/disposition paths. Those controls and focused fault-injection proof remain runtime gaps rather than operational claims here.
- [ADR 0073](../../architecture/decisions/adr-0073-evidence-calibrated-tick-budgets-and-lock-ttls.md) keeps bootstrap timing formulas separate from measured production safety settings; current operator/configuration surfaces do not yet prove evidence-calibrated values or the complete lease/cleanup health split.
